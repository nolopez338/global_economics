Page 1 gives $\Phi(z)=P(Z\le z)$ for positive $z$ on a 0.01 grid (through $2.59$).
Page 2 gives positive t critical values for upper-tail probabilities
$0.30,0.20,0.10,0.05,0.025,0.01,0.005$, for df $1$--$30$ and selected larger df.
Those entries do not give arbitrary CDF values. Use a suitable calculator's t CDF and inverse t CDF for this activity;
normal CDF and inverse normal are also allowed. Numerical CDF and quantile results in the solutions are supplementary to the tables.
Never turn table bounds into a single probability or error.

Write $F_\nu(t)=P(T\le t)$ for $T\sim t_\nu$.
Symmetry gives $F_\nu(-c)=1-F_\nu(c)$; a right tail is $1-F_\nu(c)$.
An interval has probability $F_\nu(b)-F_\nu(a)$ and its outside complement has probability
$F_\nu(a)+1-F_\nu(b)$. Replace $F_\nu$ by $\Phi$ for normal calculations.
Endpoints have 0 probability. For central area $p$, each tail has $(1-p)/2$;
for combined symmetric tails $q$, each tail has $q/2$.

A contextual location-scale model is explicitly $X=m+aT$, with $a>0$ a scale parameter;
$a$ is not automatically the standard deviation. Thus $T=(X-m)/a$.
These are stipulated models for signed deviations, not claims that arbitrary individual measurements follow t.
For independent random observations from a normal population, with unknown population standard deviation,
the sampling statistic instead is
\[
T=\frac{\bar X-\mu}{S/\sqrt n}\sim t_{n-1}.
\]
Here $\bar X$ and $S$ vary across samples; do not treat observed $s$ as a fixed population standard deviation.

Attempt each task before checking the worked solution. Calculate with full precision, then report
probabilities to 4 decimals, standardized quantiles to 3 decimals, contextual values to 2 decimals,
and percentage errors to 2 decimals. Displayed rounded intermediate values are not the inputs used for errors.
Diagrams shade the requested region; curves continue beyond the displayed range.

\subsection{Delivery schedule deviations}
\label{c8-d1}
\textbf{Problem.} A planning team describes how far delivery time falls below or rises above its scheduled time, in minutes. Use the chance pattern identified by the row marked 9 on page 2 of the supplied reference tables. Its middle represents exactly meeting the scheduled time. Each step of 1 in that pattern represents 5 minutes here. Amounts below the middle mean less than the scheduled time, and amounts above it mean more.
\QuestionsTasks[sec:c8-direct]{{8}/{sol:c8-d1}/{Find the chance of arriving before 7.5 minutes after the scheduled time, and the chance of arriving more than 6 minutes early. Show how to express each requested outcome using the reference pattern. Explain how matching chances on either side of the middle or the chance of all other outcomes helps, where needed, and interpret each answer.}}
\subsubsection*{Worked solution}\phantomsection\label{sol:c8-d1}
\par\textbf{Translating the information into mathematics}
Let $X$ be delivery time minus its scheduled time, in minutes, and let $T$ be the value in the reference pattern. The specified row gives Student's t distribution with 9 degrees of freedom. The middle and conversion give
\[X=0+5T,\qquad T=\frac{X-0}{5},\qquad T\sim t_{9}.\]
The location is 0 and the scale is 5 minutes. This scale is not automatically a population standard deviation.

Standardize each boundary using $(x-0)/5$.
\par\textbf{Part 1.}\[P(X<7.5)=P(T<1.5).\]
\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[x=0.78cm,y=2.8cm]
\fill[blue!22] (-4.5,0) -- plot[domain=-4.50000000:1.50000000,samples=100] (\x,{0.3880349089*(1+\x*\x/9)^(-5)}) -- (1.5,0) -- cycle;
\draw[thick,blue!70!black] plot[domain=-4.50000000:4.50000000,samples=100] (\x,{0.3880349089*(1+\x*\x/9)^(-5)});
\draw[->] (-4.7,0)--(4.8,0);
\draw[densely dashed] (1.5,0)--(1.5,0.1271513);\node[below,font=\scriptsize] at (1.5,0) {$ 1.500 $};
\node[font=\small] at (0,0.49) {$ T\sim t_{9} $};
\node[below,font=\scriptsize] at (0,0) {$0$};\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}
For a negative boundary, use $F_\nu(-c)=1-F_\nu(c)$. The region is cumulative area to the left.
Supplementary CDF values: $F_{9}(1.5)\approx 0.9160747$.
\[P(T<1.5)=F_{9}(1.5)\approx 0.9161.\]
\textbf{Interpretation.} About 91.61\% of deliveries arrive before 7.5 minutes after their scheduled time.

\begin{center}

\[P(T<-1.2)=F_{9}(-1.2)\approx 0.1304.\]
\textbf{Interpretation.} About 13.04\% of deliveries arrive more than 6 minutes early.

\subsection{Top delivery delays}
\label{c8-i1}
\textbf{Problem.} A planning team describes how far delivery time falls below or rises above its scheduled time, in minutes. Use the chance pattern identified by the row marked 9 on page 2 of the supplied reference tables. Its middle represents exactly meeting the scheduled time. Each step of 1 in that pattern represents 5 minutes here. Amounts below the middle mean less than the scheduled time, and amounts above it mean more.
\QuestionsTasks[sec:c8-inverse]{{8}/{sol:c8-i1}/{Find how many minutes late a delivery must be so that only 5 percent of deliveries arrive later. Express the requested outcome using the reference pattern, work out the share at or below each boundary, find the boundaries in that pattern, and convert to the original units. Explain how the shares above or below the boundaries are used and interpret the result.}}
\subsubsection*{Worked solution}\phantomsection\label{sol:c8-i1}
\par\textbf{Translating the information into mathematics}
Let $X_T$ be delivery time minus its scheduled time, in minutes, and let $T$ be the value in the reference pattern. The specified row gives Student's t distribution with 9 degrees of freedom. The middle and conversion give
\[X_T=0+5T,\qquad T=\frac{X_T-0}{5},\qquad T\sim t_{9}.\]
The location is 0 and the scale is 5 minutes. This scale is not automatically a population standard deviation.

\[P(X_T>x)=0.0500\quad\Longrightarrow\quad P(T\le c)=0.9500,\qquad c=\frac{x-0}{5}.\]
A top percentage is a right tail, so subtract it from 1 before inverse CDF.
\[c_T=F_{9}^{-1}(0.95)\approx 1.833.\]
\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[x=0.78cm,y=2.8cm]
\fill[blue!22] (1.8331129326562428,0) -- plot[domain=1.83311293:4.50000000,samples=100] (\x,{0.3880349089*(1+\x*\x/9)^(-5)}) -- (4.5,0) -- cycle;
\draw[thick,blue!70!black] plot[domain=-4.50000000:4.50000000,samples=100] (\x,{0.3880349089*(1+\x*\x/9)^(-5)});
\draw[->] (-4.7,0)--(4.8,0);
\draw[densely dashed] (1.8331129326562428,0)--(1.8331129326562428,0.0794214);\node[below,font=\scriptsize] at (1.8331129326562428,0) {$ 1.833 $};
\node[font=\small] at (0,0.49) {$ T\sim t_{9} $};
\node[below,font=\scriptsize] at (0,0) {$0$};\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}
\[x_T=0+5c_T\approx 9.17\text{ minutes}.\]
\textbf{Interpretation.} About 5\% of deviations are above 9.17 minutes.

\subsection{2-tail checks on filling weights}
\label{c8-i5}
\textbf{Problem.} A quality team repeatedly chooses 6 filling weights at random. Each choice is independent: knowing 1 result does not change the chances for another. The full group follows the bell-shaped chance pattern used on page 1 of the supplied reference tables, allowing for where the group is centered and how widely its values are spread. The full-group average exists, but its spread is unknown. For each chosen group, find its average and subtract the full-group average. To measure the spread within the chosen group, find how far each value is from its group average, multiply each difference by itself, add the results, divide by 1 fewer than the number chosen, and find the positive number which, multiplied by itself, gives that result. Divide this spread by the positive number which, multiplied by itself, gives 6, then divide the earlier difference between averages by that result to obtain a score. Find 2 boundaries equally far below and above 0 so that 10 percent of scores altogether fall below the lower boundary or above the upper boundary.
\QuestionsTasks[sec:c8-inverse]{{8}/{sol:c8-i5}/{Express the requested outcome using the score, work out the share at or below each boundary, and find both boundaries. Explain how the share outside the boundaries is divided and interpret the result.}}
\subsubsection*{Worked solution}\phantomsection\label{sol:c8-i5}
\par\textbf{Translating the information into mathematics}
Let $X_1,\ldots,X_{6}$ be the independent random filling weights, from a normal population with mean $\mu$ and unknown standard deviation $\sigma$. Let $\bar X$ be the sample mean and $S$ the sample standard deviation. The described spread and score are
\[S^2=\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{6}(X_j-\bar X)^2}{5},\qquad T=\frac{\bar X-\mu}{S/\sqrt{6}}\sim t_{5}.\]
Let $c>0$ be the upper boundary; the lower boundary is $-c$.

\[P(|T|>c)=0.1000\quad\Longrightarrow\quad P(T>c)=P(T<-c)=0.0500.\]
Divide the combined outside area by 2 because symmetry makes the tails equal.
\[F_{5}(c)=0.9500,\qquad c_T=F_{5}^{-1}(0.95)\approx 2.015.\]
\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[x=0.78cm,y=2.8cm]
\fill[blue!22] (-4.5,0) -- plot[domain=-4.50000000:-2.01504837,samples=100] (\x,{0.3796066898*(1+\x*\x/5)^(-3)}) -- (-2.0150483733330287,0) -- cycle;
\fill[blue!22] (2.0150483733330287,0) -- plot[domain=2.01504837:4.50000000,samples=100] (\x,{0.3796066898*(1+\x*\x/5)^(-3)}) -- (4.5,0) -- cycle;
\draw[thick,blue!70!black] plot[domain=-4.50000000:4.50000000,samples=100] (\x,{0.3796066898*(1+\x*\x/5)^(-3)});
\draw[->] (-4.7,0)--(4.8,0);
\draw[densely dashed] (-2.0150483733330287,0)--(-2.0150483733330287,0.0637968);\node[below,font=\scriptsize] at (-2.0150483733330287,0) {$ -2.015 $};
\draw[densely dashed] (2.0150483733330287,0)--(2.0150483733330287,0.0637968);\node[below,font=\scriptsize] at (2.0150483733330287,0) {$ 2.015 $};
\node[font=\small] at (0,0.49) {$ T\sim t_{5} $};
\node[below,font=\scriptsize] at (0,0) {$0$};\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}
The standardized endpoints are $[-2.015,2.015]$.
\textbf{Interpretation.} The t interval contains 90\% of standardized sample-mean outcomes; 5\% lie in each excluded tail.
