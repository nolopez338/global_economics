\subsection{The shape depends on the degrees of freedom.}
\label{sec:c8-model}
Assume $n\ge2$ independent random observations from a normal population. The population mean $\mu$ and standard deviation $\sigma>0$ are fixed parameters; $\bar X$ and $S$ are the random sample mean and sample standard deviation. When unknown $\sigma$ is estimated by $S$,
\[
T=\frac{\bar X-\mu}{S/\sqrt n}\sim t_\nu,\qquad \nu=n-1,
\]
where $n$ is sample size and $\nu$ (df) is degrees of freedom. The denominator estimates the standard error of $\bar X$; $T$ has no units. $t_\nu$ denotes Student's t-distribution, continuous and symmetric about zero with total probability 1. Its tails are heavier than those of $\mathcal N(0,1)$, the standard normal distribution (mean 0, standard deviation 1). As $\nu$ increases, t approaches the standard normal distribution and its positive critical values decrease toward normal values.

\subsection{Symmetry connects tail areas and central areas.}
\label{sec:c8-areas}
For $T\sim t_\nu$, $\alpha$ is an upper-tail probability and $t^*_{\alpha,\nu}$ its positive critical value:
\[
P(T>t^*_{\alpha,\nu})=\alpha,\qquad 0<\alpha<\tfrac12.
\]
Write $P(\cdot)$ for probability and $\sim$ for follows the distribution. A cutoff $c$ is a fixed boundary (possibly negative); $\pm c$ denotes both $-c$ and $c$, and $|Y|$ is the absolute value of $Y$. For continuous distributions, endpoints do not affect probabilities: $<$ and $\le$ (or $>$ and $\ge$) are interchangeable. $\approx$ means approximate equality.

For $Y=T$ symmetric about zero, $c>0$, and $\alpha=P(Y>c)$:
\[
\begin{aligned}
P(Y<-c)&=\alpha,& P(Y<c)&=1-\alpha,& P(Y>-c)&=1-\alpha,\\
P(|Y|>c)&=2\alpha,& P(-c<Y<c)&=1-2\alpha.&
\end{aligned}
\]
A combined two-tail area $q$ uses $\alpha=q/2$; a central area $p$ uses $\alpha=(1-p)/2$, where $p,q$ are probabilities. The symmetric cutoffs are $\pm c$. For any real endpoints $a<b$,
\[
P(a<Y<b)=1-P(Y\le a)-P(Y\ge b),\qquad
P(Y>c)=1-P(Y\le c).
\]

\subsection{Read the linked Student's t-distribution table.}
\label{sec:c8-table}
On reference page 2, select row $\nu$ and column $\alpha$: the entry is $t^*_{\alpha,\nu}$, a cutoff, not a probability. Columns are $0.30,0.20,0.10,0.05,0.025,0.01,0.005$; all df needed here have rows. Convert the requested area to one upper tail first; negate the entry for a lower-tail cutoff.

Entries are rounded to three decimals: report cutoffs to three decimals and probabilities at printed cutoffs as approximations.
For adjacent upper-tail columns with $\alpha_1>\alpha_2$,
\[
t^*_{\alpha_1,\nu}<c<t^*_{\alpha_2,\nu}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \alpha_2<P(T>c)<\alpha_1.
\]
These are probability bounds, not an interpolated estimate. Symmetry, doubling, and complements give other bounds; for example,
$1-2\alpha_1<P(-c<T<c)<1-2\alpha_2$.
Allow for entry rounding if a cutoff is extremely close to a printed value; the between-entry cutoffs here are well separated from that uncertainty.

\subsection{Study three worked table examples.}
\label{sec:c8-examples}
\subsubsection*{C8 Example 1: Find a positive cutoff and a negative cutoff.}
An analyst models a standardized mean return from 10 independent observations. Find the positive cutoff exceeded by the standardized sample mean with probability 0.05. Also find the negative cutoff below which the standardized sample mean falls with probability 0.05.

\subsubsection*{C8 Worked-example solution}
Here $\nu=10-1=9$. Each request concerns one tail. Row $9$, column $\alpha=0.05$, gives $t^*_{0.05,9}=1.833$ to three decimal places. Thus the positive cutoff is approximately $1.833$ and the negative cutoff is approximately $-1.833$. Symmetry gives
\[
P(T<-1.833)=P(T>1.833)\approx0.05.
\]

\subsubsection*{C8 Example 2: Find symmetric cutoffs for a central probability.}
A standardized household-expenditure mean uses 15 observations and has 14 degrees of freedom. Find symmetric cutoffs enclosing a central probability of 0.95.

\subsubsection*{C8 Worked-example solution}
$T\sim t_{\nu}$ with $\nu=n-1=15-1=14$. The outside probability is $1-0.95=0.05$, shared equally between the two tails. Use $\alpha=0.05/2=0.025$. Row $14$ gives
\[
t^*_{0.025,14}=2.145,\qquad
P(-2.145<T<2.145)\approx1-2(0.025)=0.95.
\]
The cutoffs are approximately $-2.145$ and $2.145$; equivalently, $P(|T|>2.145)\approx0.05$.

\subsubsection*{C8 Example 3: Bound probabilities using symmetry.}
A standardized delivery-time mean uses 20 observations and has 19 degrees of freedom. Give probability bounds for the standardized sample mean exceeding 2.30, falling below negative 2.30, and falling outside the symmetric cutoffs negative 2.30 and 2.30.

\subsubsection*{C8 Worked-example solution}
$T\sim t_{\nu}$ with $\nu=n-1=20-1=19$. In row $19$,
\[
t^*_{0.025,19}=2.093<2.30<2.539=t^*_{0.01,19}.
\]
Larger positive cutoffs leave smaller upper-tail areas. Symmetry then gives
\[
0.01<P(T>2.30)<0.025,\qquad
0.01<P(T<-2.30)<0.025.
\]
Doubling the equal tail areas and taking the complement gives
\[
0.02<P(|T|>2.30)<0.05,\qquad
0.95<P(-2.30<T<2.30)<0.98.
\]

\subsection{Find probability ranges for revenue planning.}

\QuestionsTasks[sec:c8-practice]{{8}/{sol:c8-planning}/{For symmetric cutoffs of negative 3.00 and 3.00, give lower and upper bounds for the probability that the standardized sample mean falls below negative 3.00, the combined probability that it falls outside the cutoffs, and the probability that it lies between them. \subsubsection*{C8 Solution}
\phantomsection
\label{sol:c8-planning}
$T\sim t_{\nu}$, where $\nu=n-1=10-1=9$. Since $2.821<3.00<3.250$, $0.005<P(T>3.00)<0.01$. Thus (a) $0.005<P(T<-3.00)<0.01$; (b) $0.01<P(|T|>3.00)<0.02$; and (c) $0.98<P(-3.00<T<3.00)<0.99$. For the asymmetric interval, the left excluded tail is approximately $0.05$ and the right excluded tail is approximately $0.025$. Therefore $P(-1.833<T<2.262)\approx1-0.05-0.025=0.925$.
