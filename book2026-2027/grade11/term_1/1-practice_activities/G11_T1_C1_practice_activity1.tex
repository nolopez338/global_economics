	\section{Introduction}
	\label{sec:introduction}

	A \textbf{random variable} assigns a numerical value to the outcome of a random process. A discrete random variable takes separate, countable values (often counts such as $0,1,2,\ldots$). A continuous random variable can take any real value in an interval, such as a waiting time or price. Its \textbf{support} is the set of possible values.

	For a discrete variable, the probability mass function (PMF) $p_X(x)=P(X=x)$ gives probability at each possible value, and the masses sum to $1$. For a continuous variable, the probability density function (PDF) $f_X(x)$ is a height, not a point probability: $P(X=x)=0$, while interval probability is area, $P(r\le X\le s)=\int_r^s f_X(x)\,dx$. A valid density is nonnegative and has total area $1$. In both cases the cumulative distribution function (CDF)
	\[
		F_X(x)=P(X\le x)
	\]
	records the probability accumulated up to $x$; it is a sum of PMF values or an integral of the PDF.

	\subsection*{Bernoulli distribution (discrete)}
	A Bernoulli variable represents \emph{one} trial with two outcomes, coded success ($1$) and failure ($0$). Clues are ``one selected account,'' ``yes/no,'' or ``paid/defaulted.'' Its support is $\{0,1\}$ and its parameter is the success probability $0\le p\le1$:
	\[
		P(X=x)=p^x(1-p)^{1-x},\quad x\in\{0,1\},
		\qquad
		F_X(x)=\begin{cases}0,&x<0,\\1-p,&0\le x<1,\\1,&x\ge1.\end{cases}
	\]
	The PMF assigns the two outcome probabilities; the CDF gives the probability of a coded outcome no greater than $x$. For example, $X=1$ may mean that one randomly selected invoice is overdue.

	\subsection*{Binomial distribution (discrete)}
	A binomial variable counts successes in a \emph{fixed} number $n$ of independent Bernoulli trials having the same success probability $p$. Its recognition clues are a stated sample size, binary outcomes, independence, and constant $p$. Its support is $\{0,1,\ldots,n\}$ and its parameters are $n$ and $p$:
	\[
		P(X=x)=\binom{n}{x}p^x(1-p)^{n-x},\quad x=0,\ldots,n,
		\qquad F_X(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor x\rfloor}\binom{n}{k}p^k(1-p)^{n-k},
	\]
	where the CDF is $0$ below $0$ and $1$ at or above $n$. The PMF gives the probability of exactly $x$ successes; the CDF gives that of at most $x$. An example is the number of renewed subscriptions among $n$ independently contacted customers.

	\subsection*{Geometric distribution (discrete)}
	Here $X$ counts the number of independent Bernoulli trials \emph{required to obtain the first success}; thus this activity uses support $\{1,2,3,\ldots\}$. Look for repeated independent attempts, constant success probability $p$, and stopping at the first success:
	\[
		P(X=x)=(1-p)^{x-1}p,\quad x=1,2,\ldots,
		\qquad F_X(x)=\begin{cases}0,&x<1,\\1-(1-p)^{\lfloor x\rfloor},&x\ge1.\end{cases}
	\]
	The PMF describes $x-1$ failures followed by a success; the CDF describes success occurring by trial $x$. For example, $X$ can be the number of sales pitches until the first order.

	\subsection*{Poisson distribution (discrete)}
	A Poisson variable counts events in a fixed time, distance, area, or other interval when events occur separately at an approximately constant average rate $\lambda>0$. Clues are an event count, a fixed interval, and a stable rate. Its support is $\{0,1,2,\ldots\}$:
	\[
		P(X=x)=e^{-\lambda}\frac{\lambda^x}{x!},\quad x=0,1,2,\ldots,
		\qquad F_X(x)=e^{-\lambda}\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor x\rfloor}\frac{\lambda^k}{k!}.
	\]
	The PMF gives the chance of exactly $x$ events and the CDF the chance of no more than $x$. A call centre might use it for the number of incoming calls in a fixed five-minute period.

	\subsection*{Uniform distribution (continuous)}
	A uniform variable treats all values in a bounded interval $[a,b]$ as having equal density. A stated minimum and maximum plus ``equally plausible anywhere'' are decisive clues. Its support is $[a,b]$, with parameters $a<b$:
	\[
		f_X(x)=\begin{cases}\dfrac1{b-a},&a\le x\le b,\\0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}
		\qquad
		F_X(x)=\begin{cases}0,&x<a,\\\dfrac{x-a}{b-a},&a\le x\le b,\\1,&x>b.\end{cases}
	\]
	The constant PDF makes equal-length subintervals equally probable; the CDF is the fraction of the support lying at or below $x$. A courier arrival time known only to be equally plausible from 2:00 to 2:30 p.m. is an example.

	\subsection*{Triangular distribution (continuous)}
	A triangular variable has minimum $a$, maximum $b$, and mode (most likely value) $c$, where $a<c<b$. Density rises linearly toward $c$ and falls linearly afterward---the key distinction from one straight linear density across the whole support. Its parameters are $a,c,b$ and support is $[a,b]$:
	\[
	 f_X(x)=\begin{cases}
	 0,&x<a,\\
	 \dfrac{2(x-a)}{(b-a)(c-a)},&a\le x\le c,\\
	 \dfrac{2(b-x)}{(b-a)(b-c)},&c<x\le b,\\
	 0,&x>b,
	 \end{cases}
	\]
	\[
	 F_X(x)=\begin{cases}
	 0,&x<a,\\
	 \dfrac{(x-a)^2}{(b-a)(c-a)},&a\le x\le c,\\
	 1-\dfrac{(b-x)^2}{(b-a)(b-c)},&c<x\le b,\\
	 1,&x>b.
	 \end{cases}
	\]
	The PDF expresses increasing then decreasing plausibility; its accumulated area is the CDF. A project cost with an expert minimum, maximum, and most likely estimate is a common business example.

	\subsection*{Linear distribution (continuous)}
	A linear distribution has a PDF $f_X(x)=mx+d$ throughout a bounded support $[a,b]$, and $0$ outside it. The constants must satisfy $mx+d\ge0$ on $[a,b]$ and
	\[
		\int_a^b(mx+d)\,dx=\frac{m}{2}(b^2-a^2)+d(b-a)=1.
	\]
	Its support is $[a,b]$ and its parameters may be written $a,b,m,d$ subject to these restrictions. The CDF is
	\[
	 F_X(x)=\begin{cases}0,&x<a,\\\dfrac{m}{2}(x^2-a^2)+d(x-a),&a\le x\le b,\\1,&x>b.\end{cases}
	\]
	Clues specify one density line increasing or decreasing across the entire interval, without an interior mode. The PDF gives relative concentration and the CDF its accumulated area. For instance, longer checkout times from 0 to 8 minutes may have density $f(t)=t/32$, increasing linearly over the whole interval.

	\subsection*{Normal distribution (continuous)}
	A normal variable models approximately symmetric, bell-shaped observations clustered around a centre, with values farther away increasingly uncommon. Its support is all real numbers and its parameters are mean $\mu$ and standard deviation $\sigma>0$:
	\[
		f_X(x)=\frac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}\exp\!\left[-\frac{(x-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right],
		\qquad F_X(x)=\Phi\!\left(\frac{x-\mu}{\sigma}\right).
	\]
	Here $\Phi$ is the standard normal CDF (it is normally read from technology or a table). The PDF describes the bell's relative concentration and the CDF gives area to the left. A large firm's many employee commute times may be approximately normal when they are symmetric around a mean.

	\label{sec:bogota-venture-decisions}

	Consider the following four situations.

	\begin{enumerate}
		\item A Bogotá fintech processes the payment for a freelance designer who needs the money to cover this month's workspace rent. Because a rejected transfer delays the freelancer's cash flow and creates follow-up work for the platform, the fintech reviews one specific transfer. Let $X_1=1$ if that transfer clears and $X_1=0$ if it is rejected, the only two possible outcomes.
		\item A new meal-preparation venture in Chapinero is seeking recurring lunch contracts to stabilise its weekly revenue. Its sales team calls potential corporate clients independently, using the same pitch and facing the same probability of securing a tasting appointment on every call. It records $X_2$, the number of calls made up to and including the first appointment, to budget sales time and decide whether direct calling is affordable.
		\item A Colombian flower exporter must decide whether a packed shipment can be released from its facility near Bogotá without costly re-sorting. It checks exactly $40$ stems from a stable packing process; each stem independently passes or fails the export-grade inspection with the same pass probability. The variable $X_3$ counts how many of the $40$ stems pass, providing evidence for the shipment decision.
		\item A promoter preparing a concert in Bogotá must ensure that refund enquiries do not overwhelm customer support after a schedule announcement. During a fixed one-hour period, refund requests occur separately at an approximately stable average rate. The promoter records $X_4$, the number of requests in that hour, to choose how many support agents to schedule while controlling labour costs.
	\end{enumerate}

		\item $X_2$ is \textbf{geometric}: independent, equal-probability calls continue until the first appointment.
		\item $X_3$ is \textbf{binomial}: it counts passes in a fixed set of $40$ independent binary inspections with constant pass probability.
		\item $X_4$ is \textbf{Poisson}: it counts separate events in a fixed hour under an approximately stable rate.
	\end{enumerate}

		\item A circular-economy venture in Kennedy buys used appliances, refurbishes them, and must leave enough margin for parts, technician labour, and warranty service. For one appliance, it estimates resale price $X_2$ between COP~$400{,}000$ and COP~$900{,}000$, with COP~$650{,}000$ most likely. Density rises linearly to that price and falls linearly afterward, helping the buyer set a prudent inventory bid.
		\item An international e-commerce platform serves Colombian sellers whose peso revenue can change when cross-border transaction values are adjusted for exchange-rate movements. For a transaction, it models the real-valued adjustment $X_3$ from $-1.5\%$ to $1.5\%$, treating every value in that bounded interval as equally plausible. Sellers can use the range to stress-test margins and decide whether their prices provide an adequate currency-risk buffer.
		\item A Colombian coffee exporter must protect quality in storage because unsuitable moisture can reduce the value of inventory awaiting shipment. From many bags processed under stable conditions, it observes moisture percentage $X_4$, which is approximately symmetric around $11\%$. Percentages become progressively less common farther below or above $11\%$, and the exporter uses this pattern to assess storage risk and prioritise inspections.
	\end{enumerate}

	\subsubsection*{C1 - Characterise the random variables.}
	\phantomsection
	\label{subsec:technology-commerce-sustainability-c1}

	All four variables are continuous.
	\begin{enumerate}
		\item $X_1$ is \textbf{linear}: its density increases along one straight line across a bounded support.
		\item $X_2$ is \textbf{triangular}: stated bounds, an interior most likely price, and linear rise and fall identify it.
		\item $X_3$ is \textbf{uniform}: all real values in a bounded interval are equally plausible.
		\item $X_4$ is \textbf{normal}: moisture values are approximately symmetric around a central value and rarer farther away.
	\end{enumerate}
