For every problem, use the same seven-step routine: (1) identify the \textbf{support};
(2) name the density's shape; (3) use total area $=1$ to find any unknown
height or parameter; (4) mark the requested interval; (5) split its region
into familiar figures; (6) calculate and add or subtract their areas; and
(7) state the probability in context.  A density is a height, so it may exceed
$1$; it is the \emph{area} that must total $1$.  Endpoints do not affect a
continuous probability, for example $P(a<X<b)=P(a\le X\le b)$.

\subsection{Uniform distribution}\label{sec:c2-intro-uniform}
A uniform density has one constant height on support $[L,U]$:
\[
 f_X(x)=\begin{cases}h,&L\le x\le U,\\0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}
 \qquad (U-L)h=1,\quad h=\frac1{U-L}.
\]
Thus every probability is a rectangle: $P(a\le X\le b)=(b-a)h$ for
$L\le a<b\le U$.

\textbf{Worked example.} A delivery time $T$ is equally plausible from $2$ to
$8$ minutes.  The support is $[2,8]$ and the whole rectangle has width $6$ and
height $h$, so $6h=1$ and $h=1/6$.  For $3\le T\le6$, the relevant rectangle
has width $6-3=3$ and height $1/6$:
\[
 P(3\le T\le6)=A_{\rm rectangle}=3\left(\frac16\right)=\frac12.
\]
There is a $50\%$ chance that delivery takes from $3$ to $6$ minutes.
\CtwoProbGraph{2}{8}{1/6}{3}{6}{2,3,6,8}{0.22}

\subsection{Linear distribution}\label{sec:c2-intro-linear}
A single linear density has $f_X(x)=mx+d$ throughout one support $[L,U]$
(and $0$ outside), with nonnegative endpoint heights
$h_L=f(L)$ and $h_U=f(U)$.  Its whole area is the trapezoid
\[
 1=\frac12(h_L+h_U)(U-L).
\]
An interval $[a,b]$ is also a trapezoid of width $b-a$ and parallel heights
$f(a),f(b)$.  A triangle is only the special case in which one endpoint height
is zero.

\textbf{Worked example.} The density of a shop's daily price adjustment $X$
(in dollars) is $f(x)=kx+0.1$ on $[0,4]$.  The support width is $4$; its endpoint
heights are $0.1$ and $4k+0.1$.  Normalisation gives
\[
 1=\tfrac12[0.1+(4k+0.1)]4=8k+0.4,
 \qquad k=0.075.
\]
For $1\le X\le3$, $f(1)=0.175$ and $f(3)=0.325$.  The required figure is a
trapezoid of width $2$ with those parallel heights:
\[
 P(1\le X\le3)=\tfrac12(0.175+0.325)(2)=0.5.
\]
Both support endpoints have positive density, so this is genuinely linear,
not triangular.
\COneGraph{0}{4}{0.075*x+0.1}{1}{3}{0,1,3,4}{0.45}
\secInicio[sec:c2-introduction][sec:c2-intro-linear]

\subsection{Triangular distribution}\label{sec:c2-intro-triangular}
A triangular density has support $[L,U]$, height $0$ at both ends, and mode
$M$ ($L<M<U$) with height $H$.  It rises linearly on $[L,M]$ and falls linearly
on $[M,U]$.  Since the whole figure has base $U-L$,
\[
 1=\tfrac12(U-L)H,\qquad H=\frac2{U-L}.
\]
Heights inside either sloping side are found by proportional similar triangles.

\textbf{Worked example.} A project's cost overrun $C$ (thousand dollars) has
minimum $0$, most likely value $2$, and maximum $6$.  Its support is $[0,6]$,
mode is $2$, and $\tfrac12(6)H=1$, so $H=1/3$.  To find
$P(1\le C\le4)$, subtract two tail triangles.  At $C=1$ the height is $1/6$;
at $C=4$ it is also $1/6$.  The left tail has base $1$, height $1/6$; the
right tail has base $2$, height $1/6$:
\[
 P(1\le C\le4)=1-\tfrac12(1)(\tfrac16)-\tfrac12(2)(\tfrac16)
 =1-\tfrac1{12}-\tfrac16=\tfrac34.
\]
The region crosses the mode, but subtraction avoids splitting it into two
trapezoids.
\COneGraph{0}{6}{(x<=2)*(x/6)+(x>2)*((6-x)/12)}{1}{4}{0,1,2,4,6}{0.4}
\secInicio[sec:c2-introduction][sec:c2-intro-triangular]

\subsection{Piecewise uniform distribution}\label{sec:c2-intro-piecewise-uniform}
Here the density is constant \emph{within} each interval but may jump at a
breakpoint.  Normalisation is the sum of rectangles:
$1=\sum(\text{segment width})(\text{segment height})$.

\textbf{Worked example.} A warehouse waiting time $W$ has
\[
 f_W(w)=\begin{cases}k,&0\le w<2,\\2k,&2\le w\le5,\\0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}
\]
The support is $[0,5]$.  A width-$2$, height-$k$ rectangle plus a width-$3$,
height-$2k$ rectangle must have area $1$: $2k+6k=1$, so $k=1/8$.
The event $1\le W\le4$ spans the jump and uses two rectangles:
\[
 P(1\le W\le4)=\underbrace{(2-1)(1/8)}_{A_1}
 +\underbrace{(4-2)(1/4)}_{A_2}=\frac18+\frac12=\frac58.
\]
\CtwoProbGraph{0}{5}{(x<2)*(1/8)+(x>=2)*(1/4)}{1}{4}{0,1,2,4,5}{0.3}
\secInicio[sec:c2-introduction][sec:c2-intro-piecewise-uniform]

\subsection{Piecewise linear distribution}\label{sec:c2-intro-piecewise-linear}
A piecewise-linear density joins straight segments at stated breakpoints.
Read the height at each endpoint and breakpoint, then treat each piece as a
triangle, rectangle, or trapezoid.  Continuity means adjacent pieces meet at
the same height.

\textbf{Worked example.} Demand $D$ has support $[0,6]$.  Its density joins
$(0,0)$, $(2,H)$, $(4,H)$, and $(6,0)$.  The whole shape is a left triangle
(base $2$), rectangle (width $2$), and right triangle (base $2$):
\[
 1=\tfrac12(2)H+(2)H+\tfrac12(2)H=4H,
 \qquad H=\tfrac14.
\]
For $1\le D\le5$, subtract the two congruent tail triangles.  By linearity,
the heights at $1$ and $5$ are $H/2=1/8$:
\[
 P(1\le D\le5)=1-\tfrac12(1)(\tfrac18)-\tfrac12(1)(\tfrac18)=\frac78.
\]
This reusable approach identifies all three geometric pieces and uses
subtraction from total area $1$.
\COneGraph{0}{6}{(x<=2)*(x/8)+(x>2)*(x<=4)*(1/4)+(x>4)*((6-x)/8)}{1}{5}{0,1,2,4,5,6}{0.3}
\secInicio[sec:c2-introduction][sec:c2-intro-piecewise-linear]

\subsection{Service window}\label{sec:c2-uniform-service}
A Bogotá restaurant hires an independent refrigeration technician to make an
urgent visit between 10:00 and 18:00.  Because the technician's earlier jobs
vary in length, the restaurant models the arrival time $T$ (hour of the day)
as uniform on $[10,18]$.  The manager must decide when to keep an employee
available to receive the technician and wants to assess the risk of a late
arrival before finalising the shift schedule.
\CtwoProbGraph[t]{10}{18}{1/8}{12}{15}{10,12,15,16,18}{0.16}
\textbf{Questions/Tasks.}
\begin{enumerate}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-uniform-service-a}{2.1}{Find the constant density and identify its support.}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-uniform-service-b}{2.2}{Find $P(12\le T\le15)$ using a rectangle.}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-uniform-service-c}{2.3}{Find $P(T>16)$ and interpret it.}
\end{enumerate}
\subsubsection*{C2.1 - Find the constant density and identify its support.}
\phantomsection
\label{subsec:c2-uniform-service-a}

The support is the stated interval $[10,18]$, whose width is $18-10=8$.  If the constant density is $h$, normalisation gives
\[
 1=(18-10)h=8h \quad\Longrightarrow\quad h=\frac18.
\]
Thus $f_T(t)=1/8$ for $10\le t\le18$ (and $0$ otherwise).

\subsubsection*{C2.2 - Find $P(12\le T\le15)$ using a rectangle.}
\phantomsection
\label{subsec:c2-uniform-service-b}

The requested interval has width $15-12=3$.  Using the density from C2.1, its rectangular area is
\[
 P(12\le T\le15)=(15-12)\left(\frac18\right)=\frac38.
\]

\subsubsection*{C2.3 - Find $P(T>16)$ and interpret it.}
\phantomsection
\label{subsec:c2-uniform-service-c}

The part of the support above $16$ is $[16,18]$, of width $2$.  Therefore
\[
 P(T>16)=(18-16)\left(\frac18\right)=\frac14.
\]
Hence there is a one-quarter chance that the technician arrives after 16:00.

\subsection{Price quotation}\label{sec:c2-uniform-price}
A small Bogotá online retailer imports phone accessories and must set a
selling price before its overseas supplier confirms the next wholesale quote.
Based on comparable offers, the unit quote $Q$ (dollars) is modelled as uniform
on $[20,U]$ with density $1/12$.  The owner needs to recover the upper end of
the quotation range and compare the chances of quotes in different bands to
choose a price with enough margin.
\CtwoProbGraph[q]{20}{32}{1/12}{23}{29}{20,23,24,26,29,32}{0.11}
\textbf{Questions/Tasks.}
\begin{enumerate}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-uniform-price-a}{2.1}{Determine $U$ from total rectangular area.}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-uniform-price-b}{2.2}{Calculate $P(23\le Q\le29)$ and $P(Q<23\text{ or }Q>29)$.}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-uniform-price-c}{2.3}{Compare $P(20\le Q\le24)$ and $P(26\le Q\le32)$ geometrically.}
\end{enumerate}
\subsubsection*{C2.1 - Determine $U$ from total rectangular area.}
\phantomsection
\label{subsec:c2-uniform-price-a}

The full rectangle must have area $1$.  Its width is $U-20$ and its height is $1/12$, so
\[
 (U-20)\frac1{12}=1 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad U-20=12
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad U=32.
\]

\subsubsection*{C2.2 - Calculate $P(23\le Q\le29)$ and $P(Q<23\text{ or }Q>29)$.}
\phantomsection
\label{subsec:c2-uniform-price-b}

The central interval has width $29-23=6$, so
\[
 P(23\le Q\le29)=6\left(\frac1{12}\right)=\frac12.
\]
The two outside regions form its complement; hence
\[
 P(Q<23\text{ or }Q>29)=1-\frac12=\frac12.
\]

\subsubsection*{C2.3 - Compare $P(20\le Q\le24)$ and $P(26\le Q\le32)$ geometrically.}
\phantomsection
\label{subsec:c2-uniform-price-c}

For a uniform density, probability is interval width times $1/12$.  Thus
\[
 P(20\le Q\le24)=4\left(\frac1{12}\right)=\frac13,
 \qquad P(26\le Q\le32)=6\left(\frac1{12}\right)=\frac12.
\]
The second probability is larger because its rectangle is wider; location within the support does not affect the height.

\subsection{Production run}\label{sec:c2-uniform-production}
A family-owned snack producer in Bogotá supplies several neighbourhood shops.
Variations in setup time make its daily output $Y$ (hundreds of units) uniform
on $[4,9]$.  The operations manager treats 550--800 units as the range that
can be packed and dispatched with the regular crew, so the probability of
falling inside or outside that range informs staffing and delivery plans.
\CtwoProbGraph[y]{4}{9}{1/5}{5.5}{8}{4,5.5,8,9}{0.26}
\textbf{Questions/Tasks.}
\begin{enumerate}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-uniform-production-a}{2.1}{Write the density, including its value outside the support.}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-uniform-production-b}{2.2}{Find $P(5.5\le Y\le8)$ by rectangle area.}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-uniform-production-c}{2.3}{Find $P(Y<5.5\text{ or }Y>8)$ using a complement and interpret it.}
\end{enumerate}
\subsubsection*{C2.1 - Write the density, including its value outside the support.}
\phantomsection
\label{subsec:c2-uniform-production-a}

The support width is $9-4=5$, so the constant height is $1/5$.  Including values outside the support,
\[
 f_Y(y)=\begin{cases}\dfrac15,&4\le y\le9,\\[1mm]0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}
\]
Indeed, $5(1/5)=1$.

\subsubsection*{C2.2 - Find $P(5.5\le Y\le8)$ by rectangle area.}
\phantomsection
\label{subsec:c2-uniform-production-b}

The interval width is $8-5.5=2.5$.  Its rectangular area is
\[
 P(5.5\le Y\le8)=2.5\left(\frac15\right)=\frac{2.5}{5}=\frac12.
\]

\subsubsection*{C2.3 - Find $P(Y<5.5\text{ or }Y>8)$ using a complement and interpret it.}
\phantomsection
\label{subsec:c2-uniform-production-c}

The event outside $[5.5,8]$ is the complement of the event in C2.2.  Therefore
\[
 P(Y<5.5\text{ or }Y>8)=1-P(5.5\le Y\le8)=1-\frac12=\frac12.
\]
Thus output is outside $550$--$800$ units on half of the days.

\subsection{Increasing demand}\label{sec:c2-linear-increasing}
A Bogotá food-delivery startup is planning courier capacity for a weekend
promotion.  Its demand index $X$, from $0$ to $4$, has density $f(x)=kx$ on
$[0,4]$ and $0$ otherwise; the increasing density reflects that stronger
demand levels are more plausible during the promotion.  The dispatcher wants
the probabilities of demand between $2$ and $3$ and above $3$ to decide how
much reserve courier capacity to arrange.
\COneGraph[x]{0}{4}{x/8}{2}{3}{0,2,3,4}{0.62}
\textbf{Questions/Tasks.}
\begin{enumerate}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-linear-increasing-a}{2.1}{Find $k$ by the area of the whole triangle.}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-linear-increasing-b}{2.2}{Find $P(2\le X\le3)$ as a trapezoid.}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-linear-increasing-c}{2.3}{Find $P(X>3)$ as a triangle or trapezoid.}
\end{enumerate}
\subsubsection*{C2.1 - Find $k$ by the area of the whole triangle.}
\phantomsection
\label{subsec:c2-linear-increasing-a}

The density forms a triangle with base $4$ and height $f(4)=4k$.  Normalisation gives
\[
 1=\frac12(4)(4k)=8k \quad\Longrightarrow\quad k=\frac18.
\]

\subsubsection*{C2.2 - Find $P(2\le X\le3)$ as a trapezoid.}
\phantomsection
\label{subsec:c2-linear-increasing-b}

Using $k=1/8$, the endpoint heights are $f(2)=2/8=1/4$ and $f(3)=3/8$.  The width-$1$ trapezoid therefore has area
\[
 P(2\le X\le3)=\frac12(3-2)\left(\frac14+\frac38\right)=\frac5{16}.
\]

\subsubsection*{C2.3 - Find $P(X>3)$ as a triangle or trapezoid.}
\phantomsection
\label{subsec:c2-linear-increasing-c}

At $x=3$ and $x=4$, the heights are $3/8$ and $1/2$.  The region is a width-$1$ trapezoid, so
\[
 P(X>3)=\frac12(4-3)\left(\frac38+\frac12\right)=\frac7{16}.
\]
(The endpoints have probability zero, so using $>$ rather than $\ge$ does not change the area.)

\subsection{Decreasing delay}\label{sec:c2-linear-decreasing}
A Colombian flower exporter tracks the delay before refrigerated shipments
leave its packing facility.  The dispatch delay $D$ (minutes) has density
$f(d)=k(6-d)$ on $[0,6]$, so short delays are more likely and no delay exceeds
six minutes in the model.  Since delays beyond two minutes can compress the
transfer window at the airport, the logistics coordinator wants to quantify
that risk when setting the loading timetable.
\COneGraph[d]{0}{6}{(6-x)/18}{0}{2}{0,2,6}{0.42}
\textbf{Questions/Tasks.}
\begin{enumerate}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-linear-decreasing-a}{2.1}{Find $k$ from the whole triangular area.}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-linear-decreasing-b}{2.2}{Find $P(D\le2)$ geometrically.}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-linear-decreasing-c}{2.3}{Use a complement to find $P(D>2)$ and interpret it.}
\end{enumerate}
\subsubsection*{C2.1 - Find $k$ from the whole triangular area.}
\phantomsection
\label{subsec:c2-linear-decreasing-a}

The support region is a triangle of base $6$ and height $f(0)=6k$.  Hence
\[
 1=\frac12(6)(6k)=18k \quad\Longrightarrow\quad k=\frac1{18}.
\]

\subsubsection*{C2.2 - Find $P(D\le2)$ geometrically.}
\phantomsection
\label{subsec:c2-linear-decreasing-b}

With $k=1/18$, $f(0)=1/3$ and $f(2)=(6-2)/18=2/9$.  The area from $0$ to $2$ is a trapezoid:
\[
 P(D\le2)=\frac12(2)\left(\frac13+\frac29\right)=\frac59.
\]

\subsubsection*{C2.3 - Use a complement to find $P(D>2)$ and interpret it.}
\phantomsection
\label{subsec:c2-linear-decreasing-c}

The events $D\le2$ and $D>2$ are complementary.  Using C2.2,
\[
 P(D>2)=1-P(D\le2)=1-\frac59=\frac49.
\]
Thus four ninths of dispatches wait more than two minutes.

\subsection{Trapezoidal returns}\label{sec:c2-linear-trapezoid}
A Bogotá freelancer is comparing the uncertain payoff from committing spare
cash to a short-term investment rather than keeping it available for expenses.
The investment's return index $R$ has a density that is one straight line on
$[0,5]$, with $f(0)=0.1$ and $f(5)=h>0$.  To judge whether the middle return
range from $1$ to $4$ is sufficiently likely, the freelancer first needs a
valid density and then the probability inside and outside that range.
\COneGraph[r]{0}{5}{0.1+0.04*x}{1}{4}{0,1,4,5}{0.38}
\textbf{Questions/Tasks.}
\begin{enumerate}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-linear-trapezoid-a}{2.1}{Determine $h$ by normalisation, then write the linear density.}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-linear-trapezoid-b}{2.2}{Calculate $P(1\le R\le4)$ as a trapezoid.}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-linear-trapezoid-c}{2.3}{Calculate $P(R<1\text{ or }R>4)$ by a complement.}
\end{enumerate}
\subsubsection*{C2.1 - Determine $h$ by normalisation, then write the linear density.}
\phantomsection
\label{subsec:c2-linear-trapezoid-a}

The whole support is a width-$5$ trapezoid with endpoint heights $0.1$ and $h$.  Thus
\[
 1=\frac12(0.1+h)(5) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad 0.1+h=0.4
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad h=0.3.
\]
Its slope is $(0.3-0.1)/5=0.04$, so $f(r)=0.1+0.04r$ on $[0,5]$ (and $0$ otherwise).

\subsubsection*{C2.2 - Calculate $P(1\le R\le4)$ as a trapezoid.}
\phantomsection
\label{subsec:c2-linear-trapezoid-b}

First evaluate the endpoint heights:
\[
 f(1)=0.1+0.04(1)=0.14,\qquad f(4)=0.1+0.04(4)=0.26.
\]
The interval has width $3$, so its trapezoidal area is
\[
 P(1\le R\le4)=\frac12(0.14+0.26)(4-1)=0.6.
\]

\subsubsection*{C2.3 - Calculate $P(R<1\text{ or }R>4)$ by a complement.}
\phantomsection
\label{subsec:c2-linear-trapezoid-c}

The event $R<1$ or $R>4$ is the complement of the central interval.  Therefore
\[
 P(R<1\text{ or }R>4)=1-P(1\le R\le4)=1-0.6=0.4.
\]
Both support endpoint heights are positive, consistent with a trapezoid rather than a triangle.

\subsection{Symmetric sales}\label{sec:c2-triangular-symmetric}
A new Bogotá coffee kiosk must order perishable ingredients before observing
its daily sales.  Sales $S$ (hundreds of drinks) are modelled with triangular
support $[0,8]$ and mode $4$, based on the owner's minimum, most likely, and
maximum planning estimates.  The regular ingredient order covers sales from
$2$ to $6$, so the owner wants to measure both the chance that this range is
adequate and the relative risks of very low sales and stockout-level demand.
\COneGraph[s]{0}{8}{(x<=4)*(x/16)+(x>4)*((8-x)/16)}{2}{6}{0,2,4,6,8}{0.31}
\textbf{Questions/Tasks.}
\begin{enumerate}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-triangular-symmetric-a}{2.1}{Find the modal height.}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-triangular-symmetric-b}{2.2}{Find $P(2\le S\le6)$ by subtracting tail triangles.}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-triangular-symmetric-c}{2.3}{Compare $P(S<2)$ and $P(S>6)$.}
\end{enumerate}
\subsubsection*{C2.1 - Find the modal height.}
\phantomsection
\label{subsec:c2-triangular-symmetric-a}

The entire triangular density has base $8-0=8$ and height $H$.  Normalisation gives
\[
 1=\frac12(8)H=4H \quad\Longrightarrow\quad H=\frac14.
\]

\subsubsection*{C2.2 - Find $P(2\le S\le6)$ by subtracting tail triangles.}
\phantomsection
\label{subsec:c2-triangular-symmetric-b}

Linearity and symmetry give $f(2)=f(6)=H/2=1/8$.  Each excluded tail is a triangle of base $2$ and height $1/8$, so
\[
 P(2\le S\le6)=1-\frac12(2)\frac18-\frac12(2)\frac18
 =1-\frac18-\frac18=\frac34.
\]

\subsubsection*{C2.3 - Compare $P(S<2)$ and $P(S>6)$.}
\phantomsection
\label{subsec:c2-triangular-symmetric-c}

The mode $4$ is the midpoint of $[0,8]$, so the two tails are congruent.  Calculating each area,
\[
 P(S<2)=\frac12(2)\frac18=\frac18,
 \qquad P(S>6)=\frac12(2)\frac18=\frac18.
\]
Therefore the probabilities are equal.

\subsection{Asymmetric project time}\label{sec:c2-triangular-asymmetric}
A Colombian freelance developer is estimating the time needed to complete an
e-commerce integration for a client.  Completion time $T$ (weeks) is
triangular on $[1,7]$ with mode $3$, combining the optimistic, most likely,
and delayed outcomes in the project plan.  Because finishing between weeks
$2$ and $5$ would keep both the client's launch and the developer's next
contract on schedule, the developer wants the probability of that window.
\COneGraph[t]{1}{7}{(x<=3)*((x-1)/6)+(x>3)*((7-x)/12)}{2}{5}{1,2,3,5,7}{0.41}
\textbf{Questions/Tasks.}
\begin{enumerate}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-triangular-asymmetric-a}{2.1}{Find the modal height and the heights at $2$ and $5$.}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-triangular-asymmetric-b}{2.2}{Find $P(2\le T\le5)$ using two trapezoids split at the mode.}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-triangular-asymmetric-c}{2.3}{Interpret the probability in context.}
\end{enumerate}
\subsubsection*{C2.1 - Find the modal height and the heights at $2$ and $5$.}
\phantomsection
\label{subsec:c2-triangular-asymmetric-a}

The support has width $7-1=6$, so
\[
 1=\frac12(6)H \quad\Longrightarrow\quad H=\frac13.
\]
On the rising side, $2$ is halfway from $1$ to the mode $3$, giving $f(2)=H/2=1/6$.  On the falling side,
\[
 f(5)=H\frac{7-5}{7-3}=\frac13\frac24=\frac16.
\]

\subsubsection*{C2.2 - Find $P(2\le T\le5)$ using two trapezoids split at the mode.}
\phantomsection
\label{subsec:c2-triangular-asymmetric-b}

Split the region at the mode.  From $2$ to $3$ the trapezoid has width $1$ and heights $1/6,1/3$; from $3$ to $5$ it has width $2$ and heights $1/3,1/6$.  Hence
\[
 P(2\le T\le5)=\frac12(1)\left(\frac16+\frac13\right)
 +\frac12(2)\left(\frac13+\frac16\right)
 =\frac14+\frac12=\frac34.
\]

\subsubsection*{C2.3 - Interpret the probability in context.}
\phantomsection
\label{subsec:c2-triangular-asymmetric-c}

C2.2 gives $P(2\le T\le5)=3/4$.  Converting to a percentage,
\[
 \frac34\times100\%=75\%.
\]
Thus there is a $75\%$ chance that completion occurs between two and five weeks.

\subsection{Inventory tail}\label{sec:c2-triangular-tail}
A Medellín-based seller ships handmade products to customers across Colombia
and must reserve packaging inventory for the next sales period.  Demand $Q$
(hundreds of packages) has triangular support $[0,10]$, mode $2$, and unknown
height $H$.  Holding too much stock ties up cash, while demand of at least $6$
could exhaust the standard reserve, so the owner wants the upper-tail risk and
the probability that demand stays between $1$ and $6$.
\COneGraph[q]{0}{10}{(x<=2)*(x/10)+(x>2)*((10-x)/40)}{1}{6}{0,1,2,6,10}{0.25}
\textbf{Questions/Tasks.}
\begin{enumerate}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-triangular-tail-a}{2.1}{Determine $H$.}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-triangular-tail-b}{2.2}{Find $P(Q\ge6)$ from the right tail triangle.}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-triangular-tail-c}{2.3}{Find $P(1\le Q\le6)$ by subtracting both outside tails.}
\end{enumerate}
\subsubsection*{C2.1 - Determine $H$.}
\phantomsection
\label{subsec:c2-triangular-tail-a}

The full triangular support has base $10$.  Therefore
\[
 1=\frac12(10)H=5H \quad\Longrightarrow\quad H=\frac15.
\]

\subsubsection*{C2.2 - Find $P(Q\ge6)$ from the right tail triangle.}
\phantomsection
\label{subsec:c2-triangular-tail-b}

On the descending side from $2$ to $10$, proportional heights give
\[
 f(6)=H\frac{10-6}{10-2}=\frac15\frac48=\frac1{10}.
\]
The right tail from $6$ to $10$ is a triangle, so
\[
 P(Q\ge6)=\frac12(10-6)f(6)=\frac12(4)\frac1{10}=\frac15.
\]

\subsubsection*{C2.3 - Find $P(1\le Q\le6)$ by subtracting both outside tails.}
\phantomsection
\label{subsec:c2-triangular-tail-c}

At $q=1$, halfway along the rising side, $f(1)=H/2=1/10$.  The excluded left tail has area $\frac12(1)(1/10)=1/20$; by C2.2 the excluded right tail has area $1/5$.  Thus
\[
 P(1\le Q\le6)=1-\frac1{20}-\frac15
 =1-\frac1{20}-\frac4{20}=\frac34.
\]

\subsection{Freight loading}\label{sec:c2-piecewise-uniform-freight}
A logistics operator near Bogotá coordinates the loading of trucks carrying
goods to regional distribution centres.  Loading time $L$ (hours) has density
$k$ on $[0,2)$ and $3k$ on $[2,4]$, reflecting that loads requiring the longer
handling process occur more often.  A loading bay is booked in fixed blocks,
so the operator wants the probability of a one-to-three-hour job and the risk
that a truck occupies the bay for at least two hours.
\CtwoProbGraph[l]{0}{4}{(x<2)*(1/8)+(x>=2)*(3/8)}{1}{3}{0,1,2,3,4}{0.46}
\textbf{Questions/Tasks.}
\begin{enumerate}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-piecewise-uniform-freight-a}{2.1}{Find $k$ using two rectangles.}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-piecewise-uniform-freight-b}{2.2}{Find $P(1\le L\le3)$ by summing rectangles.}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-piecewise-uniform-freight-c}{2.3}{Find and interpret $P(L\ge2)$.}
\end{enumerate}
\subsubsection*{C2.1 - Find $k$ using two rectangles.}
\phantomsection
\label{subsec:c2-piecewise-uniform-freight-a}

The two support intervals both have width $2$, with heights $k$ and $3k$.  Normalisation gives
\[
 1=2k+2(3k)=8k \quad\Longrightarrow\quad k=\frac18.
\]

\subsubsection*{C2.2 - Find $P(1\le L\le3)$ by summing rectangles.}
\phantomsection
\label{subsec:c2-piecewise-uniform-freight-b}

From $1$ to $2$ the rectangle has width $1$ and height $1/8$; from $2$ to $3$ it has width $1$ and height $3/8$.  Therefore
\[
 P(1\le L\le3)=1\left(\frac18\right)+1\left(\frac38\right)=\frac12.
\]

\subsubsection*{C2.3 - Find and interpret $P(L\ge2)$.}
\phantomsection
\label{subsec:c2-piecewise-uniform-freight-c}

The interval $[2,4]$ has width $2$ and density $3k=3/8$.  Thus
\[
 P(L\ge2)=2\left(\frac38\right)=\frac34=75\%.
\]
Hence $75\%$ of loads take at least two hours.

\subsection{Density audit}\label{sec:c2-piecewise-uniform-validity}
A fintech team in Bogotá is testing a model for the time $X$ (days) that new
merchants take to complete account activation.  Before using the model to
forecast support workload, an analyst proposes $f(x)=0.2$ on $[0,2)$, $0.1$
on $[2,5)$, and $0.15$ on $[5,7]$, with $0$ elsewhere.  The team must audit
whether this is a valid probability density and, if it is, estimate the share
of merchants whose activation takes from one to six days.
\CtwoProbGraph[x]{0}{7}{(x<2)*0.2+(x>=2)*(x<5)*0.1+(x>=5)*0.15}{1}{6}{0,1,2,5,6,7}{0.26}
\textbf{Questions/Tasks.}
\begin{enumerate}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-piecewise-uniform-validity-a}{2.1}{Verify nonnegativity and calculate the three rectangular areas.}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-piecewise-uniform-validity-b}{2.2}{Decide whether this is a valid density and justify.}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-piecewise-uniform-validity-c}{2.3}{If valid, find $P(1\le X\le6)$.}
\end{enumerate}
\subsubsection*{C2.1 - Verify nonnegativity and calculate the three rectangular areas.}
\phantomsection
\label{subsec:c2-piecewise-uniform-validity-a}

The proposed heights $0.2$, $0.1$, and $0.15$ are all nonnegative.  Multiplying width by height on each interval gives
\[
 A_1=2(0.2)=0.4,\qquad A_2=3(0.1)=0.3,\qquad A_3=2(0.15)=0.3.
\]

\subsubsection*{C2.2 - Decide whether this is a valid density and justify.}
\phantomsection
\label{subsec:c2-piecewise-uniform-validity-b}

A density must be nonnegative and have total area $1$.  Nonnegativity was verified in C2.1, and
\[
 A_1+A_2+A_3=0.4+0.3+0.3=1.
\]
Therefore the proposed function is a valid probability density.

\subsubsection*{C2.3 - If valid, find $P(1\le X\le6)$.}
\phantomsection
\label{subsec:c2-piecewise-uniform-validity-c}

The event uses width $1$ in the first interval, all width $3$ of the second, and width $1$ in the third.  Hence
\[
 P(1\le X\le6)=1(0.2)+3(0.1)+1(0.15)
 =0.2+0.3+0.15=0.65.
\]

\subsection{Order processing}\label{sec:c2-piecewise-uniform-orders}
An independent Bogotá cosmetics retailer fulfils online orders through a
shared warehouse.  Processing time $P$ (hours) has density $0.1$ on $[0,2)$,
$h$ on $[2,5)$, and $0.2$ on $[5,7]$, representing three operational time
bands observed by the retailer.  To select realistic customer notifications,
the owner needs to determine the missing density height and assess how often
processing falls between one and six hours or outside that window.
\CtwoProbGraph[p]{0}{7}{(x<2)*0.1+(x>=2)*(x<5)*(2/15)+(x>=5)*0.2}{1}{6}{0,1,2,5,6,7}{0.26}
\textbf{Questions/Tasks.}
\begin{enumerate}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-piecewise-uniform-orders-a}{2.1}{Find $h$ from total area $1$.}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-piecewise-uniform-orders-b}{2.2}{Find $P(1\le P\le6)$ using three rectangles.}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-piecewise-uniform-orders-c}{2.3}{Find $P(P<1\text{ or }P>6)$ as a check.}
\end{enumerate}
\subsubsection*{C2.1 - Find $h$ from total area $1$.}
\phantomsection
\label{subsec:c2-piecewise-uniform-orders-a}

Normalisation adds the three rectangular areas:
\[
 2(0.1)+3h+2(0.2)=1.
\]
Thus $0.6+3h=1$, so $3h=0.4$ and
\[
 h=\frac{0.4}{3}=\frac2{15}.
\]

\subsubsection*{C2.2 - Find $P(1\le P\le6)$ using three rectangles.}
\phantomsection
\label{subsec:c2-piecewise-uniform-orders-b}

The interval crosses all three pieces.  Their included widths are $1$, $3$, and $1$, respectively, so
\[
 P(1\le P\le6)=1(0.1)+3\left(\frac2{15}\right)+1(0.2)
 =0.1+0.4+0.2=0.7.
\]

\subsubsection*{C2.3 - Find $P(P<1\text{ or }P>6)$ as a check.}
\phantomsection
\label{subsec:c2-piecewise-uniform-orders-c}

The complement consists of $[0,1)$ and $(6,7]$.  These are width-$1$ rectangles with heights $0.1$ and $0.2$, so
\[
 P(P<1\text{ or }P>6)=1(0.1)+1(0.2)=0.3.
\]
Also, $1-0.7=0.3$, which checks the result from C2.2.

\subsection{Demand peak}\label{sec:c2-piecewise-linear-demand}
A Bogotá meal-preparation business is deciding how much kitchen capacity to
reserve for orders placed through digital platforms.  Its demand index $D$ on
$[0,6]$ has a continuous density joining $(0,0)$, $(2,H)$, $(5,H/2)$, and
$(6,0)$, capturing a rise to the most common demand region followed by a
gradual decline.  The existing staffing plan covers index values from $1$ to
$5$, so the owner wants to determine how often demand should remain in that
planning range.
\COneGraph[d]{0}{6}{(x<=2)*(x/7)+(x>2)*(x<=5)*((8-x)/21)+(x>5)*((6-x)/7)}{1}{5}{0,1,2,5,6}{0.34}
\textbf{Questions/Tasks.}
\begin{enumerate}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-piecewise-linear-demand-a}{2.1}{Find $H$ by decomposing the support into one triangle and two trapezoids.}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-piecewise-linear-demand-b}{2.2}{Find $P(1\le D\le5)$ by subtracting the two tail triangles.}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-piecewise-linear-demand-c}{2.3}{Interpret the result.}
\end{enumerate}
\subsubsection*{C2.1 - Find $H$ by decomposing the support into one triangle and two trapezoids.}
\phantomsection
\label{subsec:c2-piecewise-linear-demand-a}

The three pieces have areas
\[
 A_1=\frac12(2)H=H,\quad
 A_2=\frac12(3)\left(H+\frac H2\right)=\frac{9H}{4},\quad
 A_3=\frac12(1)\frac H2=\frac H4.
\]
Thus $1=H+9H/4+H/4=7H/2$, giving $H=2/7$.

\subsubsection*{C2.2 - Find $P(1\le D\le5)$ by subtracting the two tail triangles.}
\phantomsection
\label{subsec:c2-piecewise-linear-demand-b}

At $x=1$, linearity gives height $H/2=1/7$, so the left tail area is $\frac12(1)(1/7)=1/14$.  The right tail from $5$ to $6$ has base $1$ and height $H/2=1/7$, also giving $1/14$.  Therefore
\[
 P(1\le D\le5)=1-\frac1{14}-\frac1{14}=\frac67.
\]

\subsubsection*{C2.3 - Interpret the result.}
\phantomsection
\label{subsec:c2-piecewise-linear-demand-c}

The probability from C2.2 is $6/7$.  In percentage form,
\[
 \frac67\times100\%\approx85.7\%.
\]
Thus demand lies in the planning range from $1$ to $5$ about $85.7\%$ of the time.

\subsection{Delivery profile}\label{sec:c2-piecewise-linear-delivery}
A Colombian e-commerce business studies the time $T$ (hours) between an order
leaving its Bogotá warehouse and reaching a local customer.  Its delivery-time
density is continuous through $(0,0)$, $(2,H)$, $(4,H)$, $(6,H/2)$, and
$(8,0)$, representing a peak across the usual two-to-four-hour operating
window and declining likelihood thereafter.  The logistics manager wants to
evaluate a broad one-to-seven-hour service promise and a narrower
three-to-six-hour staffing window before updating delivery estimates.
\COneGraph[t]{0}{8}{(x<=2)*(x/10)+(x>2)*(x<=4)*(1/5)+(x>4)*(x<=6)*((8-x)/20)+(x>6)*((8-x)/20)}{1}{7}{0,1,2,3,4,6,7,8}{0.25}
\textbf{Questions/Tasks.}
\begin{enumerate}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-piecewise-linear-delivery-a}{2.1}{Find $H$ using the triangle, rectangle, and trapezoids across the support.}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-piecewise-linear-delivery-b}{2.2}{Find $P(1\le T\le7)$ using a complement.}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-piecewise-linear-delivery-c}{2.3}{Find $P(3\le T\le6)$ by a rectangle plus a trapezoid.}
\end{enumerate}
\subsubsection*{C2.1 - Find $H$ using the triangle, rectangle, and trapezoids across the support.}
\phantomsection
\label{subsec:c2-piecewise-linear-delivery-a}

From left to right, the geometric areas are
\[
 \frac12(2)H=H,\quad 2H,\quad
 \frac12(2)\left(H+\frac H2\right)=\frac{3H}{2},\quad
 \frac12(2)\frac H2=\frac H2.
\]
Their sum is $5H=1$, so $H=1/5$.

\subsubsection*{C2.2 - Find $P(1\le T\le7)$ using a complement.}
\phantomsection
\label{subsec:c2-piecewise-linear-delivery-b}

At $t=1$, $f(1)=H/2=1/10$, so the left tail area is $\frac12(1)(1/10)=1/20$.  From $(6,H/2)$ to $(8,0)$, $f(7)=H/4=1/20$, so the right tail area is $\frac12(1)(1/20)=1/40$.  Hence
\[
 P(1\le T\le7)=1-\frac1{20}-\frac1{40}=\frac{37}{40}.
\]

\subsubsection*{C2.3 - Find $P(3\le T\le6)$ by a rectangle plus a trapezoid.}
\phantomsection
\label{subsec:c2-piecewise-linear-delivery-c}

From $3$ to $4$ there is a width-$1$, height-$H=1/5$ rectangle.  From $4$ to $6$ there is a width-$2$ trapezoid with heights $1/5$ and $H/2=1/10$.  Thus
\[
 P(3\le T\le6)=1\left(\frac15\right)
 +\frac12(2)\left(\frac15+\frac1{10}\right)
 =\frac15+\frac3{10}=\frac12.
\]

\subsection{Liquidity window}\label{sec:c2-piecewise-linear-liquidity}
A Bogotá digital marketplace monitors a liquidity index $X$ to plan the cash
available for weekly seller payouts and refunds.  The index has a continuous
density joining $(0,0)$, $(1,H)$, $(3,H)$, $(5,0)$, and $(7,H)$ on support
$[0,7]$; the piecewise profile represents several possible cash-flow regimes
in the marketplace's internal planning model.  Values from $0.5$ to $4$ fit
the normal funding plan, while values above $4$ may require additional liquid
funds, so the finance team needs both probabilities.
\COneGraph[x]{0}{7}{(x<=1)*(2*x/9)+(x>1)*(x<=3)*(2/9)+(x>3)*(x<=5)*((5-x)/9)+(x>5)*((x-5)/9)}{0.5}{4}{0,0.5,1,3,4,5,7}{0.27}
\textbf{Questions/Tasks.}
\begin{enumerate}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-piecewise-linear-liquidity-a}{2.1}{Find $H$ from all four geometric pieces.}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-piecewise-linear-liquidity-b}{2.2}{Find $P(0.5\le X\le4)$, splitting at $1$ and $3$.}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-piecewise-linear-liquidity-c}{2.3}{Find $P(X>4)$ by a complement and identify the pieces avoided.}
\end{enumerate}
\subsubsection*{C2.1 - Find $H$ from all four geometric pieces.}
\phantomsection
\label{subsec:c2-piecewise-linear-liquidity-a}

The support consists of a base-$1$ triangle, a width-$2$ rectangle, and two base-$2$ triangles.  Thus
\[
 1=\frac12(1)H+2H+\frac12(2)H+\frac12(2)H
 =\frac92H,
\]
so $H=2/9$.

\subsubsection*{C2.2 - Find $P(0.5\le X\le4)$, splitting at $1$ and $3$.}
\phantomsection
\label{subsec:c2-piecewise-linear-liquidity-b}

Linearity gives $f(0.5)=H/2=1/9$ and $f(4)=H/2=1/9$.  Splitting at $1$ and $3$ produces three areas:
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 P(0.5\le X\le4)
 &=\frac12(0.5)\left(\frac19+\frac29\right)
   +2\left(\frac29\right)
   +\frac12(1)\left(\frac29+\frac19\right)\\
 &=\frac1{12}+\frac49+\frac16=\frac{25}{36}.
 \end{aligned}
\]

\subsubsection*{C2.3 - Find $P(X>4)$ by a complement and identify the pieces avoided.}
\phantomsection
\label{subsec:c2-piecewise-linear-liquidity-c}

Use $P(X>4)=1-P(X\le4)$.  From $0$ to $4$, the avoided pieces are the full first triangle, the full rectangle, and the trapezoid from $3$ to $4$:
\[
 P(X\le4)=\frac12(1)\frac29+2\left(\frac29\right)
 +\frac12(1)\left(\frac29+\frac19\right)
 =\frac19+\frac49+\frac16=\frac{13}{18}.
\]
Therefore $P(X>4)=1-13/18=5/18$.  This avoids separately adding the $4$--$5$ and $5$--$7$ regions.
