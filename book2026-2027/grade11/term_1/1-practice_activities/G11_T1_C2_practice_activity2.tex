For every problem, use the same seven-step routine: (1) identify the \textbf{support};
(2) name the density's shape; (3) use total area $=1$ to find any unknown
height or parameter; (4) mark the requested interval; (5) split its region
into familiar figures; (6) calculate and add or subtract their areas; and
(7) state the probability in context.  A density is a height, so it may exceed
$1$; it is the \emph{area} that must total $1$.  Endpoints do not affect a
continuous probability, for example $P(a<X<b)=P(a\le X\le b)$.

\subsection{Uniform distribution}\label{sec:c2-intro-uniform}
A uniform density has one constant height on support $[L,U]$:
\[
 f_X(x)=\begin{cases}h,&L\le x\le U,\\0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}
 \qquad (U-L)h=1,\quad h=\frac1{U-L}.
\]
Thus every probability is a rectangle: $P(a\le X\le b)=(b-a)h$ for
$L\le a<b\le U$.

\textbf{Worked example.} A delivery time $T$ is equally plausible from $2$ to
$8$ minutes.  The support is $[2,8]$ and the whole rectangle has width $6$ and
height $h$, so $6h=1$ and $h=1/6$.  For $3\le T\le6$, the relevant rectangle
has width $6-3=3$ and height $1/6$:
\[
 P(3\le T\le6)=A_{\rm rectangle}=3\left(\frac16\right)=\frac12.
\]
There is a $50\%$ chance that delivery takes from $3$ to $6$ minutes.

\subsection{Linear distribution}\label{sec:c2-intro-linear}
A single linear density has $f_X(x)=mx+d$ throughout one support $[L,U]$
(and $0$ outside), with nonnegative endpoint heights
$h_L=f(L)$ and $h_U=f(U)$.  Its whole area is the trapezoid
\[
 1=\frac12(h_L+h_U)(U-L).
\]
An interval $[a,b]$ is also a trapezoid of width $b-a$ and parallel heights
$f(a),f(b)$.  A triangle is only the special case in which one endpoint height
is zero.

\textbf{Worked example.} The density of a shop's daily price adjustment $X$
(in dollars) is $f(x)=kx+0.1$ on $[0,4]$.  The support width is $4$; its endpoint
heights are $0.1$ and $4k+0.1$.  Normalisation gives
\[
 1=\tfrac12[0.1+(4k+0.1)]4=8k+0.4,
 \qquad k=0.075.
\]
For $1\le X\le3$, $f(1)=0.175$ and $f(3)=0.325$.  The required figure is a
trapezoid of width $2$ with those parallel heights:
\[
 P(1\le X\le3)=\tfrac12(0.175+0.325)(2)=0.5.
\]
Both support endpoints have positive density, so this is genuinely linear,
not triangular.
\COneGraph{0}{4}{0.075*x+0.1}{1}{3}{0,1,3,4}{0.45}
\secInicio[sec:c2-introduction][sec:c2-intro-linear]

\subsection{Triangular distribution}\label{sec:c2-intro-triangular}
A triangular density has support $[L,U]$, height $0$ at both ends, and mode
$M$ ($L<M<U$) with height $H$.  It rises linearly on $[L,M]$ and falls linearly
on $[M,U]$.  Since the whole figure has base $U-L$,
\[
 1=\tfrac12(U-L)H,\qquad H=\frac2{U-L}.
\]
Heights inside either sloping side are found by proportional similar triangles.

\textbf{Worked example.} A project's cost overrun $C$ (thousand dollars) has
minimum $0$, most likely value $2$, and maximum $6$.  Its support is $[0,6]$,
mode is $2$, and $\tfrac12(6)H=1$, so $H=1/3$.  To find
$P(1\le C\le4)$, subtract two tail triangles.  At $C=1$ the height is $1/6$;
at $C=4$ it is also $1/6$.  The left tail has base $1$, height $1/6$; the
right tail has base $2$, height $1/6$:
\[
 P(1\le C\le4)=1-\tfrac12(1)(\tfrac16)-\tfrac12(2)(\tfrac16)
 =1-\tfrac1{12}-\tfrac16=\tfrac34.
\]
The region crosses the mode, but subtraction avoids splitting it into two
trapezoids.
\COneGraph{0}{6}{(x<=2)*(x/6)+(x>2)*((6-x)/12)}{1}{4}{0,1,2,4,6}{0.4}
\secInicio[sec:c2-introduction][sec:c2-intro-triangular]

\subsection{Piecewise uniform distribution}\label{sec:c2-intro-piecewise-uniform}
Here the density is constant \emph{within} each interval but may jump at a
breakpoint.  Normalisation is the sum of rectangles:
$1=\sum(\text{segment width})(\text{segment height})$.

\textbf{Worked example.} A warehouse waiting time $W$ has
\[
 f_W(w)=\begin{cases}k,&0\le w<2,\\2k,&2\le w\le5,\\0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}
\]
The support is $[0,5]$.  A width-$2$, height-$k$ rectangle plus a width-$3$,
height-$2k$ rectangle must have area $1$: $2k+6k=1$, so $k=1/8$.
The event $1\le W\le4$ spans the jump and uses two rectangles:
\[
 P(1\le W\le4)=\underbrace{(2-1)(1/8)}_{A_1}
 +\underbrace{(4-2)(1/4)}_{A_2}=\frac18+\frac12=\frac58.
\]
\CtwoProbGraph{0}{5}{(x<2)*(1/8)+(x>=2)*(1/4)}{1}{4}{0,1,2,4,5}{0.3}
\secInicio[sec:c2-introduction][sec:c2-intro-piecewise-uniform]

\subsection{Piecewise linear distribution}\label{sec:c2-intro-piecewise-linear}
A piecewise-linear density joins straight segments at stated breakpoints.
Read the height at each endpoint and breakpoint, then treat each piece as a
triangle, rectangle, or trapezoid.  Continuity means adjacent pieces meet at
the same height.

\textbf{Worked example.} Demand $D$ has support $[0,6]$.  Its density joins
$(0,0)$, $(2,H)$, $(4,H)$, and $(6,0)$.  The whole shape is a left triangle
(base $2$), rectangle (width $2$), and right triangle (base $2$):
\[
 1=\tfrac12(2)H+(2)H+\tfrac12(2)H=4H,
 \qquad H=\tfrac14.
\]
For $1\le D\le5$, subtract the two congruent tail triangles.  By linearity,
the heights at $1$ and $5$ are $H/2=1/8$:
\[
 P(1\le D\le5)=1-\tfrac12(1)(\tfrac18)-\tfrac12(1)(\tfrac18)=\frac78.
\]
This reusable approach identifies all three geometric pieces and uses
subtraction from total area $1$.
\COneGraph{0}{6}{(x<=2)*(x/8)+(x>2)*(x<=4)*(1/4)+(x>4)*((6-x)/8)}{1}{5}{0,1,2,4,5,6}{0.3}
\secInicio[sec:c2-introduction][sec:c2-intro-piecewise-linear]

\subsection{Microcredit disbursement window}\label{sec:c2-uniform-service}
A Bogotá fintech releases approved working-capital loans to neighbourhood
businesses after completing fraud and bank-account checks.  Let $T$ be the
number of hours from approval to disbursement.  For applications that require
no manual review, operational data support a uniform model from $2$ to $10$
hours.  A bakery owner needs the money within seven hours to pay a flour
supplier before dispatch closes; the fintech also monitors a four-hour service
band when planning its automated processing capacity.

\CtwoProbGraph[t]{2}{10}{1/8}{3}{7}{2,3,7,10}{0.16}

\textbf{Questions/Tasks.}
\begin{enumerate}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-uniform-service-a}{C2.1}{Identify the model, random variable, parameters, and support; then write its pdf.}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-uniform-service-b}{C2.2}{Calculate $P(3\le T\le7)$ from the relevant rectangular area.}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-uniform-service-c}{C2.3}{Use a complement to calculate $P(T>7)$.}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-uniform-service-d}{C2.4}{Interpret both probabilities for the fintech and the bakery owner.}
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection*{C2.1 - Identify the model, random variable, parameters, and support; then write its pdf.}
\phantomsection\label{subsec:c2-uniform-service-a}
The random variable $T$ measures disbursement time in hours.  The model is
uniform with lower parameter $L=2$, upper parameter $U=10$, and support
$[2,10]$.  If its constant height is $h$, the entire pdf is a rectangle of
width $10-2=8$.  Since total probability is $1$,
\[
 1=A_{\mathrm{rectangle}}=(10-2)h=8h
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad h=\frac18.
\]
Therefore
\[
 f_T(t)=\begin{cases}\dfrac18,&2\le t\le10,\\[2mm]0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}
\]
The equation and graph agree: the pdf is constant exactly on the stated
operational window, and its full rectangular area is $8(1/8)=1$.

\subsubsection*{C2.2 - Calculate $P(3\le T\le7)$ from the relevant rectangular area.}
\phantomsection\label{subsec:c2-uniform-service-b}
The shaded service band has width $7-3=4$ and height $1/8$.  Thus
\[
 P(3\le T\le7)=A_{\mathrm{rectangle}}
 =(7-3)\left(\frac18\right)=\frac48=\frac12.
\]
Hence half of comparable automatic disbursements take between three and seven
hours.

\subsubsection*{C2.3 - Use a complement to calculate $P(T>7)$.}
\phantomsection\label{subsec:c2-uniform-service-c}
The event $T\le7$ is the rectangle from $2$ to $7$:
\[
 P(T\le7)=(7-2)\left(\frac18\right)=\frac58.
\]
Because an exact time has probability zero in a continuous model,
\[
 P(T>7)=1-P(T\le7)=1-\frac58=\frac38.
\]
This is the same as the right-hand rectangle $(10-7)(1/8)=3/8$, which checks
the complement calculation.
\CtwoThresholdGraph{2}{10}{1/8}{7}{2,7,10}{0.16}{1/8}

\subsubsection*{C2.4 - Interpret both probabilities for the fintech and the bakery owner.}
\phantomsection\label{subsec:c2-uniform-service-d}
The value $P(T>7)=3/8=37.5\%$ means the bakery owner faces a substantial risk
of missing the supplier's dispatch if relying on this loan alone.  Meanwhile,
$P(3\le T\le7)=1/2$ tells the fintech that half of these cases occupy its
central four-hour processing band.  These figures can support a warning to the
borrower and the allocation of extra automated-processing capacity; they do
not guarantee the outcome of one application.

\subsection{Coffee auction premium}\label{sec:c2-linear-coffee}
A Bogotá specialty-coffee roaster buys small lots from Colombian producers.
On an auction morning, let $X$ be the premium above the reference price,
measured in thousands of pesos per kilogram.  The premium lies between $0$
and $8$, and buyer competition makes larger premiums progressively more
plausible.  The purchasing manager proposes the single linear density
$f_X(x)=k(x+2)$ on that interval.  Premiums above $6$ thousand pesos threaten
the margin on existing café contracts, while the $2$--$6$ band can be absorbed
without changing menu prices.

\COneGraph[x]{0}{8}{(x+2)/48}{2}{6}{0,2,6,8}{0.24}

\textbf{Questions/Tasks.}
\begin{enumerate}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-linear-coffee-a}{C2.1}{Identify the model and support, determine $k$ geometrically, and write the pdf.}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-linear-coffee-b}{C2.2}{Calculate $P(2\le X\le6)$ as a trapezoid.}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-linear-coffee-c}{C2.3}{Calculate $P(X>6)$ and verify it using a complement.}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-linear-coffee-d}{C2.4}{Interpret the probabilities for the purchasing decision.}
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection*{C2.1 - Identify the model and support, determine $k$ geometrically, and write the pdf.}
\phantomsection\label{subsec:c2-linear-coffee-a}
This is a linear distribution, not a triangular distribution: its support is
$[0,8]$ and both endpoint heights are positive,
\[
 f_X(0)=2k,\qquad f_X(8)=10k.
\]
The full region is a trapezoid of width $8$.  Normalisation therefore gives
\[
 1=\frac12(8)(2k+10k)=48k
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad k=\frac1{48}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 f_X(x)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{x+2}{48},&0\le x\le8,\\[2mm]0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}
\]
The increasing line represents the auction assumption that a higher premium
has a greater density, while the total trapezoidal area confirms a valid pdf.

\subsubsection*{C2.2 - Calculate $P(2\le X\le6)$ as a trapezoid.}
\phantomsection\label{subsec:c2-linear-coffee-b}
Read the two heights from the equation:
\[
 f_X(2)=\frac4{48}=\frac1{12},\qquad
 f_X(6)=\frac8{48}=\frac16.
\]
The shaded region is a width-$4$ trapezoid, not a rectangle, because its height
changes across the interval.  Hence
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 P(2\le X\le6)
 &=\frac12(6-2)\left(f_X(2)+f_X(6)\right)\\
 &=\frac12(4)\left(\frac1{12}+\frac16\right)
 =2\left(\frac3{12}\right)=\frac12.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Thus the premium falls in the absorbable band in $50\%$ of auctions described
by this model.

\subsubsection*{C2.3 - Calculate $P(X>6)$ and verify it using a complement.}
\phantomsection\label{subsec:c2-linear-coffee-c}
Directly, the region from $6$ to $8$ is a trapezoid with heights
$f_X(6)=1/6$ and $f_X(8)=10/48=5/24$:
\[
 P(X>6)=\frac12(8-6)\left(\frac16+\frac5{24}\right)
 =\frac4{24}+\frac5{24}=\frac38.
\]
For a check, the region from $0$ to $6$ has heights $1/24$ and $1/6$:
\[
 P(X\le6)=\frac12(6)\left(\frac1{24}+\frac16\right)=\frac58,
 \qquad 1-\frac58=\frac38.
\]
Both geometric routes agree.

\subsubsection*{C2.4 - Interpret the probabilities for the purchasing decision.}
\phantomsection\label{subsec:c2-linear-coffee-d}
Although half of auctions land in the manageable $2$--$6$ band, the
$37.5\%$ chance of a premium above $6$ is the more important margin-risk
measure.  The manager could cap bids, renegotiate café prices, or reserve a
smaller lot; the probability quantifies the frequency of the risky region,
not the size of a loss.

\subsection{Last-mile order volume}\label{sec:c2-triangular-lastmile}
A Bogotá last-mile cooperative must contract motorcycles before a national
online-sales event.  Let $Q$ be next-day order volume in thousands of orders.
Planners set an optimistic minimum of $2$, a most likely value of $5$, and a
capacity-limited maximum of $10$, so they use a triangular model.  The regular
fleet handles $4$--$8$ thousand orders; demand above $7$ thousand triggers an
expensive same-day contract with external couriers.

\COneGraph[q]{2}{10}{(x<=5)*((x-2)/12)+(x>5)*((10-x)/20)}{4}{8}{2,4,5,7,8,10}{0.30}

\textbf{Questions/Tasks.}
\begin{enumerate}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-triangular-lastmile-a}{C2.1}{Interpret the parameters, find the peak height, and write the piecewise pdf.}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-triangular-lastmile-b}{C2.2}{Find $P(4\le Q\le8)$ by subtracting two tail triangles.}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-triangular-lastmile-c}{C2.3}{Find $P(Q>7)$ from a geometric area.}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-triangular-lastmile-d}{C2.4}{Interpret both probabilities for fleet contracting.}
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection*{C2.1 - Interpret the parameters, find the peak height, and write the piecewise pdf.}
\phantomsection\label{subsec:c2-triangular-lastmile-a}
The support endpoints $L=2$ and $U=10$ are the modelled minimum and maximum;
the mode $M=5$ is the most likely order volume.  If the peak is $H$, the whole
triangle has base $10-2=8$, so
\[
 1=\frac12(8)H \quad\Longrightarrow\quad H=\frac14.
\]
The rising segment has slope $(1/4)/(5-2)=1/12$, and the falling segment has
slope $-(1/4)/(10-5)=-1/20$.  Therefore
\[
 f_Q(q)=\begin{cases}
 \dfrac{q-2}{12},&2\le q\le5,\\[2mm]
 \dfrac{10-q}{20},&5<q\le10,\\[2mm]
 0,&\text{otherwise.}
 \end{cases}
\]
Both formulas give $f_Q(5)=1/4$, so the equation connects continuously at the
mode and matches the displayed triangle.

\subsubsection*{C2.2 - Find $P(4\le Q\le8)$ by subtracting two tail triangles.}
\phantomsection\label{subsec:c2-triangular-lastmile-b}
The requested interval crosses the mode.  Rather than add two trapezoids,
subtract the two simpler tails.  From the pdf,
\[
 f_Q(4)=\frac{4-2}{12}=\frac16,\qquad
 f_Q(8)=\frac{10-8}{20}=\frac1{10}.
\]
The left tail from $2$ to $4$ and right tail from $8$ to $10$ are triangles:
\[
 A_L=\frac12(4-2)\frac16=\frac16,\qquad
 A_R=\frac12(10-8)\frac1{10}=\frac1{10}.
\]
Thus
\[
 P(4\le Q\le8)=1-A_L-A_R
 =1-\frac16-\frac1{10}=\frac{11}{15}.
\]

\subsubsection*{C2.3 - Find $P(Q>7)$ from a geometric area.}
\phantomsection\label{subsec:c2-triangular-lastmile-c}
The point $7$ lies on the descending side, where
\[
 f_Q(7)=\frac{10-7}{20}=\frac3{20}.
\]
The complete region to its right is a triangle of base $10-7=3$ and height
$3/20$.  Therefore
\[
 P(Q>7)=\frac12(3)\left(\frac3{20}\right)=\frac9{40}=22.5\%.
\]
This tail calculation uses the graph directly and needs no integration.

\subsubsection*{C2.4 - Interpret both probabilities for fleet contracting.}
\phantomsection\label{subsec:c2-triangular-lastmile-d}
The regular fleet range covers order volume with probability
$11/15\approx73.3\%$.  However, external couriers are needed with probability
$9/40=22.5\%$.  The cooperative can use the first figure to evaluate its base
capacity and the second to negotiate contingent motorcycle availability; the
remaining probability is not automatically a failure because volumes below
$4$ require fewer riders.

\subsection{Merchant reconciliation time}\label{sec:c2-piecewise-uniform-reconciliation}
A Colombian payments processor reconciles QR-code transactions from Bogotá
merchants before releasing daily balances.  Let $T$ be reconciliation time in
minutes.  Monitoring suggests three operational regimes: density $k$ from
$0$ to $3$ minutes, $2k$ from $3$ to $8$ minutes, and $k/2$ from $8$ to $12$
minutes.  The processor promises that most balances will clear from minute
$2$ through minute $9$; times above $8$ minutes require a slower exception
queue and increase support contacts.

\CtwoProbGraph[t]{0}{12}{(x<3)*(1/15)+(x>=3)*(x<8)*(2/15)+(x>=8)*(1/30)}{2}{9}{0,2,3,8,9,12}{0.16}

\textbf{Questions/Tasks.}
\begin{enumerate}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-piecewise-uniform-reconciliation-a}{C2.1}{Identify the support and regimes, determine $k$, and write the pdf.}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-piecewise-uniform-reconciliation-b}{C2.2}{Find $P(2\le T\le9)$ by combining three rectangles.}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-piecewise-uniform-reconciliation-c}{C2.3}{Find $P(T>8)$ and check it with a complement.}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-piecewise-uniform-reconciliation-d}{C2.4}{Interpret the results for the service promise and exception queue.}
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection*{C2.1 - Identify the support and regimes, determine $k$, and write the pdf.}
\phantomsection\label{subsec:c2-piecewise-uniform-reconciliation-a}
The support is $[0,12]$.  The model is piecewise uniform because the pdf is
constant within each regime but jumps at $3$ and $8$.  The whole graph is the
sum of three rectangles:
\[
 1=\underbrace{3k}_{0\text{--}3}
 +\underbrace{(8-3)(2k)}_{3\text{--}8}
 +\underbrace{(12-8)\frac{k}{2}}_{8\text{--}12}
 =3k+10k+2k=15k.
\]
Hence $k=1/15$, and
\[
 f_T(t)=\begin{cases}
 \dfrac1{15},&0\le t<3,\\[1mm]
 \dfrac2{15},&3\le t<8,\\[1mm]
 \dfrac1{30},&8\le t\le12,\\[1mm]
 0,&\text{otherwise.}
 \end{cases}
\]
The three rectangular areas sum to $1$, so this nonnegative piecewise function
is a valid pdf.

\subsubsection*{C2.2 - Find $P(2\le T\le9)$ by combining three rectangles.}
\phantomsection\label{subsec:c2-piecewise-uniform-reconciliation-b}
The interval crosses both breakpoints.  Split it into $[2,3)$, $[3,8)$, and
$[8,9]$ and use the height of each regime:
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 P(2\le T\le9)
 &=\underbrace{(3-2)\frac1{15}}_{A_1}
 +\underbrace{(8-3)\frac2{15}}_{A_2}
 +\underbrace{(9-8)\frac1{30}}_{A_3}\\
 &=\frac1{15}+\frac{10}{15}+\frac1{30}
 =\frac{2+20+1}{30}=\frac{23}{30}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Thus about $76.7\%$ of reconciliations fall inside the stated service window.

\subsubsection*{C2.3 - Find $P(T>8)$ and check it with a complement.}
\phantomsection\label{subsec:c2-piecewise-uniform-reconciliation-c}
Directly, the exception-queue region is a width-$4$ rectangle of height
$1/30$:
\[
 P(T>8)=(12-8)\frac1{30}=\frac2{15}.
\]
For the complement, the first two full rectangles have area
\[
 P(T\le8)=3\left(\frac1{15}\right)
 +5\left(\frac2{15}\right)=\frac15+\frac23=\frac{13}{15},
\]
so $1-13/15=2/15$, confirming the direct calculation.  Whether the endpoint
$8$ is included is immaterial for this continuous variable.

\subsubsection*{C2.4 - Interpret the results for the service promise and exception queue.}
\phantomsection\label{subsec:c2-piecewise-uniform-reconciliation-d}
The proposed $2$--$9$ minute promise contains about $76.7\%$ of cases, so it
would be misleading if presented as a near-certain outcome.  The exception
queue receives $2/15\approx13.3\%$ of reconciliations.  These probabilities
help the processor set an honest merchant message and staff the slower queue,
while the model's regime jumps reflect process categories rather than sudden
probability at an exact time.

\subsection{Weekly payout liquidity}\label{sec:c2-piecewise-linear-liquidity}
A Bogotá marketplace must hold cash for Friday seller payouts, refunds, and
chargebacks.  Let $D$ be required liquidity in billions of pesos.  Its finance
team models a continuous piecewise-linear pdf joining
$(0,0)$, $(2,H)$, $(5,H)$, $(7,H/2)$, and $(10,0)$.  The shape represents a
ramp-up to common payout requirements, a plateau, and then progressively less
likely high-liquidity weeks.  The treasury plan covers $1$ through $8$ billion
pesos; requirements above $6$ billion trigger a more expensive backup credit
line.

\subsubsection*{C2.1 - Determine $H$ from the component areas and write the piecewise pdf.}
\phantomsection\label{subsec:c2-piecewise-linear-liquidity-a}
The support $[0,10]$ contains four familiar regions: a base-$2$ triangle, a
width-$3$ rectangle, a width-$2$ trapezoid with heights $H$ and $H/2$, and a
base-$3$ triangle of height $H/2$.  Total area $1$ gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 1&=\frac12(2)H+3H+\frac12(2)\left(H+\frac H2\right)
    +\frac12(3)\frac H2\\
  &=H+3H+\frac32H+\frac34H=\frac{25}{4}H,
 \end{aligned}
\]
so $H=4/25$.  Equations of the joined segments then give
\[
 f_D(d)=\begin{cases}
 \dfrac{2d}{25},&0\le d\le2,\\[1mm]
 \dfrac4{25},&2<d\le5,\\[1mm]
 \dfrac{9-d}{25},&5<d\le7,\\[1mm]
 \dfrac{2(10-d)}{75},&7<d\le10,\\[1mm]
 0,&\text{otherwise.}
 \end{cases}
\]
At $d=2,5,7$ the adjacent formulas agree, so the equation is continuous and
matches the graph.

\subsubsection*{C2.2 - Find $P(1\le D\le8)$ by subtracting the two tail triangles.}
\phantomsection\label{subsec:c2-piecewise-linear-liquidity-b}
At $d=1$, $f_D(1)=2/25$.  The left tail is a triangle with base $1$:
\[
 A_L=\frac12(1)\frac2{25}=\frac1{25}.
\]
On the last segment, $f_D(8)=2(10-8)/75=4/75$.  The right tail from $8$ to
$10$ is another triangle:
\[
 A_R=\frac12(10-8)\frac4{75}=\frac4{75}.
\]
Therefore
\[
 P(1\le D\le8)=1-A_L-A_R
 =1-\frac1{25}-\frac4{75}=\frac{68}{75}\approx90.7\%.
\]
The complement method connects the two tail triangles in the graph to the
central treasury range without adding four internal pieces.

\subsubsection*{C2.3 - Find $P(D>6)$ by combining regions on both sides of a breakpoint.}
\phantomsection\label{subsec:c2-piecewise-linear-liquidity-c}
The event begins inside the segment from $5$ to $7$ and continues through the
segment from $7$ to $10$.  First find the height at $6$:
\[
 f_D(6)=\frac{9-6}{25}=\frac3{25},\qquad f_D(7)=\frac2{25}.
\]
From $6$ to $7$ the area is a trapezoid; from $7$ to $10$ it is a triangle:
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 P(D>6)
 &=\underbrace{\frac12(7-6)\left(\frac3{25}+\frac2{25}\right)}_{A_1}
 +\underbrace{\frac12(10-7)\frac2{25}}_{A_2}\\
 &=\frac1{10}+\frac3{25}=\frac{5+6}{50}=\frac{11}{50}=22\%.
 \end{aligned}
\]
This calculation must include both regions; stopping at the breakpoint $7$
would omit high-liquidity outcomes.

\subsubsection*{C2.4 - Interpret the probabilities for treasury planning.}
\phantomsection\label{subsec:c2-piecewise-linear-liquidity-d}
The standard $1$--$8$ billion peso plan covers approximately $90.7\%$ of
weeks.  Nevertheless, the backup credit line is triggered in $22\%$ of weeks
because its threshold begins at $6$, inside that standard range.  The two
events overlap, so their probabilities should not be added.  Treasury can use
the $22\%$ figure to price standby funding and the $90.7\%$ figure to evaluate
the overall coverage of its cash plan.
