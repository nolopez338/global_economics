The central skill in this activity is constructing a continuous probability
model from a situation, rather than merely evaluating a supplied formula.  For
every problem, use the same eight-step routine: (1) read the narrative and
define the random variable; (2) determine the \textbf{support} and geometric
shape; (3) translate verbal relationships into heights, ratios, slopes, or
breakpoints; (4) use total area $=1$ to determine all unknown quantities;
(5) write the complete pdf, including $0$ outside its support; (6) mark the
region requested by the probability; (7) calculate its area with rectangles,
triangles, trapezoids, or a complement; and (8) state the result in context
when useful.  A density is a height, so it may exceed
$1$; it is the \emph{area} that must total $1$.  Endpoints do not affect a
continuous probability, for example $P(a<X<b)=P(a\le X\le b)$.

\subsection{Uniform distribution}\label{sec:c2-intro-uniform}
A uniform density has one constant height on support $[L,U]$:
\[
 f_X(x)=\begin{cases}h,&L\le x\le U,\\0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}
 \qquad (U-L)h=1,\quad h=\frac1{U-L}.
\]
Thus every probability is a rectangle: $P(a\le X\le b)=(b-a)h$ for
$L\le a<b\le U$.

\textbf{Worked example.} A delivery time $T$ is equally plausible from $2$ to
$8$ minutes.  The support is $[2,8]$ and the whole rectangle has width $6$ and
height $h$, so $6h=1$ and $h=1/6$.  For $3\le T\le6$, the relevant rectangle
has width $6-3=3$ and height $1/6$:
\[
 P(3\le T\le6)=A_{\rm rectangle}=3\left(\frac16\right)=\frac12.
\]
There is a $50\%$ chance that delivery takes from $3$ to $6$ minutes.

\subsection{Linear distribution}\label{sec:c2-intro-linear}
A single linear density has $f_X(x)=mx+d$ throughout one support $[L,U]$
(and $0$ outside), with nonnegative endpoint heights
$h_L=f(L)$ and $h_U=f(U)$.  Its whole area is the trapezoid
\[
 1=\frac12(h_L+h_U)(U-L).
\]
An interval $[a,b]$ is also a trapezoid of width $b-a$ and parallel heights
$f(a),f(b)$.  A triangle is only the special case in which one endpoint height
is zero.

\textbf{Worked example.} A shop's daily price adjustment $X$ (in dollars) lies
between $0$ and $4$.  Its likelihood density increases linearly, and the
density at $4$ is four times that at $0$.  Translating the description, let
the endpoint heights be $h$ and $4h$.  The whole trapezoid gives
\[
 1=\tfrac12(h+4h)4=10h,\qquad h=0.1.
\]
The line through $(0,0.1)$ and $(4,0.4)$ is $f_X(x)=0.075x+0.1$ on the
support (and $0$ otherwise).  For $1\le X\le3$, $f(1)=0.175$ and
$f(3)=0.325$.  The required figure is a
trapezoid of width $2$ with those parallel heights:
\[
 P(1\le X\le3)=\tfrac12(0.175+0.325)(2)=0.5.
\]
Both support endpoints have positive density, so this is genuinely linear,
not triangular.

\subsection{Triangular distribution}\label{sec:c2-intro-triangular}
A triangular density has support $[L,U]$, height $0$ at both ends, and mode
$M$ ($L<M<U$) with height $H$.  It rises linearly on $[L,M]$ and falls linearly
on $[M,U]$.  Since the whole figure has base $U-L$,
\[
 1=\tfrac12(U-L)H,\qquad H=\frac2{U-L}.
\]
Heights inside either sloping side are found by proportional similar triangles.

\textbf{Worked example.} A project's cost overrun $C$ (thousand dollars) has
minimum $0$, most likely value $2$, and maximum $6$.  Its support is $[0,6]$,
mode is $2$, and $\tfrac12(6)H=1$, so $H=1/3$.  To find
$P(1\le C\le4)$, subtract two tail triangles.  At $C=1$ the height is $1/6$;
at $C=4$ it is also $1/6$.  The left tail has base $1$, height $1/6$; the
right tail has base $2$, height $1/6$:
\[
 P(1\le C\le4)=1-\tfrac12(1)(\tfrac16)-\tfrac12(2)(\tfrac16)
 =1-\tfrac1{12}-\tfrac16=\tfrac34.
\]
The region crosses the mode, but subtraction avoids splitting it into two
trapezoids.
\COneGraph{0}{6}{(x<=2)*(x/6)+(x>2)*((6-x)/12)}{1}{4}{0,1,2,4,6}{0.4}
\secInicio[sec:c2-introduction][sec:c2-intro-triangular]

\subsection{Piecewise uniform distribution}\label{sec:c2-intro-piecewise-uniform}
Here the density is constant \emph{within} each interval but may jump at a
breakpoint.  Normalisation is the sum of rectangles:
$1=\sum(\text{segment width})(\text{segment height})$.

\textbf{Worked example.} A warehouse waiting time $W$ ranges from $0$ to $5$
minutes.  Waiting times are equally dense within each of two regimes, $0$--$2$
and $2$--$5$, but the later regime has twice the density of the earlier one.
If the first height is $k$, the narrative makes the second $2k$.  A width-$2$,
height-$k$ rectangle plus a width-$3$, height-$2k$ rectangle must have area
$1$: $2k+6k=1$, so $k=1/8$.  Thus the pdf has heights $1/8$ and $1/4$ on
those respective regimes, and is $0$ otherwise.
The event $1\le W\le4$ spans the jump and uses two rectangles:
\[
 P(1\le W\le4)=\underbrace{(2-1)(1/8)}_{A_1}
 +\underbrace{(4-2)(1/4)}_{A_2}=\frac18+\frac12=\frac58.
\]
\CtwoProbGraph{0}{5}{(x<2)*(1/8)+(x>=2)*(1/4)}{1}{4}{0,1,2,4,5}{0.3}
\secInicio[sec:c2-introduction][sec:c2-intro-piecewise-uniform]

\subsection{Piecewise linear distribution}\label{sec:c2-intro-piecewise-linear}
A piecewise-linear density joins straight segments at stated breakpoints.
Read the height at each endpoint and breakpoint, then treat each piece as a
triangle, rectangle, or trapezoid.  Continuity means adjacent pieces meet at
the same height.

\textbf{Worked example.} Demand $D$ ranges from $0$ to $6$.  Its density starts
at zero, rises linearly to its maximum at $2$, stays at that maximum until
$4$, and then falls linearly to zero at $6$.  Calling the maximum $H$
translates the story into a left triangle
(base $2$), rectangle (width $2$), and right triangle (base $2$):
\[
 1=\tfrac12(2)H+(2)H+\tfrac12(2)H=4H,
 \qquad H=\tfrac14.
\]
For $1\le D\le5$, subtract the two congruent tail triangles.  By linearity,
the heights at $1$ and $5$ are $H/2=1/8$:
\[
 P(1\le D\le5)=1-\tfrac12(1)(\tfrac18)-\tfrac12(1)(\tfrac18)=\frac78.
\]
This reusable approach identifies all three geometric pieces and uses
subtraction from total area $1$.
\COneGraph{0}{6}{(x<=2)*(x/8)+(x>2)*(x<=4)*(1/4)+(x>4)*((6-x)/8)}{1}{5}{0,1,2,4,5,6}{0.3}

\subsection{Microcredit disbursement window}\label{sec:c2-uniform-service}
A Bogotá fintech releases approved working-capital loans after fraud and
bank-account checks.  Let $T$ be the number of hours from approval to
disbursement; differing bank response times make it continuous.  Automatic
cases never finish before $2$ hours or after $10$ hours, and operational data
show that every time in that window is equally plausible.  A bakery owner
needs the money within $7$ hours to meet a supplier's dispatch deadline.  The
fintech separately studies the central $3$--$7$ hour band when assigning
automated-processing capacity.

\CtwoProbGraph[t]{2}{10}{1/8}{3}{7}{2,3,7,10}{0.16}
\endgroup

\textbf{Questions/Tasks.}
\begin{enumerate}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-uniform-service-a}{2.1}{Using the situation, construct the complete pdf of $T$: identify its support, determine the unknown constant height from total area $1$, and include $0$ outside the support.}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-uniform-service-b}{2.2}{Calculate the deadline risk $P(T>7)$ geometrically.}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-uniform-service-c}{2.3}{Calculate $P(3\le T\le7)$ from the appropriate geometric area.}
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection*{C2.1 - Construct the pdf from the situation.}
\phantomsection\label{subsec:c2-uniform-service-a}
The narrative defines $T$ as disbursement time, bounds it by $2$ and $10$, and
says all times in that window are equally plausible.  Thus the support is
$[2,10]$ and its density is a rectangle of unknown height $h$.  Normalisation
gives
\[
 1=A_{\mathrm{rectangle}}=(10-2)h=8h,\qquad h=\frac18.
\]
Therefore the complete model is
\[
 f_T(t)=\begin{cases}\dfrac18,&2\le t\le10,\\[2mm]0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}
\]
Its area is $8(1/8)=1$, as required.

\subsubsection*{C2.2 - Calculate the tail probability $P(T>7)$.}
\phantomsection\label{subsec:c2-uniform-service-b}
The right tail is a rectangle of width $10-7=3$ and height $1/8$:
\[
 P(T>7)=A_{\mathrm{rectangle}}=(10-7)\frac18=\frac38=37.5\%.
\]
Thus the bakery has a $37.5\%$ modeled risk of missing its deadline.
\CtwoThresholdGraph{2}{10}{1/8}{7}{2,7,10}{0.16}{1/8}

\subsubsection*{C2.3 - Calculate the interval probability $P(3\le T\le7)$.}
\phantomsection\label{subsec:c2-uniform-service-c}
The central band is a rectangle of width $7-3=4$:
\[
 P(3\le T\le7)=(7-3)\frac18=\frac12.
\]
Hence half of comparable disbursements occupy this capacity-planning band.

\subsection{Coffee auction premium}\label{sec:c2-linear-coffee}
A Bogotá specialty-coffee roaster buys small lots from Colombian producers.
Let $X$ be the continuously measured premium, in thousands of pesos per
kilogram, above the reference price.  Auction rules keep it between $0$ and
$8$.  Buyer competition makes its likelihood density increase at a constant
rate across that range, and market records indicate that the density at the
upper limit is five times the density at the lower limit.  Premiums above $6$
thousand pesos threaten margins, while the $2$--$6$ band can be absorbed
without changing menu prices.

\begingroup\pgfplotsset{every axis/.append style={yticklabels=\empty}}
\COneGraph[x]{0}{8}{(x+2)/48}{2}{6}{0,2,6,8}{0.24}
\endgroup

\textbf{Questions/Tasks.}
\begin{enumerate}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-linear-coffee-a}{2.1}{Construct the complete pdf of $X$ from the narrative: determine both endpoint heights, derive the line, and include $0$ outside the support.}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-linear-coffee-b}{2.2}{Calculate the margin-risk tail $P(X>6)$ geometrically.}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-linear-coffee-c}{2.3}{Calculate $P(2\le X\le6)$ using the appropriate geometric area.}
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection*{C2.1 - Construct the pdf from the situation.}
\phantomsection\label{subsec:c2-linear-coffee-a}
The auction bounds give support $[0,8]$.  ``Increases at a constant rate''
gives one straight line, and the endpoint relationship gives heights $h$ and
$5h$, both positive.  The full trapezoid must have area $1$:
\[
 1=\frac12(8)(h+5h)=24h,\qquad h=\frac1{24},\quad5h=\frac5{24}.
\]
The line through $(0,1/24)$ and $(8,5/24)$ has slope
$(5/24-1/24)/8=1/48$ and intercept $1/24=2/48$.  Hence
\[
 f_X(x)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{x+2}{48},&0\le x\le8,\\[2mm]0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}
\]
Both endpoint heights are positive, so the model is linear rather than
triangular, and its trapezoidal area is $1$.

\subsubsection*{C2.2 - Calculate the tail probability $P(X>6)$.}
\phantomsection\label{subsec:c2-linear-coffee-b}
The heights are $f_X(6)=1/6$ and $f_X(8)=5/24$.  The right tail is a
width-$2$ trapezoid:
\[
 P(X>6)=\frac12(8-6)\left(\frac16+\frac5{24}\right)=\frac38.
\]
Thus $37.5\%$ of modeled auctions exceed the manager's margin threshold.

\subsubsection*{C2.3 - Calculate the interval probability $P(2\le X\le6)$.}
\phantomsection\label{subsec:c2-linear-coffee-c}
Since $f_X(2)=1/12$ and $f_X(6)=1/6$, the interval is a nonconstant
trapezoid of width $4$:
\[
 P(2\le X\le6)=\frac12(6-2)\left(\frac1{12}+\frac16\right)=\frac12.
\]
Thus the absorbable premium band occurs in half of the modeled auctions.

\subsection{Last-mile order volume}\label{sec:c2-triangular-lastmile}
A Bogotá last-mile cooperative contracts motorcycles before a national online
sales event.  Let $Q$ be continuously modeled next-day order volume in
thousands of orders.  Forecasts rule out fewer than $2$ or more than $10$
thousand orders; among feasible volumes, $5$ thousand is most likely.  The
likelihood rises steadily from zero at the minimum to that single peak and
then falls steadily to zero at the maximum.  Demand above $7$ thousand
triggers an expensive external-courier contract, while $4$--$8$ thousand is
the range used to evaluate the regular fleet.

\begingroup\pgfplotsset{every axis/.append style={yticklabels=\empty}}
\COneGraph[q]{2}{10}{(x<=5)*((x-2)/12)+(x>5)*((10-x)/20)}{4}{8}{2,4,5,7,8,10}{0.30}
\endgroup

\textbf{Questions/Tasks.}
\begin{enumerate}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-triangular-lastmile-a}{2.1}{Construct the complete pdf of $Q$ from the forecast: determine the peak height and both linear equations, and include $0$ outside the support.}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-triangular-lastmile-b}{2.2}{Calculate the external-contract tail $P(Q>7)$ geometrically.}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-triangular-lastmile-c}{2.3}{Calculate $P(4\le Q\le8)$, an interval crossing the most likely value.}
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection*{C2.1 - Construct the pdf from the situation.}
\phantomsection\label{subsec:c2-triangular-lastmile-a}
The minimum, most likely value, and maximum translate to $L=2$, $M=5$, and
$U=10$.  Rising from and falling to zero makes one triangle.  If its peak is
$H$, normalisation gives
\[
 1=\frac12(10-2)H,\qquad H=\frac14.
\]
The rising slope is $(1/4)/(5-2)=1/12$; the falling slope is
$-(1/4)/(10-5)=-1/20$.  Using the zero-height endpoints,
\[
 f_Q(q)=\begin{cases}
 \dfrac{q-2}{12},&2\le q\le5,\\[2mm]
 \dfrac{10-q}{20},&5<q\le10,\\[2mm]
 0,&\text{otherwise.}
 \end{cases}
\]
The pieces meet at height $1/4$ and the total triangular area is $1$.

\subsubsection*{C2.2 - Calculate the tail probability $P(Q>7)$.}
\phantomsection\label{subsec:c2-triangular-lastmile-b}
On the descending side, $f_Q(7)=3/20$.  The right tail is a triangle of base
$10-7=3$ and height $3/20$:
\[
 P(Q>7)=\frac12(3)\frac3{20}=\frac9{40}=22.5\%.
\]
This is the modeled chance that external couriers are required.

\subsubsection*{C2.3 - Calculate the interval probability $P(4\le Q\le8)$.}
\phantomsection\label{subsec:c2-triangular-lastmile-c}
The interval crosses the mode.  A clean geometric decomposition subtracts its
two outside triangles.  Since $f_Q(4)=1/6$ and $f_Q(8)=1/10$,
\[
 P(4\le Q\le8)
 =1-\underbrace{\frac12(4-2)\frac16}_{\text{left triangle}}
   -\underbrace{\frac12(10-8)\frac1{10}}_{\text{right triangle}}
 =\frac{11}{15}.
\]
Thus the regular-fleet range covers about $73.3\%$ of modeled days.

\subsection{Merchant reconciliation time}\label{sec:c2-piecewise-uniform-reconciliation}
A Colombian payments processor reconciles QR-code transactions before
releasing merchant balances.  Let $T$ be the continuously measured time in
minutes.  No automatic reconciliation takes less than $0$ or more than $12$
minutes.  The workflow has three regimes: initial validation from $0$ to $3$,
ledger matching from $3$ to $8$, and exception closing from $8$ to $12$.
Times are equally dense within each regime.  Ledger-matching observations
occur at twice the density of initial-validation observations, while
exception-closing observations occur at half the density of initial-validation
observations.  Times above $8$ require the slower exception queue; the
processor also evaluates a $2$--$9$ minute service window that crosses all
three regimes.

\begingroup\pgfplotsset{every axis/.append style={yticklabels=\empty}}
\CtwoProbGraph[t]{0}{12}{(x<3)*(1/15)+(x>=3)*(x<8)*(2/15)+(x>=8)*(1/30)}{2}{9}{0,2,3,8,9,12}{0.16}
\endgroup

\textbf{Questions/Tasks.}
\begin{enumerate}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-piecewise-uniform-reconciliation-a}{2.1}{Construct the complete pdf of $T$ from the three regimes: translate their relative densities, normalize the heights, and include $0$ outside the support.}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-piecewise-uniform-reconciliation-b}{2.2}{Calculate the exception-queue tail $P(T>8)$ geometrically.}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-piecewise-uniform-reconciliation-c}{2.3}{Calculate $P(2\le T\le9)$ by decomposing the interval at the breakpoints.}
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection*{C2.1 - Construct the pdf from the situation.}
\phantomsection\label{subsec:c2-piecewise-uniform-reconciliation-a}
The stated bounds give support $[0,12]$, with breakpoints $3$ and $8$.  Let
the initial-validation height be $k$.  The verbal ratios translate the next
two heights to $2k$ and $k/2$.  The three rectangles must total $1$:
\[
 1=3k+(8-3)(2k)+(12-8)\frac{k}{2}=15k,\qquad k=\frac1{15}.
\]
Therefore
\[
 f_T(t)=\begin{cases}
 \dfrac1{15},&0\le t<3,\\[1mm]
 \dfrac2{15},&3\le t<8,\\[1mm]
 \dfrac1{30},&8\le t\le12,\\[1mm]
 0,&\text{otherwise.}
 \end{cases}
\]
All heights are nonnegative and the component areas sum to $1$.

\subsubsection*{C2.2 - Calculate the tail probability $P(T>8)$.}
\phantomsection\label{subsec:c2-piecewise-uniform-reconciliation-b}
The exception tail is a width-$4$, height-$1/30$ rectangle:
\[
 P(T>8)=(12-8)\frac1{30}=\frac2{15}\approx13.3\%.
\]
The endpoint does not affect a continuous probability.

\subsubsection*{C2.3 - Calculate the interval probability $P(2\le T\le9)$.}
\phantomsection\label{subsec:c2-piecewise-uniform-reconciliation-c}
Split at both regime boundaries and add three rectangles:
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 P(2\le T\le9)
 &=\underbrace{(3-2)\frac1{15}}_{[2,3)}
 +\underbrace{(8-3)\frac2{15}}_{[3,8)}
 +\underbrace{(9-8)\frac1{30}}_{[8,9]}\\
 &=\frac{23}{30}\approx76.7\%.
 \end{aligned}
\]
This is the modeled proportion inside the processor's service window.

\subsection{Weekly payout liquidity}\label{sec:c2-piecewise-linear-liquidity}
A Bogotá marketplace holds cash for Friday seller payouts, refunds, and
chargebacks.  Let $D$ be continuously measured required liquidity in billions
of pesos.  Requirements range from $0$ to $10$.  Their density begins at zero,
rises at a constant rate to its maximum when requirements reach $2$, and stays
at that maximum through $5$.  It then falls at a constant rate until, at $7$,
it is half the maximum; from there it falls at a constant rate to zero at
$10$.  Requirements above $6$ trigger an expensive backup credit line.  The
$1$--$8$ billion range is used to assess how often the standard treasury plan
covers the week.

\begingroup\pgfplotsset{every axis/.append style={yticklabels=\empty}}
\COneGraph[d]{0}{10}{(x<=2)*(2*x/25)+(x>2)*(x<=5)*(4/25)+(x>5)*(x<=7)*((9-x)/25)+(x>7)*(2*(10-x)/75)}{1}{8}{0,1,2,5,6,7,8,10}{0.20}
\endgroup

\textbf{Questions/Tasks.}
\begin{enumerate}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-piecewise-linear-liquidity-a}{2.1}{Construct the complete pdf of $D$ from the narrative: determine the peak, derive every segment equation, and include $0$ outside the support.}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-piecewise-linear-liquidity-b}{2.2}{Calculate the backup-credit tail $P(D>6)$ by decomposing it at the structural change.}
 \CriterionTask{subsec:c2-piecewise-linear-liquidity-c}{2.3}{Calculate $P(1\le D\le8)$, an interval spanning all structural pieces.}
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection*{C2.1 - Construct the pdf from the situation.}
\phantomsection\label{subsec:c2-piecewise-linear-liquidity-a}
The story gives support $[0,10]$ and breakpoints $2,5,7$.  If the maximum is
$H$, the corresponding heights are $0,H,H,H/2,0$.  Thus the geometry is a
base-$2$ triangle, width-$3$ rectangle, width-$2$ trapezoid, and base-$3$
triangle.  Normalisation gives
\[
 1=\frac12(2)H+3H+\frac12(2)\left(H+\frac H2\right)
   +\frac12(3)\frac H2=\frac{25}{4}H,\qquad H=\frac4{25}.
\]
Deriving each line from its two endpoint values gives slopes $2/25$, $0$,
$-1/25$, and $-2/75$, respectively.  Therefore
\[
 f_D(d)=\begin{cases}
 \dfrac{2d}{25},&0\le d\le2,\\[1mm]
 \dfrac4{25},&2<d\le5,\\[1mm]
 \dfrac{9-d}{25},&5<d\le7,\\[1mm]
 \dfrac{2(10-d)}{75},&7<d\le10,\\[1mm]
 0,&\text{otherwise.}
 \end{cases}
\]
Adjacent equations agree at every breakpoint, and the geometric areas total
$1$.

\subsubsection*{C2.2 - Calculate the tail probability $P(D>6)$.}
\phantomsection\label{subsec:c2-piecewise-linear-liquidity-b}
The tail begins within the declining $5$--$7$ segment.  Its heights at $6$ and
$7$ are $3/25$ and $2/25$.  From $6$ to $7$ is a trapezoid; from $7$ to $10$
is a triangle:
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 P(D>6)
 &=\underbrace{\frac12(7-6)\left(\frac3{25}+\frac2{25}\right)}_{\text{trapezoid}}
 +\underbrace{\frac12(10-7)\frac2{25}}_{\text{triangle}}\\
 &=\frac1{10}+\frac3{25}=\frac{11}{50}=22\%.
 \end{aligned}
\]
This is the modeled frequency with which backup credit is triggered.

\subsubsection*{C2.3 - Calculate the interval probability $P(1\le D\le8)$.}
\phantomsection\label{subsec:c2-piecewise-linear-liquidity-c}
Although the interval spans every structural piece, its complement consists
of two simple tail triangles.  Since $f_D(1)=2/25$ and $f_D(8)=4/75$,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 P(1\le D\le8)
 &=1-\underbrace{\frac12(1)\frac2{25}}_{[0,1]}
     -\underbrace{\frac12(10-8)\frac4{75}}_{[8,10]}\\
 &=1-\frac1{25}-\frac4{75}=\frac{68}{75}\approx90.7\%.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Thus the standard treasury range covers about $90.7\%$ of modeled weeks.
