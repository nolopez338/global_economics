		\SubsectionBox{1. Identifying Probability Distributions from Contexts \score{C1-\sml{5}}}
		A variety of Colombian organizations use probability models to represent
		uncertain quantities and events. In each situation below, a random
		variable is defined from the information provided. The appropriate
		probability distribution must be identified from the characteristics
		of the situation.
		
		\begin{itemize}
			\item[\textbf{(C1)}] For each context, characterize the random
			variable and identify the appropriate probability distribution.
			\score{5}
		\end{itemize}
		
		\textbf{Context 1.} A payment platform monitors one scheduled
		electronic transfer. By the end of processing, the transfer is either
		completed successfully or not completed. The platform uses this information
		to estimate verification and support needs.
		
		\textbf{Context 2.} An agricultural technology company monitors the
		daily temperature variation inside a refrigerated storage unit. The
		variation can take any value from \(-2^\circ\mathrm{C}\) to
		\(2^\circ\mathrm{C}\), with all values in this interval considered
		equally likely. The company uses this model to set an operating margin.
		
		\textbf{Context 3.} A streaming platform sends the same offer to
		exactly 80 randomly selected eligible users. Each user either subscribes
		or does not. The platform records the number of users who subscribe to
		estimate the server capacity needed for the promotion.
		
		\textbf{Context 4.} A Bogotá parking company records the number of
		vehicle breakdowns during a fixed one-week period. Comparable records
		show that breakdowns occur independently and without a predictable
		timetable. The company uses these records to help schedule maintenance
		staff.
		
		\textbf{Context 5.} An online marketplace studies the delivery time
		for a particular type of order. Delivery times range from 20 to
		80 minutes, with deliveries around 45 minutes occurring most frequently.
		They become progressively less common toward either extreme. The
		company uses this model to estimate delivery times.
		
		\textbf{Context 6.} A quality-control laboratory tests electronic
		components until the first defective component is found. Components
		are tested independently, and testing stops after the first defect.
		The laboratory records the total number of components tested, including
		the defective one, to estimate inspection costs.
		
		\textbf{Context 7.} A cloud-computing provider studies storage usage
		among small business customers. Usage ranges from 0 to 50 gigabytes
		and becomes steadily more common toward the upper limit, without
		reaching a peak before 50 GB. The provider uses this model to plan
		capacity.
		
		\textbf{Context 8.} A retailer sends a promotional message to exactly
		120 customers selected using the same procedure. Each customer either
		makes a purchase or does not. The retailer records the number of
		purchases made in response to the message to estimate the inventory
		needed for the campaign.
		
		\textbf{Context 9.} A coffee exporter measures the moisture content
		of coffee bags under usual storage conditions. Measurements cluster
		around 11\%, with approximately symmetric behaviour around
		this value. Values become less common farther from 11\%. The exporter
		uses this model to establish quality-control thresholds.
		
		\textbf{Context 10.} A food-delivery company records the number of
		customer cancellations during a fixed three-hour dinner period in one
		delivery zone. Comparable evenings show cancellations occurring
		independently without a recurring timetable. The company uses these
		records to estimate customer-service needs.

		\SubsectionBox{2. Delivery Time for a Local Grocery Order \score{C2-\sml{3}}}
		A Colombian grocery-delivery company studies the additional time
		required to deliver orders during a busy evening period. For a particular
		delivery zone, the added delivery time can range from 0 to
		10 minutes. A delay of any length within this interval is possible, and
		shorter delays become steadily more common. Historical data indicate
		that no additional delay is 4 times as common as a 10-minute delay.
		
		\begin{itemize}
			\item[\textbf{(C2)}] Determine the probability
			that the added delivery time is between 3 and 7 minutes.
			\score{3}
		\end{itemize}
		
		\SubsectionBox{3. Daily Commute Time for a Bogotá Apprentice \score{C2-\sml{4}}}
		An apprentice records the time spent travelling to a training programme
		each day. Commute times range from 1 to 9 hours. Times become steadily
		more common from 1 hour up to a most common time of 4 hours, then
		steadily less common through 9 hours; there are no commute times right
		at either limit.
		
		\begin{itemize}
			\item[\textbf{(C2)}] Determine the probability that
			the commute takes between 3 and 7 hours.
			\score{4}
		\end{itemize}
		
		\SubsectionBox{4. Timing of an Unexpected Market Movement \score{C3-\sml{2}}}
		A financial monitoring system records the time of day at which an
		unexpected movement in an international currency market occurs. The
		movement is equally likely to occur at any time during a 24-hour period,
		so no part of the day is more likely than another.
		
		\begin{itemize}
			\item[\textbf{(C3)}] Determine the probability that the movement occurs between 9 p.m.	and 5 a.m.
			\score{2}
		\end{itemize}

		\SubsectionBox{5. Direction of an Unexpected Sensor Reading \score{C3-\sml{3}}}
		A directional sensor records the direction in which it is pointing.
		The sensor can point in any direction around a complete circle, and
		every direction is equally likely. 
		
		\begin{itemize}
			\item[\textbf{(C3)}] Determine which is
			more probable and by how much: the sensor pointing within \(\pi/12\) radians of the reference marker, or the sensor pointing within \(10^\circ\) of the reference marker.
			\score{3}
		\end{itemize}
		
		
		\SubsectionBox{6. Waiting for a Study-Room Opening \score{C4-\sml{0.5}}}
		At a university library in Bogotá, study rooms become available at an
		average rate of one room every 5 minutes.
		
		\begin{itemize}
			\item[\textbf{(C4)}] Determine the probability that the next study
			room becomes available more than 7 minutes after the start of the
			observation.
			\score{0.5}
		\end{itemize}
		
		\SubsectionBox{7. Waiting for a Market Price Alert \score{C4-\sml{0.5}}}
		A financial monitoring system detects significant price changes in a
		commodity market at an average rate of 3 alerts every 20 minutes.
		
		\begin{itemize}
			\item[\textbf{(C4)}] Determine the probability that the next
			significant price change is detected within 6 minutes after the
			start of the observation.
			\score{0.5}
		\end{itemize}
		
		\SubsectionBox{8. Waiting for a Demand Alert \score{C4-\sml{1}}}
		An economic forecasting system detects significant changes in consumer
		demand at an average rate of 4 alerts every 30 minutes.
		
		\begin{itemize}
			\item[\textbf{(C4)}] Determine the probability that the next
			significant change in consumer demand is detected between 3 and
			8 minutes after the observation time.
			\score{1}
		\end{itemize}

		\SubsectionBox{9. Processing Investment Transactions \score{C4-\sml{1}}}
		An investment platform processes buy and sell transactions throughout
		the trading day. The time between consecutive transactions is modeled
		by an exponential distribution. Based on previous observations, there
		is a \(75\%\) probability that the next transaction is processed within
		8 minutes. On average, how many transactions are expected to be
		processed in a 10-minute interval?
		
		\begin{itemize}
			\item[\textbf{(C4)}] Determine the average number of transactions
			expected to be processed in a 10-minute interval.
			\score{1}
		\end{itemize}
		
		\SubsectionBox{10. Waiting for a Loan Approval \score{C4-\sml{1}}}
		A financial institution processes loan applications throughout the
		day. The processing time for a randomly selected application is
		modeled by an exponential distribution. Based on previous observations,
		there is a \(30\%\) probability that an application takes more than
		12 minutes to process. Find the average number of applications
		processed per minute.
		
		\begin{itemize}
			\item[\textbf{(C4)}] Determine the average number of applications
			processed per minute.
			\score{1}
		\end{itemize}
	
		
		\SubsectionBox{11. Comparing Investment Transaction Rates \score{C4-\sml{2}}}
		Two investment platforms process buy and sell transactions throughout
		the trading day. The transactions processed by each platform are
		modeled by independent Poisson processes.
		
		For Platform A, there is a \(75\%\) probability that the next
		transaction is processed within 8 minutes. For Platform B, there is a
		\(60\%\) probability that the next transaction is processed within
		5 minutes.
		
		Assume both platforms begin processing transactions at the same time.
		On average, how many minutes does it take for one platform to be ahead
		of the other by 1 transaction?
		
		\begin{itemize}
			\item[\textbf{(C4)}] Determine the rate parameter \(\lambda\) for
			each platform and the expected time until one platform is ahead of
			the other by 1 transaction.
			\score{2}
		\end{itemize}
