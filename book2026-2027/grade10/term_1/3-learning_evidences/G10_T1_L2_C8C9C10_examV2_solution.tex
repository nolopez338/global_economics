\subsection*{(b) Subsidy and eligibility \score{2}}

The updated payoff table is:

\begin{center}\small\setlength{\tabcolsep}{4pt}
	\begin{tabular}{lrrr}\toprule
		Alternative & High & Normal & Low\\
		$P(S_j)$ & 0.2 & 0.6 & 0.2\\ \midrule

	\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The updated decision statistics are:

\begin{center}\small\setlength{\tabcolsep}{4pt}
	\begin{tabular}{lrrrr}\toprule
		Alt. & Max & Min & Regret & EMV\\ \midrule
		$A_1$ & 100 & 70 & 140 & 88\\
		$A_2$ & 150 & 40 & 100 & 126\\
		$A_3$ & 240 & $\min(48,-40+s)$ & $\max(102,110-s)$ & $68.8+0.2s$\\
		$A_4$ & 170 & $-20+s$ & $\max(70,90-s)$ & $108+0.2s$\\
		\bottomrule
	\end{tabular}
\end{center}

An alternative is eligible only if all its payoffs are nonnegative.
For integer subsidies $0\le s\le100$:

\begin{center}
	\begin{tabular}{lll}\toprule
		Alternative & Eligibility condition & Eligible subsidies\\\midrule
		$A_1$ & $70\ge0$ & $0\le s\le100$\\
		$A_2$ & $40\ge0$ & $0\le s\le100$\\
		$A_3$ & $-40+s\ge0$ & $40\le s\le100$\\
		$A_4$ & $-20+s\ge0$ & $20\le s\le100$\\
		\bottomrule
	\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Thus, the eligible sets are:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
	0\le s\le19: & \{A_1,A_2\},\\
	20\le s\le39: & \{A_4,A_1,A_2\},\\
	40\le s\le100: & \{A_3,A_4,A_1,A_2\}.
\end{array}
\]

The minimum payoffs are
\[
m_1=70,\quad m_2=40,\quad
m_3(s)=\min(48,-40+s),\quad m_4(s)=-20+s.
\]

Since $m_3(s)\le48<70=m_1$ and $m_2=40<70$ throughout
the allowed range, only $A_4$ can challenge $A_1$.

For $A_4$ to beat $A_1$:
\[
-20+s>70
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad s>90.
\]

Thus, $A_1$ is the sole Maximin choice for $0\le s\le89$;
at $s=90$, $A_4$ and $A_1$ tie with minimum payoff 70;
and $A_4$ is the sole Maximin choice for $91\le s\le100$.
Therefore, the smallest allowed integer subsidy is
\[
\boxed{s=91}.
\]

At $s=91$, all four alternatives are eligible and their minimum
payoffs are $70,40,48,71$, respectively. Hence $A_4$ wins uniquely
with a guaranteed profit of 71 thousand USD.

The updated decision statistics, before applying
eligibility restrictions, are:

\begin{center}\small\setlength{\tabcolsep}{4pt}
	\begin{tabular}{lrrrr}\toprule
		Alt. & Max & Min & Regret & EMV\\ \midrule
		$C_1$ & $g$ & 64 & $180-g$ & $0.4g+38.4$\\
		$C_2$ & 156 & 20 & 44 & 74.4\\
		$C_3$ & 180 & -10 & 74 & 66\\
		$C_4$ & 140 & 50 & 40 & 86\\
		\bottomrule
	\end{tabular}
\end{center}

An alternative is eligible only if both payoffs
are at least 40. For integers $96\le g\le160$:

\begin{center}
	\begin{tabular}{lll}\toprule
		Alternative & Eligibility condition & Eligible values\\\midrule
		$C_1$ & $g\ge40,\ 64\ge40$ & $96\le g\le160$\\
		$C_2$ & $20<40$ & None\\
		$C_3$ & $-10<40$ & None\\
		$C_4$ & $140\ge40,\ 50\ge40$ & $96\le g\le160$\\
		\bottomrule
	\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The eligible set is always $\{C_1,C_4\}$.
Recalculate regrets using only these alternatives,
with state benchmarks
\[
b_H=\max\{g,140\},\qquad b_L=64.
\]

Their maximum regrets are
\[
R(C_1)=\max\{140-g,0\},
\]
\[
R(C_4)=\max\{g-140,14\}.
\]

For $96\le g\le140$, these become
\[
R(C_1)=140-g,\qquad R(C_4)=14.
\]

For $C_1$ to be the sole Minimax Regret choice:
\[
140-g<14
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad g>126.
\]

At $g=126$, both alternatives have maximum regret 14.
Therefore, the smallest eligible integer is
\[
\boxed{g=127}.
\]

At this value, the eligible payoff table is:

\begin{center}\small\setlength{\tabcolsep}{4pt}
	\begin{tabular}{lrr}\toprule
		Alternative & High & Low\\ \midrule
		$C_1$ & 127 & 64\\
		$C_4$ & 140 & 50\\
		\bottomrule
	\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The corresponding maximum regrets are
\[
R(C_1)=13,\qquad R(C_4)=14.
\]

Thus, $C_1$ wins uniquely with $g=127$,
corresponding to \$12,700 in large-audience profit.
