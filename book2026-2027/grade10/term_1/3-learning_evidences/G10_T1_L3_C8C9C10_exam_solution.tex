\section*{Evaluated criteria}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{C\arabic*},start=8]
\item Compares the Maximax, Maximin, Minimax Regret, and EMV/Bayes decision criteria by contrasting their information requirements, risk orientations, recommended alternatives, and limitations.

\phantomsection\label{sol:q1b}
\subsection*{(b) Subsidy and eligibility \score{2}}

The updated payoff table is:

\begin{center}\small\setlength{\tabcolsep}{4pt}
	\begin{tabular}{lrrr}\toprule
		Alternative & High & Normal & Low\\
		$P(S_j)$ & 0.2 & 0.6 & 0.2\\ \midrule

		Flexible ($A_2$) & 85 & 65 & $-10+s$\\
		Basic ($A_3$) & 50 & 45 & 35\\

		\bottomrule
	\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The updated decision statistics are:

\begin{center}\small\setlength{\tabcolsep}{4pt}
	\begin{tabular}{lrrrr}\toprule
		Alt. & Max & Min & Regret & EMV\\ \midrule
		$A_1$ & 120 & $\min(25,-20+s)$ & $\max(50,55-s)$ & $35+0.2s$\\
		$A_2$ & 85 & $-10+s$ & $\max(35,45-s)$ & $54+0.2s$\\
		$A_3$ & 50 & 35 & 70 & 44\\
		$A_4$ & 75 & 20 & 50 & 63\\
		\bottomrule
	\end{tabular}
\end{center}

An alternative is eligible only if all its payoffs are nonnegative.
For integer subsidies $0\le s\le50$:

\begin{center}
	\begin{tabular}{lll}\toprule
		Alternative & Eligibility condition & Eligible subsidies\\\midrule
		$A_1$ & $-20+s\ge0$ & $20\le s\le50$\\
		$A_2$ & $-10+s\ge0$ & $10\le s\le50$\\
		$A_3$ & $35\ge0$ & $0\le s\le50$\\
		$A_4$ & $20\ge0$ & $0\le s\le50$\\
		\bottomrule
	\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Thus, the eligible sets are:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
	0\le s\le9: & \{A_3,A_4\},\\
	10\le s\le19: & \{A_2,A_3,A_4\},\\
	20\le s\le50: & \{A_1,A_2,A_3,A_4\}.
\end{array}
\]

The minimum payoffs are
\[
m_1(s)=\min(25,-20+s),\quad
m_2(s)=-10+s,\quad
m_3=35,\quad m_4=20.
\]

Since $m_1(s)\le25<35=m_3$ and $m_4=20<35$ throughout
the allowed range, only $A_2$ can challenge $A_3$.

For $A_2$ to beat $A_3$:
\[
-10+s>35
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad s>45.
\]

Thus, $A_3$ is the sole Maximin choice for $0\le s\le44$;
at $s=45$, $A_2$ and $A_3$ tie with minimum payoff 35;
and $A_2$ is the sole Maximin choice for $46\le s\le50$.
Therefore, the smallest allowed integer subsidy is
\[
\boxed{s=46}.
\]

At $s=46$, all four alternatives are eligible and their minimum
payoffs are $25,36,35,20$, respectively. Hence $A_2$ wins uniquely
with a guaranteed profit of 36 thousand USD.

\phantomsection\label{sol:q2b}
\subsection*{(b) Sponsored payoff threshold \score{2}}

The updated payoff table is:

\begin{center}\small\setlength{\tabcolsep}{4pt}
	\begin{tabular}{lrr}\toprule
		Alternative & High & Low\\
		$P(S_j)$ & 0.4 & 0.6\\ \midrule
		Large ($C_1$) & 80 & 10\\
		Compact ($C_2$) & $g$ & 32\\
		Standard ($C_3$) & 70 & 25\\
		Premium ($C_4$) & 90 & -5\\
		\bottomrule
	\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The updated decision statistics, before applying
eligibility restrictions, are:

\begin{center}\small\setlength{\tabcolsep}{4pt}
	\begin{tabular}{lrrrr}\toprule
		Alt. & Max & Min & Regret & EMV\\ \midrule
		$C_1$ & 80 & 10 & 22 & 38\\
		$C_2$ & $g$ & 32 & $90-g$ & $0.4g+19.2$\\
		$C_3$ & 70 & 25 & 20 & 43\\
		$C_4$ & 90 & -5 & 37 & 33\\
		\bottomrule
	\end{tabular}
\end{center}

An alternative is eligible only if both payoffs
are at least 20. For integers $48\le g\le80$:

\begin{center}
	\begin{tabular}{lll}\toprule
		Alternative & Eligibility condition & Eligible values\\\midrule
		$C_1$ & $10<20$ & None\\
		$C_2$ & $g\ge20,\ 32\ge20$ & $48\le g\le80$\\
		$C_3$ & $70\ge20,\ 25\ge20$ & $48\le g\le80$\\
		$C_4$ & $-5<20$ & None\\
		\bottomrule
	\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The eligible set is always $\{C_2,C_3\}$.
Recalculate regrets using only these alternatives,
with state benchmarks
\[
b_H=\max\{g,70\},\qquad b_L=32.
\]

Their maximum regrets are
\[
R(C_2)=\max\{70-g,0\},
\]
\[
R(C_3)=\max\{g-70,7\}.
\]

For $48\le g\le70$, these become
\[
R(C_2)=70-g,\qquad R(C_3)=7.
\]

For $C_2$ to be the sole Minimax Regret choice:
\[
70-g<7
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad g>63.
\]

At $g=63$, both alternatives have maximum regret 7.
Therefore, the smallest eligible integer is
\[
\boxed{g=64}.
\]

At this value, the eligible payoff table is:

\begin{center}\small\setlength{\tabcolsep}{4pt}
	\begin{tabular}{lrr}\toprule
		Alternative & High & Low\\ \midrule
		$C_2$ & 64 & 32\\
		$C_3$ & 70 & 25\\
		\bottomrule
	\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The corresponding maximum regrets are
\[
R(C_2)=6,\qquad R(C_3)=7.
\]

Thus, $C_2$ wins uniquely with $g=64$,
corresponding to \$6,400 in high-attendance profit.
