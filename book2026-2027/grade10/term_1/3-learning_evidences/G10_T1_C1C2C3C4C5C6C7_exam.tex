{\small\textbf{Learning objective:} Characterise economic decisions under uncertainty, organise payoff tables, calculate profits from revenues and costs, and justify choices using Maximax, Maximin, Minimax Regret, EMV/Bayes, and probability sensitivity analysis.}
&
{\footnotesize\textbf{Assessment criteria}\par
	\textbf{C1.} Characterises decision-making problems under uncertainty by identifying decision alternatives, states of nature, consequences, and the economic or financial context of the decision.\par
	\textbf{C2.} Organises decision problems using payoff tables, placing alternatives, states of nature, and payoffs in a clear structure for comparison.\par
	\textbf{C3.} Calculates profits by combining revenues with fixed and variable costs across different states of nature, determining the corresponding payoffs for each alternative, and organising the results in payoff tables for comparison.\par
	\textbf{C4.} Applies the Maximin and Maximax criteria to make optimistic or conservative decisions without probabilities, selecting the alternative with the highest possible payoff or the best worst-case, interpreting risks or limitations.\par
	\textbf{C5.} Constructs opportunity loss tables and applies the Minimax Regret criterion to select the alternative that minimises the maximum opportunity loss.\par
	\textbf{C6.} Calculates Expected Monetary Value using stated or estimated probabilities and applies the Bayes decision criterion to select the alternative with the highest expected payoff.\par
	\textbf{C7.} Uses subjective probabilities and sensitivity analysis to examine how changes in probability assumptions affect the preferred decision.}\\\hline
\end{tabular}

\begin{minipage}{\linewidth}
\SubsectionBox{Craft Workshop \score{C1-\sml{3} C2-\sml{6}}}
A craft studio must choose a pottery workshop or a weaving workshop before tourist demand is known. Demand will be strong or weak. Pottery provides a net contribution of 12 USD per participant and a fixed sponsorship contribution of 80 USD per workshop. Weaving provides 15 USD per participant and a fixed sponsorship contribution of 50 USD per workshop. Strong demand adds a further net contribution of 6 USD per participant to either workshop; weak demand adds 2 USD per participant. Pottery attracts 30 participants with strong demand or 18 with weak demand; weaving attracts 24 or 16, respectively. All contributions are additive, and no other adjustments are needed.

Characterize the key elements of the decision-making situation \sscore{C1-3} 
and construct the payoff table. \sscore{C2-6}

\end{minipage}\par

\begin{minipage}{\linewidth}
\SubsectionBox{Guided Cycling Tours \score{C1-\sml{3} C3-\sml{6}}}
A tour operator must choose a town route or a countryside route before seasonal demand is known. Demand will be strong or weak. The town route sells 90 places with strong demand or 60 with weak demand; the countryside route sells 70 or 40, respectively. Base revenue per place is 32 USD for the town route and 45 USD for the countryside route. Strong demand adds a surcharge of 8 USD per place to either route; weak demand adds 3 USD per place. Variable cost per place is 18 USD for the town route and 26 USD for the countryside route. Total fixed seasonal costs, for either route, are 500 USD with strong demand and 650 USD with weak demand. These fixed costs are paid once per season, and there are no other revenues or costs.

Characterize the key elements of the decision-making situation \sscore{C1-3} 
and construct the payoff table. \sscore{C3-6}
\end{minipage}\par
