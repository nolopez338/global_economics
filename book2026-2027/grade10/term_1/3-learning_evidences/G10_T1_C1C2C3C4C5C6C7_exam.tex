{\small\textbf{Learning objective:} Characterise economic decisions under uncertainty, organise payoff tables, calculate profits from revenues and costs, and justify choices using Maximax, Maximin, Minimax Regret, EMV/Bayes, and probability sensitivity analysis.}
&
{\footnotesize\textbf{Assessment criteria}\par
C1. Characterises decision-making problems under uncertainty by identifying decision alternatives, states of nature, consequences, and the economic or financial context of the decision.\par
C2. Organises decision problems using payoff tables, placing alternatives, states of nature, and payoffs in a clear structure for comparison.\par
C3. Calculates profits by combining revenues with fixed and variable costs across different states of nature, determining the corresponding payoffs for each alternative, and organising the results in payoff tables for comparison.\par
C4. Applies the Maximin and Maximax criteria to make optimistic or conservative decisions without probabilities, selecting the alternative with the highest possible payoff or the best worst-case, interpreting risks or limitations.\par
C5. Constructs opportunity loss tables and applies the Minimax Regret criterion to select the alternative that minimises the maximum opportunity loss.\par
C6. Calculates Expected Monetary Value using stated or estimated probabilities and applies the Bayes decision criterion to select the alternative with the highest expected payoff.\par
C7. Uses subjective probabilities and sensitivity analysis to examine how changes in probability assumptions affect the preferred decision.}\\\hline
\end{tabular}

\SubsectionBox{1. Book Fair Stall \score{C1-\sml{1} C2-\sml{2}}}
A bookseller must choose a small stall or a large stall before attendance at a weekend fair is known. Attendance will be either high or low. A small stall earns a profit of 18 thousand USD with high attendance and 11 thousand USD with low attendance. A large stall earns 34 thousand USD with high attendance and 3 thousand USD with low attendance.
\begin{itemize}
\item[\textbf{(C1)}] Characterise the decision: identify the alternatives, states of nature, consequences, and economic context. \score{1}
\item[\textbf{(C2)}] Construct the payoff table with labelled rows, columns, and units. \score{2}
\end{itemize}

\SubsectionBox{2. Laundry Service \score{C1-\sml{1} C2-\sml{3}}}
A laundry business must choose a self-service plan or a staffed plan before weekly demand is known. Demand will be high or low. The self-service plan earns a net contribution of 3 USD per load and handles 160 loads with high demand or 100 with low demand. The staffed plan earns a net contribution of 5 USD per load and handles 130 loads with high demand or 70 with low demand. For either plan, high demand brings a fixed weekly bonus of 40 USD, while low demand causes a fixed weekly loss of 30 USD. These fixed amounts are additional to the per-load contributions; there are no other adjustments.
\begin{itemize}
\item[\textbf{(C1)}] Identify the alternatives, states, consequences, and economic context. \score{1}
\item[\textbf{(C2)}] Calculate the payoffs and construct a labelled payoff table with units. \score{3}
\end{itemize}
\end{multicols}

\SubsectionBox{3. Craft Workshop \score{C1-\sml{2} C2-\sml{5}}}
A craft studio must choose a pottery workshop or a weaving workshop before tourist demand is known. Demand will be strong or weak. Pottery provides a net contribution of 12 USD per participant and a fixed sponsorship contribution of 80 USD per workshop. Weaving provides 15 USD per participant and a fixed sponsorship contribution of 50 USD per workshop. Strong demand adds a further net contribution of 6 USD per participant to either workshop; weak demand adds 2 USD per participant. Pottery attracts 30 participants with strong demand or 18 with weak demand; weaving attracts 24 or 16, respectively. All contributions are additive, and no other adjustments are needed.
\begin{itemize}
\item[\textbf{(C1)}] Identify the alternatives, states, consequences, and economic context; explain which elements the studio controls and which it does not. \score{2}
\item[\textbf{(C2)}] Calculate all four payoffs, showing how you combine the contributions, and construct a labelled payoff table with units. \score{5}
\end{itemize}

\SubsectionBox{5. School-Bag Production \score{C1-\sml{1} C3-\sml{3}}}
A workshop must choose canvas bags or recycled-fabric bags before market conditions are known. The two possible conditions are strong demand with cheap materials, or weak demand with expensive materials. Canvas-bag sales are 80 bags in the first state and 50 in the second; recycled-fabric bag sales are 70 and 40, respectively. Revenue per bag is 24 USD for canvas and 28 USD for recycled fabric. Variable cost per bag is 10 USD in the first state and 16 USD in the second, for either product. All bags produced are sold, and there are no fixed or other costs.
\begin{itemize}
\item[\textbf{(C1)}] Identify the alternatives, states, consequences, and economic context. \score{1}
\item[\textbf{(C3)}] Calculate revenues, costs, and profits for every outcome; construct a labelled profit payoff table with units. \score{3}
\end{itemize}

\SubsectionBox{6. Guided Cycling Tours \score{C1-\sml{2} C3-\sml{5}}}
A tour operator must choose a town route or a countryside route before seasonal demand is known. Demand will be strong or weak. The town route sells 90 places with strong demand or 60 with weak demand; the countryside route sells 70 or 40, respectively. Base revenue per place is 32 USD for the town route and 45 USD for the countryside route. Strong demand adds a surcharge of 8 USD per place to either route; weak demand adds 3 USD per place. Variable cost per place is 18 USD for the town route and 26 USD for the countryside route. Total fixed seasonal costs, for either route, are 500 USD with strong demand and 650 USD with weak demand. These fixed costs are paid once per season, and there are no other revenues or costs.
\begin{itemize}
\item[\textbf{(C1)}] Identify the alternatives, states, consequences, and economic context; distinguish the controlled choice from the uncertain demand. \score{2}
\item[\textbf{(C3)}] Calculate total revenues, total costs, and profits for all four outcomes. Construct a labelled profit payoff table with units. \score{5}
\end{itemize}
