\subsection*{(b) Subsidy and eligibility \score{2}}

The updated payoff table is:

\begin{center}\small\setlength{\tabcolsep}{4pt}

The updated decision statistics are:

\begin{center}\small\setlength{\tabcolsep}{4pt}
	\begin{tabular}{lrrrr}\toprule
		Alt. & Max & Min & Regret & EMV\\ \midrule
				$A_1$ & 105 & 68 & 140 & 89.25\\
		$A_2$ & 155 & 38 & 100 & 123.25\\
		$A_3$ & 245 & $\min(50,-45+s)$ & $\max(105,113-s)$ & $75+0.25s$\\
		$A_4$ & 175 & $-25+s$ & $\max(70,93-s)$ & $103.5+0.25s$\\
		\bottomrule
	\end{tabular}
\end{center}

An alternative is eligible only if all its payoffs are nonnegative.
For integer subsidies $0\le s\le105$:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}\toprule
Alternative & Eligibility condition & Eligible subsidies\\\midrule
$A_1$ & $68\ge0$ & $0\le s\le105$\\
$A_2$ & $38\ge0$ & $0\le s\le105$\\
$A_3$ & $-45+s\ge0$ & $45\le s\le105$\\
$A_4$ & $-25+s\ge0$ & $25\le s\le105$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Thus, the eligible sets are:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
0\le s\le24: & \{A_1,A_2\},\\
25\le s\le44: & \{A_4,A_1,A_2\},\\
45\le s\le105: & \{A_3,A_4,A_1,A_2\}.
\end{array}
\]

The minimum payoffs are
\[
m_1=68,\quad m_2=38,\quad
m_3(s)=\min(50,-45+s),\quad m_4(s)=-25+s.
\]

Since $m_3(s)\le50<68=m_1$ and $m_2=38<68$ throughout
the allowed range, only $A_4$ can challenge $A_1$.

For $A_4$ to beat $A_1$:
\[
-25+s>68
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad s>93.
\]

Thus, $A_1$ is the sole Maximin choice for $0\le s\le92$;
at $s=93$, $A_4$ and $A_1$ tie with minimum payoff 68;
and $A_4$ is the sole Maximin choice for $94\le s\le105$.
Therefore, the smallest allowed integer subsidy is
\[
\boxed{s=94}.
\]

At $s=94$, all four alternatives are eligible and their minimum
payoffs are $68,38,49,69$, respectively. Hence $A_4$ wins uniquely
with a guaranteed profit of 69 thousand USD.

		$P(S_j)$ & 0.45 & 0.55\\ \midrule

The updated decision statistics, before applying
eligibility restrictions, are:

\begin{center}\small\setlength{\tabcolsep}{4pt}
	\begin{tabular}{lrrrr}\toprule
		Alt. & Max & Min & Regret & EMV\\ \midrule
				$C_1$ & $g$ & 66 & $186-g$ & $0.45g+36.3$\\
		$C_2$ & 160 & 22 & 44 & 84.1\\
		$C_3$ & 186 & -14 & 80 & 76\\
		$C_4$ & 144 & 50 & 42 & 92.3\\
		\bottomrule
	\end{tabular}
\end{center}

An alternative is eligible only if both payoffs
are at least 42. For integers $98\le g\le164$:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}\toprule
Alternative & Eligibility condition & Eligible values\\\midrule
$C_1$ & $g\ge42,\ 66\ge42$ & $98\le g\le164$\\
$C_2$ & $22<42$ & None\\
$C_3$ & $-14<42$ & None\\
$C_4$ & $144\ge42,\ 50\ge42$ & $98\le g\le164$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The eligible set is always $\{C_1,C_4\}$.
Recalculate regrets using only these alternatives,
with state benchmarks
\[
b_H=\max\{g,144\},\qquad b_L=66.
\]

Their maximum regrets are
\[
R(C_1)=\max\{144-g,0\},
\]
\[
R(C_4)=\max\{g-144,16\}.
\]

For $98\le g\le144$, these become
\[
R(C_1)=144-g,\qquad R(C_4)=16.
\]

For $C_1$ to be the sole Minimax Regret choice:
\[
144-g<16
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad g>128.
\]

At $g=128$, both alternatives have maximum regret 16.
Therefore, the smallest eligible integer is
\[
\boxed{g=129}.
\]

At this value, the eligible payoff table is:

\begin{center}\small\setlength{\tabcolsep}{4pt}
\begin{tabular}{lrr}\toprule
Alternative & High & Low\\ \midrule
$C_1$ & 129 & 66\\
$C_4$ & 144 & 50\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The corresponding maximum regrets are
\[
R(C_1)=15,\qquad R(C_4)=16.
\]

Thus, $C_1$ wins uniquely with $g=129$,
corresponding to \$12,900 in large-audience profit.
