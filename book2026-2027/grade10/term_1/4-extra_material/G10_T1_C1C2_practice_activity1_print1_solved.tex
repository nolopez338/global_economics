\section*{Payoff Extraction Ladder}
\vspace{-3mm}

For each context, identify the two decision alternatives, the two states of nature, the uncertain event, and the payoff components. Then construct the corresponding \(2\times2\) payoff table. All monetary values are in thousand USD.

\section*{Context Descriptions}
\vspace{-3mm}

\begin{enumerate}

\item
A school event company must choose between a compact snack stand and a full snack stand before attendance is known. Attendance may be high or low. Analysts have already converted each possible consequence into direct profit. The compact stand produces a profit of 18 under high attendance and 11 under low attendance. The full stand produces a profit of 27 under high attendance and 6 under low attendance.

\item
An esports organizer must choose between a standard streaming setup and a professional streaming setup before online viewership is known. Viewership may be high or low. After all setup costs, the sponsorship agreement gives a fixed profit of 12 if the standard setup is chosen and 18 if the professional setup is chosen. The agreement does not change with the realized viewership level.

\item
A weekend pop-up business must choose between an indoor booth and a mobile stall before market demand is known. The indoor booth contributes a fixed 16 to profit, while the mobile stall contributes a fixed 11. Strong demand adds a fixed market bonus of 8 to either alternative. Weak demand instead reduces profit by a fixed 3 because of permit and cleanup expenses.

\item
A meal-preparation business must choose between a standard production plan and a premium production plan before demand is known. The standard plan earns 2 per completed batch and is expected to complete 12 batches under strong demand and 8 under weak demand. The premium plan earns 3 per completed batch and is expected to complete 9 batches under strong demand and 5 under weak demand. There are no additional fixed adjustments.

\item
A local delivery firm must choose between a bicycle fleet and an electric-van fleet before traffic conditions are known. The bicycle fleet earns 4 per completed delivery batch and is expected to complete 14 batches in clear traffic and 10 in congested traffic. The electric-van fleet earns 6 per batch and is expected to complete 12 and 8 batches under the same respective conditions. Clear traffic also adds a fixed scheduling bonus of 5 to either fleet, while congestion creates a fixed loss of 4.

\item
A recycling contractor must choose between manual sorting and automated sorting before recovered-material prices are known. Manual sorting contributes 3 per processed batch and automated sorting contributes 5 per batch. High material prices add another 4 per batch to either alternative, while low material prices add only 1 per batch. Manual sorting is expected to process 11 batches when prices are high and 8 when they are low. Automated sorting is expected to process 9 and 7 batches under the same respective states.

\item
A city-tour operator must choose between a standard tour and a premium tour before tourism demand is known. The standard tour contributes 2 per booking and also provides a fixed contract contribution of 6. The premium tour contributes 4 per booking and provides a fixed contract contribution of 10. High tourism demand adds another 3 per booking to either tour, while low demand adds 1 per booking. The standard tour is expected to complete 16 bookings under high demand and 10 under low demand; the premium tour is expected to complete 12 and 7 bookings.

\item
A food-distribution hub must choose between electric sorting and manual sorting before demand is known. Electric sorting contributes 5 per processed batch and provides a fixed contract contribution of 8. Manual sorting contributes 3 per batch and provides a fixed contract contribution of 12. Strong demand adds another 2 per processed batch and a fixed distributor bonus of 6. Soft demand adds 1 per batch but creates a fixed unused-capacity loss of 4. Electric sorting is expected to process 14 batches under strong demand and 9 under soft demand; manual sorting is expected to process 16 and 11 batches under the same respective states.

\end{enumerate}
