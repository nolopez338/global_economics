	For each problem, identify the decision alternatives, the states of nature,
	the uncertain event, and the consequence. Then calculate revenue, cost, and
	profit for every alternative--state pair and construct the payoff table.
	\vspace{-5mm}

	\section*{Context Descriptions}
	\vspace{-3mm}

	\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=0.22in]
		\item \textbf{Direct case.}
		A small caf\'e must decide whether to launch an artisan-dessert line or keep
		the current menu before foot traffic is known. Traffic may be high or low.
		All values are in thousand USD. Launching the dessert line produces revenue
		and cost of 58 and 16 under high traffic, and 27 and 19 under low traffic.
		Keeping the current menu produces revenue and cost of 49 and 21 under high
		traffic, and 41 and 21 under low traffic.

		\item \textbf{One-component problem.}
		A custom-printing cooperative must choose between producing premium
		notebooks and producing canvas bags before market conditions are known.
		Conditions may be strong demand with stable material prices or weak demand
		with higher material prices. The respective quantities are 6 and 4
		production lots for premium notebooks, and 5 and 3 lots for canvas bags.
		Revenue per lot depends on the product: 12 thousand USD for premium
		notebooks and 15 thousand USD for canvas bags. Cost per lot depends on the
		state: 5 thousand USD under strong demand and stable prices, and 8 thousand
		USD under weak demand and higher prices.

		\item \textbf{Two-component problem.}
		A farm cooperative must choose between a standard produce box and a premium
		produce box before seasonal demand is known. Demand may be strong or weak.
		Quantities, in hundreds of subscriptions, are 7 and 4 for the standard box
		and 6 and 3 for the premium box under strong and weak demand, respectively.
		The plan-based revenue rates are 8 and 11 thousand USD per hundred
		subscriptions for the standard and premium boxes. Strong demand adds a
		state-based revenue rate of 3 thousand USD per hundred subscriptions; weak
		demand adds 1. The plan-based cost rates are 4 and 6 thousand USD per
		hundred subscriptions, respectively. The fixed distribution costs are 9
		thousand USD under strong demand and 13 thousand USD under weak demand.

		\item \textbf{Three-component problem.}
		A regional logistics company must choose a standard or express delivery plan
		before demand and traffic conditions are known. Conditions may be high
		demand with light traffic or moderate demand with heavy traffic. Quantities,
		in hundreds of delivery batches, are 8 and 5 for the standard plan and 10
		and 6 for the express plan under the two states, respectively. The
		plan-based revenue rates are 9 and 11 thousand USD per hundred batches; the
		state-based revenue rates are 3 and 2; and the plan-based fixed revenues are
		16 and 24 thousand USD. The plan-based cost rates are 4 and 5 thousand USD
		per hundred batches; the state-based cost rates are 1 and 3; and the
		state-based fixed costs are 10 and 15 thousand USD.
	\end{enumerate}

	\secInicio[sec:solutions-extraction][sec:problem2-custom-printing-c1-c3]
	\paragraph*{C3 - Calculating profit and building the payoff table.}
	Revenue has one alternative-based variable component, while cost has one
	state-based variable component:
	\[
	R=q(A_i,S_j)r(A_i),\qquad C=q(A_i,S_j)c(S_j),\qquad P=R-C.
	\]
	\textbf{Calculations (thousand USD).}
	\begin{center}\small
		\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{LXXX}
			\toprule Cell & Revenue & Cost & Profit \\ \midrule
			$(A_1,S_1)$ & $6(12)=72$ & $6(5)=30$ & $72-30=42$ \\
			$(A_1,S_2)$ & $4(12)=48$ & $4(8)=32$ & $48-32=16$ \\
			$(A_2,S_1)$ & $5(15)=75$ & $5(5)=25$ & $75-25=50$ \\
			$(A_2,S_2)$ & $3(15)=45$ & $3(8)=24$ & $45-24=21$ \\ \bottomrule
		\end{tabularx}
	\end{center}

	{Profit in thousand USD for each plan--market-state pair}

	\secInicio[sec:solutions-extraction][sec:problem3-farm-subscription-c1-c3]
	\paragraph*{C3 - Calculating profit and building the payoff table.}
	Revenue combines alternative-based and state-based variable components. Cost
	combines an alternative-based variable component and a state-based fixed component:
	\[
	R=q(A_i,S_j)[r(A_i)+r(S_j)],\quad
	C=q(A_i,S_j)c(A_i)+f_c(S_j),\quad P=R-C.
	\]
	\textbf{Calculations (thousand USD).}
	\begin{center}\small
		\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{LXXX}
			\toprule Cell & Revenue & Cost & Profit \\ \midrule
			$(A_1,S_1)$ & $7(8+3)=77$ & $7(4)+9=37$ & $77-37=40$ \\
			$(A_1,S_2)$ & $4(8+1)=36$ & $4(4)+13=29$ & $36-29=7$ \\
			$(A_2,S_1)$ & $6(11+3)=84$ & $6(6)+9=45$ & $84-45=39$ \\
			$(A_2,S_2)$ & $3(11+1)=36$ & $3(6)+13=31$ & $36-31=5$ \\ \bottomrule
		\end{tabularx}
	\end{center}
	\textbf{Final payoff table (profit in thousand USD).}
	\begin{center}
		\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{L>{\centering\arraybackslash}X>{\centering\arraybackslash}X}
			\toprule Alternative & Strong seasonal demand & Weak seasonal demand \\ \midrule
			Standard produce box & 40 & 7 \\
			Premium produce box & 39 & 5 \\ \bottomrule
		\end{tabularx}
	\end{center}

	{Profit in thousand USD for each delivery-plan--state pair}

	\secInicio[sec:solutions-extraction][sec:problem4-regional-delivery-c1-c3]
	\paragraph*{C3 - Calculating profit and building the payoff table.}
	Revenue has alternative- and state-based variable components and an
	alternative-based fixed component. Cost has alternative- and state-based
	variable components and a state-based fixed component:
	\[
	\begin{aligned}
	R&=q(A_i,S_j)[r(A_i)+r(S_j)]+f_r(A_i),\\
	C&=q(A_i,S_j)[c(A_i)+c(S_j)]+f_c(S_j),\\
	P&=R-C.
	\end{aligned}
	\]
	\textbf{Calculations (thousand USD).}
	\begin{center}\small
		\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{LXXX}
			\toprule Cell & Revenue & Cost & Profit \\ \midrule
			$(A_1,S_1)$ & $8(9+3)+16=112$ & $8(4+1)+10=50$ & $112-50=62$ \\
			$(A_1,S_2)$ & $5(9+2)+16=71$ & $5(4+3)+15=50$ & $71-50=21$ \\
			$(A_2,S_1)$ & $10(11+3)+24=164$ & $10(5+1)+10=70$ & $164-70=94$ \\
			$(A_2,S_2)$ & $6(11+2)+24=102$ & $6(5+3)+15=63$ & $102-63=39$ \\ \bottomrule
		\end{tabularx}
	\end{center}

			Standard delivery plan & 62 & 21 \\
			Express delivery plan & 94 & 39 \\ \bottomrule
		\end{tabularx}
