		\vspace{0.25cm}
		\SubsectionBox{1. Weekend Market Stall (C1--C2)}\vspace{-0.3cm}
		A craft cooperative must choose between a central stall, $A_1$, and a neighbourhood stall, $A_2$, before weekend demand is known. Demand may be strong, $S_1$, or weak, $S_2$. The resulting profits, in thousand USD, are supplied directly:
		\[
		P_{11}=14,\quad P_{12}=5,\quad P_{21}=10,\quad P_{22}=8.
		\]
		The first subscript identifies the alternative and the second identifies the state.
		\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\alph*)}]
			\item \textbf{(C1)} Identify the decision alternatives, the uncertain event, its states of nature, and the consequence represented by each $P_{ij}$.
			\item \textbf{(C2)} Construct the payoff table, with alternatives in rows and states in columns.
		\end{enumerate}

		\vspace{0.25cm}
		\SubsectionBox{2. Urban Delivery Service (C1--C2)}\vspace{-0.3cm}
		A retailer must choose either dedicated vans ($A_1$) or flexible couriers ($A_2$) before the delivery network is clear ($S_1$), busy ($S_2$), or disrupted ($S_3$). Payoffs are measured in thousand USD and follow
		\[
		P_{ij}=q_{ij}\left(v_i^A+v_j^S\right)+f_i^A+f_j^S.
		\]
		The supplied components are
		\[
		\begin{aligned}
		v_1^A&=0.7,&v_2^A&=0.5,& f_1^A&=8,&f_2^A&=13,\\
		v_1^S&=0.2,&v_2^S&=0.1,&v_3^S&=-0.1,\\
		f_1^S&=4,&f_2^S&=0,&f_3^S&=-6.
		\end{aligned}
		\]
		The modeled numbers of deliveries are
		\begin{center}
		\begin{tabular}{lccc}
		\toprule
		Alternative & $S_1$ & $S_2$ & $S_3$ \\
		\midrule
		Dedicated vans & 80 & 55 & 30 \\
		Flexible couriers & 70 & 60 & 45 \\
		\bottomrule
		\end{tabular}
		\end{center}
		\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\alph*)}]
			\item \textbf{(C1)} Identify the alternatives, uncertain event, states of nature, and economic consequence.
			\item \textbf{(C2)} Calculate every $P_{ij}$ and organise the results in a payoff table.
		\end{enumerate}

		\SubsectionBox{3. School Lunch Service (C1--C3)}\vspace{-0.3cm}
		A school enterprise must choose a hot-lunch menu ($A_1$) or a sandwich menu ($A_2$) before demand is high ($S_1$) or low ($S_2$). The consequence is profit in thousand USD. Total revenues and total costs are supplied below.
		\begin{center}
		\begin{tabular}{lrrrr}
		\toprule
		Cell & $(A_1,S_1)$ & $(A_1,S_2)$ & $(A_2,S_1)$ & $(A_2,S_2)$ \\
		\midrule
		$R(A_i,S_j)$ & 24 & 15 & 21 & 18 \\
		$C(A_i,S_j)$ & 10 & 9 & 12 & 11 \\
		\bottomrule
		\end{tabular}
		\end{center}
		\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\alph*)}]
			\item \textbf{(C1)} Identify the alternatives, uncertain event, states of nature, and consequence.
			\item \textbf{(C3)} For every cell, calculate $P(A_i,S_j)=R(A_i,S_j)-C(A_i,S_j)$ and construct the payoff table.
		\end{enumerate}

		\vspace{0.25cm}
		\SubsectionBox{4. Cold-Chain Distribution (C1--C3)}\vspace{-0.3cm}
		A food distributor must choose a compact refrigerated fleet ($A_1$) or a large refrigerated fleet ($A_2$) before demand and fuel conditions are favourable ($S_1$), standard ($S_2$), or adverse ($S_3$). Quantities $q(A_i,S_j)$, measured in hundreds of delivery batches, are
		\[
		\begin{aligned}
		q(A_1,S_1)&=8,&q(A_1,S_2)&=6,&q(A_1,S_3)&=3,\\
		q(A_2,S_1)&=11,&q(A_2,S_2)&=7,&q(A_2,S_3)&=4.
		\end{aligned}
		\]
		All rates are in thousand USD per hundred batches and all fixed amounts are in thousand USD:
		\[
		\begin{aligned}
		r^A(A_1)&=12,&r^A(A_2)&=16,& c^A(A_1)&=5,&c^A(A_2)&=7,\\
		r^S(S_1)&=3,&r^S(S_2)&=2,&r^S(S_3)&=1,\\
		c^S(S_1)&=1,&c^S(S_2)&=2,&c^S(S_3)&=4,\\
		F^{R,A}(A_1)&=16,&F^{R,A}(A_2)&=25,&F^{C,A}(A_1)&=20,&F^{C,A}(A_2)&=35,\\
		F^{R,S}(S_1)&=12,&F^{R,S}(S_2)&=8,&F^{R,S}(S_3)&=4,\\
		F^{C,S}(S_1)&=6,&F^{C,S}(S_2)&=10,&F^{C,S}(S_3)&=14.
		\end{aligned}
		\]
		For each pair, use
		\[
		\begin{aligned}
		R(A_i,S_j)&=q(A_i,S_j)[r^A(A_i)+r^S(S_j)]+F^{R,A}(A_i)+F^{R,S}(S_j),\\
		C(A_i,S_j)&=q(A_i,S_j)[c^A(A_i)+c^S(S_j)]+F^{C,A}(A_i)+F^{C,S}(S_j).
		\end{aligned}
		\]
		\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\alph*)}]
			\item \textbf{(C1)} Identify the alternatives, uncertain event, states of nature, and economic consequence.
			\item \textbf{(C3)} Calculate each revenue, cost, and profit $P(A_i,S_j)=R(A_i,S_j)-C(A_i,S_j)$, then construct the payoff table.
		\end{enumerate}

		\vspace{0.25cm}
		\SubsectionBox{5. Mobile Produce Shop (C4)}\vspace{-0.3cm}
		A produce seller must locate a mobile shop at the business district ($A_1$) or the residential district ($A_2$). Demand may be high ($S_1$) or low ($S_2$), and no probabilities are available. Profits, in thousand USD, are:
		\begin{center}
		\begin{tabular}{lcc}
		\toprule
		Alternative & $S_1$ & $S_2$ \\
		\midrule
		Business district & 48 & 12 \\
		Residential district & 35 & 24 \\
		\bottomrule
		\end{tabular}
		\end{center}
		\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\alph*)}]
			\item Apply Maximax and state the selected location. Briefly interpret why this is the optimistic choice.
			\item Apply Maximin and state the selected location. Briefly interpret why this is the conservative choice and one limitation of using only its worst-case payoff.
		\end{enumerate}

		\SubsectionBox{6. Warehouse Technology (C4)}\vspace{-0.3cm}
		A wholesaler must choose one of four warehouse systems before market and supply conditions are known. The possible states are strong demand ($S_1$), steady demand ($S_2$), and weak demand with delays ($S_3$). No probabilities are available. The profits, in thousand USD, are:
		\begin{center}
		\begin{tabular}{lrrr}
		\toprule
		Alternative & $S_1$ & $S_2$ & $S_3$ \\
		\midrule
		Automated system & 96 & 52 & 18 \\
		Modular system & 78 & 61 & 42 \\
		Robotic system & 88 & 48 & 30 \\
		Flexible system & 72 & 64 & 50 \\
		\bottomrule
		\end{tabular}
		\end{center}
		\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\alph*)}]
			\item Apply Maximax and state the selected system. Interpret the opportunity and risk reflected by this optimistic decision.
			\item Apply Maximin and state the selected system. Interpret why a conservative decision-maker may prefer it and give one limitation of the criterion.
		\end{enumerate}
