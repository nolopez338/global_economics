	\subsection{Expected Value and Sensitivity Analysis}\label{sec:c4-intro-expected-value}

	
	When probabilities are subjective or estimated, a decision-maker can
	examine how changes in the probability assumptions affect the preferred
	alternative. The Expected Value criterion allows each alternative to be
	represented as a function of the probability assigned to the possible
	states of nature. For a payoff table with alternatives in rows and states of nature in columns, let
	$
	P_{ay}(A_i,S_j)
	$
	represent the payoff obtained when alternative $A_i$ is selected and
	state of nature $S_j$ occurs.
	
	Let $p$ represent the subjective probability that state of nature $S_1$
	occurs. Since there are two possible states,
	\begin{center}
		$P(S_1)=p$ \qquad and \qquad $P(S_2)=1-p$ \qquad with \quad $0\leq p\leq 1$
	\end{center}
	
	For each alternative, calculate its Expected Value as a function of $p$:
	$$
	EV(A_i)
	=
	pP_{ay}(A_i,S_1)
	+
	(1-p)P_{ay}(A_i,S_2).
	$$
	
	This expression shows how the expected payoff changes when the
	subjective probability of $S_1$ changes. The preferred alternative depends on the value of $p$. To determine
	which alternative is preferred, compare their expected-value functions.	For two alternatives $A_1$ and $A_2$, the threshold probability is found
	by solving
	$$
	EV(A_1)=EV(A_2).
	$$
	
	At this threshold, the decision-maker is indifferent between the two
	alternatives. The preferred alternative on each side of the threshold
	is determined by comparing the corresponding expected values. Therefore, sensitivity analysis identifies how changes in the subjective
	probability affect the preferred decision.
	
	\subsection{General 2x2 Problem}\label{sec:c4-intro-2x2}

	
	Consider the following payoff table, where the probability of state
	$S_1$ is $p$ and the probability of state $S_2$ is $1-p$:
	\begin{center}
		\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
		\begin{tabular}{lcc}
			\toprule
			Alternative
			& \begin{tabular}{c}
				$S_1$\\
				$(p)$
			\end{tabular}
			& \begin{tabular}{c}
				$S_2$\\
				$(1-p)$
			\end{tabular}
			\\
			\midrule
			$A_1$
			& $P_{ay}(A_1,S_1)$
			& $P_{ay}(A_1,S_2)$
			\\
			$A_2$
			& $P_{ay}(A_2,S_1)$
			& $P_{ay}(A_2,S_2)$
			\\
			\bottomrule
		\end{tabular}
	\end{center}
	
	Determine the preferred alternative as a function of $p$, including the
	threshold probability at which the decision-maker is indifferent.

	\subsubsection{Solution}
	
	\textbf{Step 1: Identify the alternatives, states of nature, and probabilities}
	
	Alternatives: $A_1, A_2$ \qquad
	States of nature: $S_1, S_2$
	
	Probabilities: $P(S_1) = p$	\qquad	$P(S_2) = 1-p$
	
	\noindent
	\setlength{\columnseprule}{0.4pt}
	
	\begin{multicols}{2}
		\vspace{0pt}
		
		\textbf{Step 2: Calculate the expected value of $A_1$}
		
		For alternative $A_1$:
		
		\[
		EV(A_1)
		=
		pP_{ay}(A_1,S_1)
		+
		(1-p)P_{ay}(A_1,S_2).
		\]
		
		This expression shows how the expected payoff of $A_1$ changes as
		$p$ changes.
		
		\columnbreak
		
		\textbf{Step 3: Calculate the expected value of $A_2$}
		
		For alternative $A_2$:
		
		\[
		EV(A_2)
		=
		pP_{ay}(A_2,S_1)
		+
		(1-p)P_{ay}(A_2,S_2).
		\]
		
		This expression shows how the expected payoff of $A_2$ changes as
		$p$ changes.
		
	\end{multicols}
	
	\setlength{\columnseprule}{0pt}
	
	\textbf{Step 4: Find the threshold probability}
	
	The threshold occurs when the two alternatives have the same expected
	value:
	$$
	EV(A_1)=EV(A_2).
	$$
	
	Solve this equation for $p$. The resulting value is the probability at
	which the decision-maker is indifferent between the two alternatives.
	
	\textbf{Step 5: Perform the sensitivity analysis}
	
	Compare the expected values on each side of the threshold.
	
	\begin{center}
		\begin{minipage}{0.4\textwidth}
			\[
			\begin{aligned}
				EV(A_1)>EV(A_2) &\quad\Rightarrow\quad \text{choose } A_1,\\
				EV(A_2)>EV(A_1) &\quad\Rightarrow\quad \text{choose } A_2,\\
				EV(A_1)=EV(A_2) &\quad\Rightarrow\quad \text{indifferent}.
			\end{aligned}
			\]
		\end{minipage}
		$ \qquad \longrightarrow \qquad$
		\begin{minipage}{0.4\textwidth}
			\textbf{Decision rule}
			\[
			\begin{cases}
				A_1, & \text{if } EV(A_1)>EV(A_2),\\
				A_2, & \text{if } EV(A_2)>EV(A_1),\\
				\text{indifferent}, & \text{if } EV(A_1)=EV(A_2).
			\end{cases}
			\]
		\end{minipage}
	\end{center}
	
	Thus, the preferred alternative can be expressed as a function of the
	subjective probability $p$.
	
	\textbf{Extended payoff table}
	
	\begin{center}
		\small
		\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.5}
		\setlength{\tabcolsep}{4pt}
		
		\begin{tabular}{lccc}
			\toprule
			Alternative
			& $S_1\;(p)$
			& $S_2\;(1-p)$
			& Expected Value
			\\
			\midrule
			
			$A_1$
			& $P_{ay}(A_1,S_1)$
			& $P_{ay}(A_1,S_2)$
			&
			$pP_{ay}(A_1,S_1)
			+
			(1-p)P_{ay}(A_1,S_2)$
			\\
			
			$A_2$
			& $P_{ay}(A_2,S_1)$
			& $P_{ay}(A_2,S_2)$
			&
			$pP_{ay}(A_2,S_1)
			+
			(1-p)P_{ay}(A_2,S_2)$
			\\
			
			\bottomrule
		\end{tabular}
		
	\end{center}
	
	\subsection{General 2x3 Problem}\label{sec:c4-intro-2x3}

	
	Consider the following payoff table, where the probabilities of the states
	of nature are subjective and may vary:
	
	\begin{multicols}{2}
		
		\begin{center}
			\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
			\begin{tabular}{lccc}
				\toprule
				Alternative
				& \begin{tabular}{c}
					$S_1$\\
					$(p)$
				\end{tabular}
				& \begin{tabular}{c}
					$S_2$\\
					$(q)$
				\end{tabular}
				& \begin{tabular}{c}
					$S_3$\\
					$(1-p-q)$
				\end{tabular}
				\\
				\midrule
				$A_1$
				& $P_{ay}(A_1,S_1)$
				& $P_{ay}(A_1,S_2)$
				& $P_{ay}(A_1,S_3)$
				\\
				$A_2$
				& $P_{ay}(A_2,S_1)$
				& $P_{ay}(A_2,S_2)$
				& $P_{ay}(A_2,S_3)$
				\\

			\end{tabular}

		\columnbreak
		
		\[
		P(S_1)=p,
		\qquad
		P(S_2)=q,
		\qquad
		P(S_3)=1-p-q
		\]
		
		\[
		p\geq 0,
		\qquad
		q\geq 0,
		\qquad
		p+q\leq 1
		\]
		
	\end{multicols}
	
	
	Determine the preferred alternative as a function of the subjective
	probabilities $p$ and $q$, including any threshold values or boundaries
	at which the preferred decision changes.
	
	\subsubsection{Solution}
	
	\textbf{Step 1: Identify the alternatives, states of nature, and probabilities}
	
	Alternatives: $A_1, A_2$
	\qquad
	States of nature: $S_1, S_2, S_3$
	
	Probabilities:
	$
	P(S_1)=p,
	\qquad
	P(S_2)=q,
	\qquad
	P(S_3)=1-p-q.
	$
	
	\noindent
	\setlength{\columnseprule}{0.4pt}
	
	\begin{multicols}{2}
		\vspace{0pt}
		
		\textbf{Step 2: Calculate the expected value of $A_1$}
		\[
		\begin{aligned}
			EV(A_1)
			&=pP_{ay}(A_1,S_1)
			+qP_{ay}(A_1,S_2)\\
			&\quad +(1-p-q)P_{ay}(A_1,S_3).
		\end{aligned}
		\]
		
		This expression shows how the expected payoff of $A_1$ changes
		as the subjective probabilities change.
		
		\columnbreak
		
		\textbf{Step 3: Calculate the expected value of $A_2$}
		\[
		\begin{aligned}
			EV(A_2)
			&=pP_{ay}(A_2,S_1)
			+qP_{ay}(A_2,S_2)\\
			&\quad +(1-p-q)P_{ay}(A_2,S_3).
		\end{aligned}
		\]
		
	\end{multicols}
	
	\setlength{\columnseprule}{0pt}
	
	\textbf{Step 4: Compare the expected-value functions and perform the sensitivity analysis}
	
	Compare
	$$
	EV(A_1),\quad
	EV(A_2).
	$$
	
	The preferred alternative depends on the values of $p$ and $q$. Examine
	the preferred alternative for different feasible combinations of $p$ and
	$q$, subject to
	$$
	p\geq0,\qquad q\geq0,\qquad p+q\leq1.
	$$
	
	The boundary occurs when the expected values of the two alternatives
	are equal, indicating indifference:
	$$
	EV(A_1)=EV(A_2)
	\quad\text{at a boundary}.
	$$
	
	The preferred alternative is the one with the highest expected value:
	$$
	A^*=\arg\max_{i}EV(A_i)
	$$ \vspace{-10mm}

	\section{Contextualized 2x2 Problem - Clothing store}
	
	A local clothing store is deciding whether to launch a new advertising campaign
	before the holiday season or continue with its current marketing strategy. The
	manager is uncertain about the level of sales during the holiday season. Let
	$p$ represent the subjective probability of strong sales. Therefore, the
	probability of weak sales is $1-p$. All payoffs are expressed in thousands of
	dollars.
	
	Using the Expected Value criterion, determine how the preferred decision
	changes as the subjective probability $p$ changes. Identify the threshold
	probability at which the manager is indifferent between the two alternatives.
	
	\vspace{-2mm}
	
	\begin{center}
		\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
		\begin{tabular}{lcc}
			\toprule
			Alternative
			& \begin{tabular}{c}
				Strong sales $S_1$ \\
				$P(S_1)=p$
			\end{tabular}
			& \begin{tabular}{c}
				Weak sales $S_2$ \\
				$P(S_2)=1-p$
			\end{tabular}
			\\
			\midrule
			Launch new campaign ($A_1$) & 55 & 5 \\
			Keep current strategy ($A_2$) & 45 & 45 \\
			\bottomrule
		\end{tabular}
	\end{center}
	
	\vspace{-5mm}
	
	\subsection{Solution}
	
	\textbf{Step 1: Identify the alternatives, states of nature, and probabilities}
	
	Alternatives:
	$A_1$ = Launch new campaign,
	$A_2$ = Keep current strategy
	
	States of nature:
	$S_1$ = Strong sales,
	$S_2$ = Weak sales
	
	Probabilities:
	$
	P(S_1)=p,
	P(S_2)=1-p
	$
	with
	$
	0\leq p\leq1.
	$
	
	\vspace{-3mm}
	
	\noindent
	\setlength{\columnseprule}{0.4pt}
	\begin{multicols}{2}
		\vspace{0pt}
		
		\textbf{Step 2: Calculate the expected value of $A_1$}
		
		For the Launch new campaign alternative:
		\[
		EV(A_1)
		=
		p(55)
		+
		(1-p)(5)
		\]
		\[
		=
		55p+5-5p
		=
		50p+5.
		\]
		
		Thus, the expected payoff from launching the new campaign is
		$50p+5$ thousand dollars.
		
		\columnbreak
		
		\textbf{Step 3: Calculate the expected value of $A_2$}
		
		For the Keep current strategy alternative:
		\[
		EV(A_2)
		=
		p(45)
		+
		(1-p)(45)
		\]
		\[
		=
		45p+45-45p
		=
		45.
		\]
		
		Thus, the expected payoff from keeping the current strategy is
		$45$ thousand dollars.
		
	\end{multicols}
	
	\EMVGraph{
		{50}{5}{blue}{$EMV(A_1)=50p+5$},
		{0}{45}{red}{$EMV(A_2)=45$}
	}
	
	\setlength{\columnseprule}{0pt}
	
	\textbf{Step 4: Find the threshold probability}
	
	The threshold occurs when the two alternatives have the same expected
	value:
	$$
	EV(A_1)=EV(A_2)
	\qquad \longrightarrow \qquad
	50p+5=45
	$$
	$$
	\qquad \longrightarrow \qquad
	50p=40
	\qquad \longrightarrow \qquad
	p=0.8.
	$$
	
	Thus, the manager is indifferent between the two alternatives when the
	subjective probability of strong sales is $80\%$.
	
	\textbf{Step 5: Perform the sensitivity analysis}
	
	Compare the expected values on each side of the threshold.
	$$
	\begin{aligned}
		p<0.800 &\quad\Rightarrow\quad EV(A_1)<EV(A_2) \quad\Rightarrow\quad \text{choose } A_2,\\
		p>0.800 &\quad\Rightarrow\quad EV(A_1)>EV(A_2) \quad\Rightarrow\quad \text{choose } A_1,\\
		p=0.800 &\quad\Rightarrow\quad EV(A_1)=EV(A_2) \quad\Rightarrow\quad \text{indifferent}.
	\end{aligned}
	\qquad \longrightarrow \qquad 
	\boxed{
		\begin{cases}
			A_2, & 0\leq p<0.800,\\
			\text{indifferent}, & p=0.800,\\
			A_1, & 0.800<p\leq1.
	\end{cases}}
	$$
	
	The sensitivity analysis shows that the clothing store should keep its current
	marketing strategy when the subjective probability of strong sales is below
	$80\%$. Only when that probability exceeds $80\%$ does launching the new
	advertising campaign have the higher expected value.
	
	\textbf{Extended payoff table}
	
	\begin{center}
		\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.5}
		\setlength{\tabcolsep}{4pt}
		
		\begin{tabular}{lccc}
			\toprule
			Alternative
			& \begin{tabular}{c}Strong sales $S_1$ \\ $P(S_1)=p$\end{tabular}
			& \begin{tabular}{c}Weak sales $S_2$ \\ $P(S_2)=1-p$\end{tabular}
			& Expected Value
			\\
			\midrule
			
			$A_1$: Launch new campaign
			& $55$
			& $5$
			& $p(55)+(1-p)(5)=50p+5$
			\\
			
			$A_2$: Keep current strategy
			& $45$
			& $45$
			& $p(45)+(1-p)(45)=45$
			\\
			
			\bottomrule
		\end{tabular}
	\end{center}
	
	
	\section{2x2 - Coffee shop}
	
	A small coffee shop is considering whether to introduce a new dessert line
	or keep its current menu. The manager is uncertain about the level of
	customer traffic during the promotional weekend. Let $p$ represent the
	subjective probability of high customer traffic. Therefore, the probability
	of low customer traffic is $1-p$. All payoffs are expressed in thousands of
	dollars.
	Using the Expected Value criterion, determine how the preferred decision
	changes as the subjective probability $p$ changes. Identify the threshold
	probability at which the manager is indifferent between the two alternatives.
	\vspace{-4mm}
	\begin{center}
		\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
		\begin{tabular}{lcc}
			\toprule

	$65.5\%$.
	
	\textbf{Step 7: Perform the sensitivity analysis}
	
	Compare the expected values on each side of the thresholds.
	
	$$
	\begin{aligned}
		p<0.259
		&\quad\Rightarrow\quad
		EV(A_3)>EV(A_2),EV(A_1)
		\quad\Rightarrow\quad
		\text{choose }A_3,
		\\
		p=0.259
		&\quad\Rightarrow\quad
		EV(A_2)=EV(A_3)
		\quad\Rightarrow\quad
		\text{indifferent between }A_2\text{ and }A_3,
		\\
		0.259<p<0.655
		&\quad\Rightarrow\quad
		EV(A_2)>EV(A_1),EV(A_3)
		\quad\Rightarrow\quad
		\text{choose }A_2,
		\\
		p=0.655
		&\quad\Rightarrow\quad
		EV(A_1)=EV(A_2)
		\quad\Rightarrow\quad
		\text{indifferent between }A_1\text{ and }A_2,
		\\
		p>0.655
		&\quad\Rightarrow\quad
		EV(A_1)>EV(A_2),EV(A_3)
		\quad\Rightarrow\quad
		\text{choose }A_1.
	\end{aligned}
	$$
	
	Therefore, the preferred decision depends on the subjective probability
	of high demand:
	
	$$
	\boxed{
		\begin{cases}
			A_3, & 0\leq p<0.259,\\
			\text{indifferent between }A_2\text{ and }A_3,
			& p=0.259,\\
			A_2, & 0.259<p<0.655,\\
			\text{indifferent between }A_1\text{ and }A_2,
			& p=0.655,\\
			A_1, & 0.655<p\leq1.
	\end{cases}}
	$$
	
	The sensitivity analysis shows that the company should choose the Budget
	version when the subjective probability of high demand is below approximately
	$25.9\%$. Between $25.9\%$ and $65.5\%$, the Standard version has the highest
	expected value. When the probability of high demand exceeds $65.5\%$, the
	Premium version becomes the preferred decision.
