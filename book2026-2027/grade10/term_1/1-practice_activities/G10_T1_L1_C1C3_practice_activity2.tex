\item[\textbf{C3}] Calculates profit as revenue minus costs across states of nature and organises the resulting payoffs in payoff tables.

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

\SectionProblemsTableOfContents{
	\SectionProblemLink{sec:problem1-introductory-general-c1-c3}{Introductory General Problem (C1--C3)} \\
	\SectionProblemLink{sec:problem2-advanced-general-c1-c3}{Advanced General Problem (C1--C3)}
}\vspace{8mm}

A decision problem under uncertainty requires a choice of an
\emph{alternative} \(A_i\) before an uncertain event reveals a
\emph{state of nature} \(S_j\). The alternative is a row of the table and the
state is a column. Their combination produces a \emph{consequence}; its
monetary \emph{payoff} is profit \(P(A_i,S_j)\). \emph{Revenue}
\(R(A_i,S_j)\) is income earned, \emph{cost} \(C(A_i,S_j)\) is the value of
resources used, and
\[
P(A_i,S_j)=R(A_i,S_j)-C(A_i,S_j).
\]
A payoff table places every \(P(A_i,S_j)\) at row \(A_i\) and column \(S_j\).
The objective here is to construct that complete table, not to choose the best
alternative.

Revenue and cost are independent axes. Their general models are
\[
\begin{aligned}
	R(A_i,S_j)
	={}&
	q(A_i,S_j)
	\left[
	r(A_i)+r(S_j)
	\right]
	+
	f_r(A_i)
	+
	f_r(S_j)
	\\
	C(A_i,S_j)
	={}&
	q(A_i,S_j)
	\left[
	c(A_i)+c(S_j)
	\right]
	+
	f_c(A_i)
	+
	f_c(S_j)
\end{aligned}
\]
Therefore,
\[
\begin{aligned}
	P(A_i,S_j)
	={}&
	q(A_i,S_j)
	\left[
	r(A_i)+r(S_j)
	\right]
	+
	f_r(A_i)
	+
	f_r(S_j)
	\\
	&-
	\left\{
	q(A_i,S_j)
	\left[
	c(A_i)+c(S_j)
	\right]
	+
	f_c(A_i)
	+
	f_c(S_j)
	\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Here a \emph{variable component} is quantity \(q(A_i,S_j)\) multiplied by a
per-unit rate; a \emph{fixed component} is a lump sum independent of quantity.
The argument \(A_i\) identifies an alternative-based component, while the
argument \(S_j\) identifies a state-based component. The quantity
\(q(A_i,S_j)\) depends on both arguments and may
change in every cell. Thus, inspect the arguments: \(A_i\) means that the value
depends on the alternative, \(S_j\) means that it depends on the state, and
\((A_i,S_j)\) means that it may depend on both. A fixed lump sum is not
multiplied by quantity. A component absent from one model is zero. A directly
supplied \(R(A_i,S_j)\) or \(C(A_i,S_j)\) needs no decomposition.

\textbf{Reusable procedure.}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
	\item Identify the alternatives, uncertain event, states, consequences, and
	units.
	
	\item Separate all revenue data from all cost data.
	
	\item Classify each item as variable or fixed and as alternative-based or
	state-based; treat absent components as zero.
	
	\item For every cell, calculate \(R(A_i,S_j)\) from its revenue model and
	\(C(A_i,S_j)\) independently from its cost model.
	
	\item Subtract revenue minus costs:
	\[
	P(A_i,S_j)=R(A_i,S_j)-C(A_i,S_j).
	\]
	
	\item Put alternatives in rows and states in columns; check every cell and
	all units.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Paired ladder overview.} AV, SV, AF, and SF denote
alternative-variable, state-variable, alternative-fixed, and state-fixed
components. Within each tier, the cost list is a deliberate permutation of the
revenue list; matching structures are not assumed.

\begin{center}
	\small
	\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}lXX@{}}
		\toprule

		& Revenue structures, in problem order
		& Cost structures, in problem order \\
		\midrule

\(C(A_i,S_j)\) are supplied for every alternative--state pair. For

\[
P(A_i,S_j)=R(A_i,S_j)-C(A_i,S_j).
\]

components and cost contains exactly three nonzero components. Each model
is constructed independently, so the revenue and cost structures do not
need to match.

In a complete four-component model, both revenue and cost contain all four
possible components:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
	\item an alternative-based variable component;
	\item a state-based variable component;
	\item an alternative-based fixed component;
	\item a state-based fixed component.
\end{enumerate}

Therefore, the complete revenue and cost models are
\[
\begin{aligned}
	R(A_i,S_j)
	&=
	q(A_i,S_j)
	\left[
	r(A_i)+r(S_j)
	\right]
	+
	f_r(A_i)
	+
	f_r(S_j),
	\\
	C(A_i,S_j)
	&=
	q(A_i,S_j)
	\left[
	c(A_i)+c(S_j)
	\right]
	+
	f_c(A_i)
	+
	f_c(S_j).
\end{aligned}
\]

The payoff for each alternative--state pair is consequently
\[
\begin{aligned}
	P(A_i,S_j)
	={}&
	\left\{
	q(A_i,S_j)
	\left[
	r(A_i)+r(S_j)
	\right]
	+
	f_r(A_i)
	+
	f_r(S_j)
	\right\}
	\\
	&-
	\left\{
	q(A_i,S_j)
	\left[
	c(A_i)+c(S_j)
	\right]
	+
	f_c(A_i)
	+
	f_c(S_j)
	\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The three problems in this section use \(2\times2\), \(3\times2\), and
\(2\times3\) alternative--state structures, respectively.

\subsection{Electric Vehicle Charging Network (C1--C3)}
\label{sec:problem21-electric-vehicle-charging-network-c1-c3}

An energy company must select an electric vehicle charging-network design
before charging demand and electricity-market conditions are known. Revenue per
charging-session batch combines a rate determined by the selected network and a
rate determined by the state. Revenue also includes a fixed service payment
determined by the network and a fixed utilisation incentive determined by the
state.

Variable cost per charging-session batch combines a network-based equipment
rate and a state-based electricity rate. Fixed cost consists of an
infrastructure cost determined by the network and a maintenance cost determined
by the state.

\begin{multicols}{2}
	The company is comparing two alternatives:
	\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
		\item \(A_1\): Build the neighbourhood charging network
		\item \(A_2\): Build the rapid-charging network
	\end{enumerate}
	
	The possible states of nature are:
	\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
		\item \(S_1\): High utilisation with favourable electricity prices
		\item \(S_2\): Moderate utilisation with standard electricity prices
		\item \(S_3\): Low utilisation with expensive electricity
	\end{enumerate}
	
	The quantities, measured in hundreds of charging-session batches, are
	\[
	\begin{aligned}
		q(A_1,S_1)&=10,
		&
		q(A_1,S_2)&=7,
		&
		q(A_1,S_3)&=4,
		\\
		q(A_2,S_1)&=14,
		&
		q(A_2,S_2)&=9,
		&
		q(A_2,S_3)&=5.
	\end{aligned}
	\]
	
	The alternative-based revenue rates, in thousand USD per hundred
	charging-session batches, are
	\[
	r(A_1)=11,
	\qquad
	r(A_2)=16.
	\]
	
	The state-based revenue rates are
	\[
	\begin{aligned}
		r(S_1)&=4,
		&
		r(S_2)&=3,
		&
		r(S_3)&=2.
	\end{aligned}
	\]
	
	\columnbreak
	
	The alternative-based fixed revenues, in thousand USD, are
	\[
	f_r(A_1)=18,
	\qquad
	f_r(A_2)=30.
	\]
	
	The state-based fixed revenues, in thousand USD, are
	\[
	\begin{aligned}
		f_r(S_1)&=16,
		&
		f_r(S_2)&=10,
		&
		f_r(S_3)&=5.
	\end{aligned}
	\]
	
	The alternative-based cost rates, in thousand USD per hundred
	charging-session batches, are
	\[
	c(A_1)=4,
	\qquad
	c(A_2)=7.
	\]
	
	The state-based cost rates are
	\[
	\begin{aligned}
		c(S_1)&=2,
		&
		c(S_2)&=3,
		&
		c(S_3)&=5.
	\end{aligned}
	\]
	
	The alternative-based fixed costs, in thousand USD, are
	\[
	f_c(A_1)=25,
	\qquad
	f_c(A_2)=45.
	\]
	
	The state-based fixed costs, in thousand USD, are
	\[
	\begin{aligned}
		f_c(S_1)&=8,
		&
		f_c(S_2)&=12,
		&
		f_c(S_3)&=18.
	\end{aligned}
	\]
\end{multicols}
