\section*{Evaluated criteria}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{C\arabic*.}]
\item Characterises decision-making problems under uncertainty by identifying decision alternatives, states of nature, consequences, and the economic or financial context of the decision.
\item[\textbf{C3.}] Calculates profit as revenue minus costs across states of nature and organises the resulting payoffs in payoff tables.
\end{enumerate}
\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
\SectionProblemsTableOfContents{
\SectionProblemLink{sec:problem1-introductory-general-c1-c3}{Introductory General Problem (C1--C3)} \\
\SectionProblemLink{sec:problem2-advanced-general-c1-c3}{Advanced General Problem (C1--C3)}
}
A decision problem under uncertainty requires a choice of an \emph{alternative} $A_i$ before an uncertain event reveals a \emph{state of nature} $S_j$. The alternative is a row of the table and the state is a column. Their combination produces a \emph{consequence}; its monetary \emph{payoff} is profit $P_{ij}$. \emph{Revenue} $R_{ij}$ is income earned, \emph{cost} $C_{ij}$ is the value of resources used, and
\[
 P_{ij}=R_{ij}-C_{ij}.
\]
A payoff table places every $P_{ij}$ at row $A_i$ and column $S_j$. The objective here is to construct that complete table, not to choose the best alternative.

Revenue and cost are independent axes. Their general models are
\[
R_{ij}=q_{ij}(r_i^A+r_j^S)+F_i^{R,A}+F_j^{R,S},\qquad
C_{ij}=q_{ij}(c_i^A+c_j^S)+F_i^{C,A}+F_j^{C,S},
\]
so
\[
P_{ij}=q_{ij}(r_i^A+r_j^S)+F_i^{R,A}+F_j^{R,S}
-\left[q_{ij}(c_i^A+c_j^S)+F_i^{C,A}+F_j^{C,S}\right].
\]
Here a \emph{variable component} is quantity $q_{ij}$ multiplied by a per-unit rate; a \emph{fixed component} is a lump sum independent of quantity. Superscript $A$ marks an alternative-based component that changes by row, and superscript $S$ marks a state-based component that changes by column. The quantity $q_{ij}$ has both indices and may change in every cell. Thus inspect the indices: $i$ means the value depends on the alternative, $j$ means it depends on the state, $ij$ means it may depend on both, and a fixed lump sum is not multiplied by quantity. A component absent from one model is zero. A directly supplied $R_{ij}$ or $C_{ij}$ needs no decomposition.

\textbf{Reusable procedure.}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item Identify the alternatives, uncertain event, states, consequences, and units.
\item Separate all revenue data from all cost data.
\item Classify each item as variable or fixed and as alternative-based or state-based; treat absent components as zero.
\item For every cell, calculate $R_{ij}$ from its revenue model and $C_{ij}$ independently from its cost model.
\item Subtract revenue minus costs: $P_{ij}=R_{ij}-C_{ij}$.
\item Put alternatives in rows and states in columns; check every cell and all units.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Paired ladder overview.} AV, SV, AF, and SF denote alternative-variable, state-variable, alternative-fixed, and state-fixed components. Within each tier the cost list is a deliberate permutation of the revenue list; matching structures are not assumed.
