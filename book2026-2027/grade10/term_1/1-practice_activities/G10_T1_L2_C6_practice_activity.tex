	\subsection{Expected Value Criterion}\label{sec:c4-intro-purpose}

	
	When probabilities can be assigned to the possible states of nature,
	a decision-maker can compare alternatives using their expected monetary
	values. The Expected Monetary Value (EMV) criterion, also known as the Bayes decision criterion, combines the payoff associated with each possible state of nature with the probability that the state will occur.
	For a payoff table with alternatives in rows and states of nature in
	columns, let
	$
	P_{ay}(A_i,S_j)
	$
	represent the payoff obtained when alternative $A_i$ is selected and
	state of nature $S_j$ occurs.
	%
	\begin{center}
		Let $P(S_j)$ represent the probability that state of nature $S_j$ occurs.
	\end{center}
	%
	The probabilities must satisfy
	$$
	\sum_{j=1}^{n} P(S_j) = 1.
	$$
	%
	For each alternative, calculate its Expected Monetary Value:
	$$
	EMV(A_i)
	=
	\sum_{j=1}^{n}
	P(S_j)P_{ay}(A_i,S_j)
	$$
	
	or, explicitly,
	$$
	EMV(A_i)
	=
	P(S_1)P_{ay}(A_i,S_1)
	+
	P(S_2)P_{ay}(A_i,S_2)
	+
	\cdots
	+
	P(S_n)P_{ay}(A_i,S_n).
	$$
	
	The Bayes decision is the alternative with the highest expected payoff:
	$$
	A^*_{\mathrm{Bayes}}
	=
	\arg\max_i EMV(A_i).
	$$
	
	Therefore, the decision-maker selects the alternative with the highest
	Expected Monetary Value.

	\subsection{General 2x2 Problem}\label{sec:c4-intro-2x2}

	
	Consider the following payoff table, where each state of nature has an
	associated probability:
	
	\begin{center}
		\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
		\begin{tabular}{lcc}
			\toprule
			Alternative & $S_1$ & $S_2$ \\
			\midrule
			$A_1$
			& $P_{ay}(A_1,S_1)$
			& $P_{ay}(A_1,S_2)$ \\
			$A_2$
			& $P_{ay}(A_2,S_1)$
			& $P_{ay}(A_2,S_2)$ \\
			\bottomrule
		\end{tabular}
		
	\end{center}
	
	The probabilities of the states of nature are
	
	$$
	P(S_1),
	\qquad
	P(S_2),
	\qquad
	P(S_1)+P(S_2)=1.
	$$
	
	Determine the decision using the Expected Monetary Value criterion.
	
	% ============================================================
	% SOLUTION 2x2
	% ============================================================
	
	\subsubsection{Solution}
	
	\textbf{Step 1: Identify the alternatives, states of nature, and probabilities}
	
	Alternatives: $A_1, A_2$ \qquad
	States of nature: $S_1, S_2$ \qquad
	Probabilities
	$
	P(S_1),
	P(S_2)
	$
	
	\noindent
	\setlength{\columnseprule}{0.4pt}
	
	\begin{multicols}{2}
		\vspace{0pt}
		
		
		\textbf{Step 2: Calculate the EMV of $A_1$}
		
		For alternative $A_1$, multiply each payoff by the probability of
		its corresponding state of nature and add the results:
		
		\[
		EMV(A_1)
		=
		P(S_1)P_{ay}(A_1,S_1)
		+
		P(S_2)P_{ay}(A_1,S_2).
		\]
		
		Thus, $EMV(A_1)$ represents the expected payoff from selecting
		alternative $A_1$.
		
		\columnbreak
		
		\textbf{Step 3: Calculate the EMV of $A_2$}
		
		For alternative $A_2$:
		
		\[
		EMV(A_2)
		=
		P(S_1)P_{ay}(A_2,S_1)
		+
		P(S_2)P_{ay}(A_2,S_2).
		\]
		
		Thus, $EMV(A_2)$ represents the expected payoff from selecting
		alternative $A_2$.
		
		
	\end{multicols}
	
	\setlength{\columnseprule}{0pt}
	
	\textbf{Step 4: Apply the Bayes decision criterion}
	
	Compare the two expected monetary values:
	
	$$
	A^*_{\mathrm{Bayes}}
	=
	\arg\max_i EMV(A_i).
	$$
	
	Therefore, choose the alternative with the highest Expected Monetary
	Value.

	\subsection{Contextualized 2x2 Problem - Coffee shop}\label{sec:c4-intro-coffee}

	
	A small coffee shop is considering whether to introduce a new dessert line
	or keep its current menu. The manager estimates that there is a $60\%$
	probability of high customer traffic during the promotional weekend and a
	$40\%$ probability of low customer traffic. All payoffs are expressed in thousands of dollars. What would a perfectly logical decision-maker choose using the Expected
	Monetary Value criterion? \vspace{-2mm}
	\begin{center}
		\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
		\begin{tabular}{lcc}
			\toprule
			Alternative
			& \begin{tabular}{c}High traffic $S_1$ \\ $P(S_1)=0.60$\end{tabular}
			& \begin{tabular}{c}Low traffic $S_2$ \\ $P(S_2)=0.40$\end{tabular}
			\\
			\midrule
			Launch dessert line ($A_1$) & 42 & 8 \\
			Keep current menu ($A_2$) & 28 & 20 \\
			\bottomrule
		\end{tabular}
	\end{center} \vspace{-5mm}
	
	% ============================================================
	% SOLUTION 2x2
	% ============================================================
	
	\subsubsection{Solution}
	
	\textbf{Step 1: Identify the alternatives, states of nature, and probabilities}
	
	Alternatives:
	$A_1$ = Launch dessert line,
	$A_2$ = Keep current menu
	
	States of nature:
	$S_1$ = High traffic,
	$S_2$ = Low traffic
	
	Probabilities:
	$
	P(S_1)=0.60,
	P(S_2)=0.40
	$ \vspace{-3mm}
	
	\noindent
	\setlength{\columnseprule}{0.4pt} 
	\begin{multicols}{2}
		\vspace{0pt}
		
		\textbf{Step 2: Calculate the EMV of $A_1$}
		
		For the Launch dessert line alternative:
		\[
		EMV(A_1)
		=
		P(S_1)(42)
		+
		P(S_2)(8)
		\]
		\[
		=
		(0.60)(42)
		+
		(0.40)(8)
		\]
		\[
		=
		25.2+3.2
		=
		28.4.
		\]
		
		Thus, the expected payoff from launching the dessert line is
		$28.4$ thousand dollars.
		
		\columnbreak
		
		\textbf{Step 3: Calculate the EMV of $A_2$}
		
		For the Keep current menu alternative:
		\[
		EMV(A_2)
		=
		P(S_1)(28)
		+
		P(S_2)(20)
		\]
		\[
		=
		(0.60)(28)
		+
		(0.40)(20)
		\]
		\[
		=
		16.8+8
		=
		24.8.
		\]
		
		Thus, the expected payoff from keeping the current menu is
		$24.8$ thousand dollars.
		
	\end{multicols}
	
	\setlength{\columnseprule}{0pt}
	
	\newpage
	
	\textbf{Step 4: Apply the Bayes decision criterion}
	
	Compare the two expected monetary values:
	$$
	EMV(A_1)=28.4,
	\qquad
	EMV(A_2)=24.8.
	$$
	$$
	A^*_{\mathrm{Bayes}}
	=
	\arg\max_i EMV(A_i)
	=
	A_1.
	$$
	
	Thus, the Bayes criterion recommends launching the dessert line because
	it has the highest Expected Monetary Value, with an expected payoff of
	$28.4$ thousand dollars.
