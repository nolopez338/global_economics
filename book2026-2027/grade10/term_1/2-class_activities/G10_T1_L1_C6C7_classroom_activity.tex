		\section*{Learning Criterion}
		
		\textbf{C6.}
		Calculates Expected Monetary Value using stated or estimated probabilities and applies the Bayes decision criterion to select the alternative with the highest expected payoff.
		
		\textbf{C7.}
		Uses subjective probabilities and sensitivity analysis to examine how changes in probability assumptions affect the preferred decision.
		\vspace{-5mm}

		% SUMMARY

	\section{C6 \& C7 Expected Monetary Value}
	
	When probabilities can be assigned to the possible states of nature,
	the Expected Monetary Value (EMV) criterion compares alternatives by
	combining each possible payoff with the probability of the state in
	which that payoff occurs.
	
	\begin{center}
		\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
		\small
		\begin{tabular}{p{0.30\columnwidth}p{0.6\columnwidth}}
			\toprule
			\textbf{Focus}
			& Expected payoff \\
			\textbf{First step}
			& Calculate the EMV of each alternative \\
			\textbf{Second step}
			& Choose the highest EMV \\
			\bottomrule
		\end{tabular}
	\end{center}
	
	Let $P(S_j)$ represent the probability that state of nature $S_j$
	occurs. The probabilities must satisfy
	$$
	\boxed{
		\sum_{j=1}^{n} P(S_j)=1
	}
	$$
	
	For each alternative, calculate its Expected Monetary Value:
	$$
	\boxed{
		EMV(A_i)
		=
		\sum_{j=1}^{n}
		P(S_j)P_{ay}(A_i,S_j)
	}
	$$
	
	For two states of nature, this becomes
	$$
	\boxed{
		EMV(A_i)
		=
		P(S_1)P_{ay}(A_i,S_1)
		+
		P(S_2)P_{ay}(A_i,S_2)
	}
	$$
	
	Finally, choose the alternative with the highest expected monetary
	value:
	$$
	\boxed{
		A^*_{\mathrm{Bayes}}
		=
		\arg\max_i EMV(A_i)
	}
	$$
	
	\subsection*{Sensitivity Analysis}
	
	When the probabilities of the states are subjective, the preferred
	alternative may depend on the probability assigned to each state.
	Sensitivity analysis examines how the decision changes when these
	probabilities change.
	For two states of nature, let $p=P(S_1)$. Since the probabilities must
	sum to one,
	$$
	P(S_1)=p,
	\qquad
	P(S_2)=1-p,
	\qquad
	0\leq p\leq1.
	$$
	
	The EMV of each alternative can then be written as a function of $p$:
	$$
	\boxed{
		EMV(A_i)
		=
		pP_{ay}(A_i,S_1)
		+
		(1-p)P_{ay}(A_i,S_2)
	}
	$$
	
	A threshold probability occurs when two alternatives have equal
	expected monetary value:
	$$
	\boxed{
		EMV(A_i)=EMV(A_k)
	}
	$$
	
	The threshold divides the feasible probability range into regions in
	which different alternatives have the highest EMV. Therefore, the
	sensitivity analysis determines which alternative is preferred for each
	range of values of $p$ and identifies any probability at which the
	decision-maker is indifferent between alternatives.

	\section{Solved Example: Clothing Store}
		
		A local clothing store is deciding whether to launch a new advertising
		campaign before the holiday season or continue with its current
		marketing strategy. The manager estimates the probabilities of strong
		and weak sales based on previous holiday seasons.
		$
		S_1:\text{Strong Sales}
		,
		S_2:\text{Weak Sales}
		$
		\begin{center}
			\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
			\small
			\begin{tabular}{lr@{\qquad}cc}
				\cmidrule{1-4}
				Alternative & & $S_1$ & $S_2$ \\
				& $P(S_j)$: & 60\% & 40\% \\
				\cmidrule{1-4}
				\multicolumn{2}{l}{Launch new campaign ($A_1$)} & 55 & 5 \\
				\multicolumn{2}{l}{Keep current strategy ($A_2$)} & 35 & 22 \\
				\cmidrule{1-4}
			\end{tabular}
		\end{center}
		
		All payoffs are expressed in thousands of dollars. What decision should
		the manager make using the Expected Monetary Value criterion?\vspace{-4mm}
		
		\subsection{Solution}
		
		\textbf{Step 0: Identify the probabilities}
		\begin{center}
			\begin{minipage}[c]{0.2\textwidth}
				The probabilities of the states of nature are
			\end{minipage}
			\hfill
			\begin{minipage}[c]{0.28\textwidth}
				\centering
				$P(S_1)=0.6
				\qquad
				P(S_2)=0.4$
			\end{minipage}
		\end{center}
		\begin{center}
			\begin{minipage}[c]{0.2\textwidth}
				Check they sum to $1$:
			\end{minipage}
			\hfill
			\begin{minipage}[c]{0.28\textwidth}
				\centering
				$
				P(S_1)+P(S_2)
				=
				0.6+0.4
				=
				1
				$
			\end{minipage}
		\end{center}\vspace{-3mm}
		
		\textbf{Step 1.1: Calculate the EMV of $A_1$}
		
		For alternative $A_1$, multiply each payoff by the probability of its
		corresponding state and add the results:
		\[
		\begin{aligned}
			EMV(A_1)
			&=
			P(S_1)P_{ay}(A_1,S_1)
			+
			P(S_2)P_{ay}(A_1,S_2)
			\\
			&=
			(0.6)(55)+(0.4)(5)
			\\
			&=
			33+2 \quad  = \quad 
			35
		\end{aligned} 
		\]
		Thus, the expected monetary value of launching the new campaign is
		$\$35,000$.
	
		
		\textbf{Step 1.2: Calculate the EMV of $A_2$}
		
		For alternative $A_2$:
		\[
		\begin{aligned}
			EMV(A_2)
			&=
			P(S_1)P_{ay}(A_2,S_1)
			+
			P(S_2)P_{ay}(A_2,S_2)
			\\
			&=
			(0.6)(35)+(0.4)(22)
			\\
			&=
			21+8.8
			\quad  = \quad 
			29.8
		\end{aligned}
		\]\vspace{-3mm}
		
		Thus, the expected monetary value of keeping the current strategy is
		$\$29,800$.
		
		\textbf{Step 2: Apply the Bayes decision criterion}
		
		Compare the two expected monetary values:
		\[
		R^*
		=
		\max\{35,29.8\}
		=
		35
		\] \vspace{-4mm}
		\[
		A^*_{\mathrm{Bayes}}
		=
		\arg\max_i EMV(A_i)
		=
		A_1
		\]
		
		The Bayes decision criterion selects the new advertising campaign
		because it has the higher Expected Monetary Value:
		$\$35,000$ compared with $\$29,800$.

	\section{Example: Clothing Store}
	A local clothing store must choose between launching a new advertising campaign ($A_1$) or keeping its current marketing strategy ($A_2$) for the holiday season. Sales may be strong ($S_1$) or weak ($S_2$). Let $p$ be the subjective probability of strong sales, so weak sales have probability $1-p$. Payoffs are in thousands of dollars. Using the Expected Value criterion, determine how the preferred alternative changes with $p$ and identify the threshold probability at which the manager is indifferent.
	
	\vspace{-3mm}
	\begin{center}
		\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
		\small
		\begin{tabular}{lr@{\qquad}cc}
			\cmidrule{1-4}
			Alternative & & $S_1$ & $S_2$ \\
			& $P(S_j)$: & $p$ & $1-p$ \\
			\cmidrule{1-4}
			\multicolumn{2}{l}{Launch new campaign ($A_1$)} & 55 & 5 \\
			\multicolumn{2}{l}{Keep current strategy ($A_2$)} & 45 & 25 \\
			\cmidrule{1-4}
		\end{tabular}
	\end{center}
	
	\vspace{-4mm}
	
	\subsection*{Step 1.1: Calculate the expected value of $A_1$}
	
	For the Launch new campaign alternative:
	\[
	\begin{aligned}
		EV(A_1)
		&=p(55)+(1-p)(5)\\
		&=55p+5-5p = 50p+5
	\end{aligned}
	\]
	
	Thus, the expected payoff from launching the new campaign is
	$50p+5$ thousand dollars.
	
	\subsection*{Step 1.2: Calculate the expected value of $A_2$}
	
	For the Keep current strategy alternative:
	\[
	\begin{aligned}
		EV(A_2)
		&=p(45)+(1-p)(25)\\
		&=45p+25-25p = 20p+25
	\end{aligned}
	\]
	
	Thus, the expected payoff from keeping the current strategy is
	$20p+25$ thousand dollars.
	
	\subsection*{Step 2: Find the threshold probability}
	
	The threshold occurs when the two alternatives have the same expected
	value:
	\[
	EV(A_1)=EV(A_2)
	\]
	\[
	50p+5=20p+25
	\quad\longrightarrow\quad
	30p=20
	\quad\longrightarrow\quad
	p=\frac{2}{3}
	\]
	
	\subsection*{Step 3: Perform the sensitivity analysis}
	
	Compare the expected values on each side of the threshold.
	\[
	\begin{aligned}
		p<\frac{2}{3}
		&\quad\Rightarrow\quad
		EV(A_1)<EV(A_2)
		\quad\Rightarrow\quad
		\text{choose } A_2
		\\[2mm]
		p>\frac{2}{3}
		&\quad\Rightarrow\quad
		EV(A_1)>EV(A_2)
		\quad\Rightarrow\quad
		\text{choose } A_1
		\\[2mm]
		p=\frac{2}{3}
		&\quad\Rightarrow\quad
		EV(A_1)=EV(A_2)
		\quad\Rightarrow\quad
		\text{Indifferent}
	\end{aligned}
	\]
	
	\[
	\boxed{
		A^*_{\mathrm{Bayes}} =
		\begin{cases}
			A_2 & 0\leq p<\frac{2}{3}\\
			\text{Indifferent} & p=\frac{2}{3}\\
			A_1 & \frac{2}{3}<p\leq1
	\end{cases}}
	\]
