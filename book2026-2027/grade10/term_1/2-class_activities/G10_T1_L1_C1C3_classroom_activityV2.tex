	A decision under uncertainty selects an alternative \(A_i\) before a state
	\(S_j\) is known. Each pair \((A_i,S_j)\) determines a payoff
	\[
	P(A_i,S_j)=R(A_i,S_j)-C(A_i,S_j),
	\]
	placed in row \(A_i\), column \(S_j\). The objective is to construct the
	complete payoff table.
	
	Revenue and cost are
	\[
	R=q(A_i,S_j)\big[r(A_i)+r(S_j)\big]+f_r(A_i)+f_r(S_j),
	\]
	\[
	C=q(A_i,S_j)\big[c(A_i)+c(S_j)\big]+f_c(A_i)+f_c(S_j),
	\]
	\[
	P(A_i,S_j)=q(A_i,S_j)\big[r(A_i)+r(S_j)-c(A_i)-c(S_j) \big]
	+f_r(A_i)+f_r(S_j)-f_c(A_i)-f_c(S_j).
	\]
	
	Variable components are per-unit rates multiplied by \(q(A_i,S_j)\); fixed components are not. \(A_i\), \(S_j\), and \((A_i,S_j)\) indicate dependence on the alternative, state, or both. Revenue rates are gross revenue; costs are calculated separately. Missing components are zero; directly given
	\(R\) or \(C\) require no decomposition. 

	\section*{Context Descriptions}
	\vspace{-3mm}

	For each problem, \textbf{(C1)} \textit{identify the decision alternatives, the states of nature, the uncertain event, and the consequence}. Then \textbf{(C3)} \textit{calculate revenue, cost, and profit for every alternative--state pair and construct the payoff table}.
	
	\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=0.22in]
\item \textbf{Direct case.} A planetarium must select an immersive show or an astronomy workshop for its holiday programme before evening cloud cover is known. Evenings may be clear or cloudy, affecting interest in indoor astronomy activities. All amounts are total revenue and total cost, in thousand USD. For the immersive show, revenue and cost are 64 and 18 in clear evenings, and 35 and 22 in cloudy evenings. For the astronomy workshop, revenue and cost are 53 and 24 in clear evenings, and 46 and 23 in cloudy evenings. These totals include all revenue and costs for the programme.

\item \textbf{One-component problem.} An equipment rental firm must choose to offer lighting rigs or sound rigs before the external festival calendar is confirmed. The calendar may be full or reduced by organiser cancellations. Quantities are measured in rig-weeks: one rig rented for one week. Under a full and a reduced calendar, respectively, lighting rentals total 7 and 3 rig-weeks, and sound rentals total 6 and 2 rig-weeks. Gross rental revenue is 14 thousand USD per rig-week for lighting rigs and 18 for sound rigs. Total operating cost per rig-week, covering equipment hire, setup and staffing, is 6 thousand USD with a full calendar and 9 with a reduced calendar, for either rig type. There are no fixed amounts or other components.

\newpage
\noindent\textbf{Context Descriptions (continued)}
\item \textbf{Two-component problem.} A language school must choose a group course or a small-group course before overseas arrivals are known. Arrivals may be many or few. Enrolments, in hundreds of students, are 8 and 5 for the group course and 7 and 4 for the small-group course under many and few arrivals, respectively. Gross tuition rates are 9 and 12 thousand USD per hundred students for the two formats. An external education fund adds gross teaching payments of 4 thousand USD per hundred students with many arrivals and 2 with few arrivals, for either format. Teaching and materials cost 5 and 7 thousand USD per hundred students for the two formats. Fixed room-booking costs, paid once for the whole course, are 8 thousand USD with many arrivals and 14 with few arrivals, for either format. All other components are zero.

\item \textbf{Three-component problem.} An indoor climbing centre must choose bouldering sessions or rope-climbing sessions before holiday weather is known. The period may be wet or dry. Quantities, in hundreds of participant-visits, are 9 and 4 for bouldering and 11 and 7 for rope climbing under wet and dry weather, respectively. Gross session fees are 10 and 13 thousand USD per hundred participant-visits for the two types. A tourism partner adds gross referral payments of 4 and 1 thousand USD per hundred participant-visits in wet and dry weather, for either type. Fixed sponsorship revenue, received once, is 18 thousand USD for bouldering and 27 for rope climbing. Instruction and equipment costs are 6 and 7 thousand USD per hundred participant-visits for the two types. Weather-related cleaning and energy add costs of 2 and 4 thousand USD per hundred participant-visits in wet and dry weather. Fixed building-operation costs are 12 and 17 thousand USD in wet and dry weather, paid once. Both weather-based costs apply to either type; all other components are zero.

\end{enumerate}
