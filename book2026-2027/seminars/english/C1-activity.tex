	\section*{Our Goal}
	
	Innovation begins by noticing something that could be improved. First, you will generate ideas from your classroom experience. Then, as homework, you will observe your environment and identify situations that may represent a need, problem, discomfort, or opportunity for improvement.
	
	At this stage, do not design a solution. Your goal is to identify situations worth investigating.
	
	\section*{In-Class Activity: Generate Ideas}
	
	Think about your experience at school and in the classroom. Consider situations that:
	
	\begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.43\linewidth}@{\hspace{0.04\linewidth}}p{0.53\linewidth}@{}}
		\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=0.5cm,itemsep=0pt]
			\item Make an activity difficult or inefficient.
			\item Cause discomfort or frustration.
			\item Waste time, materials, or resources.
		\end{itemize}
		&
		\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=0.5cm,itemsep=0pt]
			\item Create difficulties for students, teachers, or others.
			\item Could be organized or improved.
			\item Could be done differently.
		\end{itemize}
	\end{tabular}

	\section*{Observe Your Environment}
	
	During your normal day, pay attention to your surroundings. You may observe:
	
	\begin{multicols}{2}
		\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=0.6cm,itemsep=0pt]
			\item Your school
			\item Your home
			\item Your neighborhood
			\item Public spaces
			\item Transportation
			\item Stores or businesses
			\item Recreational spaces
			\item Situations involving technology
			\item Other community environments.
		\end{itemize}
	\end{multicols}
	
	Look for situations in which people experience a difficulty, need, discomfort, inefficiency, or unmet opportunity. \vspace{6mm}
	
	\begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.32\linewidth}p{0.63\linewidth}@{}}
		Do not start by asking:
		&
		\textit{``What should I build?''}
		\\[0.15cm]
		&\\
		Instead, ask:
		&
		\textit{``What is happening, and what could be improved?''}
	\end{tabular}

	\section*{What to Record}
	
	For each opportunity, record:
	
	\begin{center}
		\small
		\begin{tabular}{|L{0.20\textwidth}|L{0.26\textwidth}|L{0.38\textwidth}|}
			\hline
			\textbf{Element} & \textbf{What to write} & \textbf{Example} \\
			\hline
			\textbf{Location} & Where? & School cafeteria \\
			\hline
			\textbf{Situation} & What is happening? & Students wait in a long line. \\
			\hline
			\textbf{Need / Problem / Discomfort} & What needs improvement? & Students spend time waiting. \\
			\hline
			\textbf{Who is affected?} & Who experiences it? & Students and cafeteria staff. \\
			\hline
			\textbf{Evidence} & What did you observe? & The line became very long. \\
			\hline
			\textbf{Why does it matter?} & Why is it worth investigating? & Students have less time to eat. \\
			\hline
			\textbf{Possible solution} & Do you already have one? & Leave open for now. \\
			\hline
		\end{tabular}
	\end{center}
	
	\textbf{Important:} First understand the situation. Do not decide on the solution too early.
