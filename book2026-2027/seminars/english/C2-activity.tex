	\section*{Our Goal}
	
	You have already identified several possible opportunities for improvement. Now, your team will select one opportunity and investigate what is actually happening in the context where it occurs.
	
	The goal is not to confirm that your original idea is correct. The goal is to understand the situation better through direct and systematic observation.
	
	At this stage, pay attention to what people do, what happens around them, how the environment affects the situation, and whether certain patterns appear repeatedly.

	\textbf{Important:} Do not try to prove that the opportunity is real. Observe the situation openly and record what actually happens.
	
	\section*{What to Observe}
	
	When observing the selected context, pay attention to different aspects of the situation:
	
	\begin{multicols}{2}
		\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=0.6cm,itemsep=0pt]
			\item People
			\item Actions and behaviors
			\item Interactions
			\item Physical spaces
			\item Objects and materials
			\item Use of resources
			\item Movement and routines
			\item Waiting or interruptions
			\item Difficulties or obstacles
			\item Repeated situations
		\end{itemize}
	\end{multicols}
	
	Look for what happens before, during, and after the situation you are investigating. \\
	
	
	\textbf{Rule:} Record observations as evidence first. Interpretations can be added separately after you have collected the evidence.

	\section*{What to Record}
	
	For each observation, record enough information for another person to understand what happened.
	
	\begin{center}
		\small
		\begin{tabular}{|L{0.17\textwidth}|L{0.24\textwidth}|L{0.46\textwidth}|}
			\hline
			\textbf{Element} & \textbf{What to write} & \textbf{Example} \\
			\hline
			\textbf{Time} & When did it happen? 
			& 8:05 a.m., beginning of Mathematics class (Sept. 10, 2026) \\
			\hline
			\textbf{Location} & Where did it happen? 
			& Grade 10 classroom, immediately after the bell \\
			\hline
			\textbf{People involved} & Who was involved? 
			& 28 students and 1 teacher \\
			\hline
			\textbf{Action} & What happened? 
			& After the bell, the teacher stopped the planned lesson to determine which students were present and record attendance. \\
			\hline
			\textbf{Evidence} & What did you directly see or hear? 
			& The teacher spent approximately 5 minutes taking attendance before the lesson continued. In a 50-minute class, this represents 10\% of the available class time. \\
			\hline
			\textbf{Context} & What else was happening? 
			& Students arrived at slightly different times. The teacher had to look between the class list and the students in the room. One student arrived after attendance had already been recorded and the teacher had to update the record. \\
			\hline
			\textbf{Pattern / Question} & What might be worth observing again? 
			& Attendance takes place during the transition between students entering the classroom and the beginning of the lesson. How much class time is spent on attendance, and which parts of the process require the most teacher attention? \\
			\hline
		\end{tabular}
	\end{center}
	
	\vspace{0.2cm}
	
	\textbf{Important:} A useful observation is specific enough that someone else could understand what happened without having been there.
