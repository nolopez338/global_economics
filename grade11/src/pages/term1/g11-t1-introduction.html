Models for uncertain phenomena

  1. Introduction

    Mathematical models describe the world.

    Probability distributions are mathematical models for different kinds of uncertain phenomena. Because real-world situations have different structures, they naturally lead us to different distributions.

    A student submits one song to the school dance playlist. Based on past selections, it has probability p = 0.40 of being chosen. Let X = 1 if it is chosen and X = 0 otherwise.

    One songChosen or not chosenp = 0.40

    Bernoulli distribution

    A discrete model for one trial with two possible outcomes.

    • X ∈ {0, 1}
    • P(X = 1) = p = 0.40
    • P(X = 0) = 1 − p = 0.60

    PMF: P(X = x) = px(1 − p)1−x,  x ∈ {0,1}

    Binomial distribution

    A discrete model in which X counts successes in n trials.

    • X ∈ {0, 1, …, 10}
    • 10 independent, comparable shots
    • Two outcomes and constant p = 0.70

    P(X = k) = C(n,k)pk(1 − p)n−k

    A discrete model for independent attempts with constant p = 0.25.

    • X is the number of matches up to the first win.
    • X ∈ {1, 2, 3, …}; there is no fixed maximum.

    P(X = k) = (1 − p)k−1p,  k = 1,2,3,…

    Count messagesOne 10-minute intervalExpected count λ = 4

    Poisson distribution

    A discrete model counting independent events over a fixed exposure at a stable rate.

    • λ = 4 is the expected message count per ten minutes.
    • X ∈ {0, 1, 2, …}

    PMF: P(X = k) = e−λλk/k!,  k = 0,1,2,…

    A music app samples a random timestamp X from a four-minute track, from 0 to 4 minutes. Every equal-length interval in the track has the same chance of being selected.

    Continuous time0 ≤ X ≤ 4 minNo moment favoured

    Uniform distribution

    A continuous model with bounded support [a,b] and constant density.

    • Here, a = 0 and b = 4 minutes.
    • Equal-length intervals have equal probabilities.
    • Probabilities are areas under the PDF.

    f(x) = 1/4 for 0 ≤ x ≤ 4, and 0 otherwise

  13. Where does a highlight begin?

    A ten-second sports clip chooses a continuous start time X between 0 and 10 seconds, with later moments steadily more likely. Its selection density is f(x) = x/50.

    Continuous time0 ≤ X ≤ 10 sDensity increases linearly

    Linear distribution

    A continuous bounded model whose PDF changes linearly with x.

    • Here, f(x) = x/50 for 0 ≤ x ≤ 10, and 0 otherwise.
    • The PDF is nonnegative and the triangular area is 1.
    • Probabilities are areas, not individual heights.

    ∫010 x/50 dx = 1

    Traffic means the trip takes at least 20 minutes and at most 40 minutes. Based on similar evenings, 28 minutes is the single most likely journey time.

    Minimum a = 20 minMode c = 28 minMaximum b = 40 min

    Triangular distribution

    A continuous model with minimum a, mode c, and maximum b.

    • Here, a = 20, c = 28, and b = 40 minutes.
    • Density rises linearly to c, then falls linearly to zero at b.
    • Probability is area under the PDF.

    For a large fictional Grade 11 group, model height X as continuous and symmetric around μ = 170 cm, with standard deviation σ = 7 cm. Heights farther from 170 cm become progressively less common.

    Continuous heightCentre μ = 170 cmSpread σ = 7 cm

    Normal distribution

    A continuous model with a symmetric, bell-shaped density.

    • μ = 170 cm controls the centre.
    • σ = 7 cm controls the spread.
    • Probabilities are areas under the curve.
    • The domain extends without bound in both directions.

  18. Summary

    Match the model to the situation

    TypeDistributionTypical situation
    DiscreteBernoulliOne situation with two possible outcomes.
    DiscreteBinomialCounting how many times an outcome occurs across a fixed number of comparable cases.
    DiscreteGeometricCounting how many attempts are required until the first desired outcome.
    DiscretePoissonCounting how many separate events occur during a fixed period or exposure.
    ContinuousUniformA bounded quantity for which no part of the possible range is expected more often than another.
    ContinuousTriangularA bounded quantity with a practical minimum, maximum, and one most common value.
    ContinuousLinearA bounded quantity whose observations become steadily more or less common across its range.
    ContinuousNormalValues concentrated around a typical value, with increasingly uncommon observations farther from it on either side.
