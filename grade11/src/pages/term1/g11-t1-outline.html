From continuous models to measured risk

Build, test, compare, and integrate probability models in one coherent investigation.

  1. Step 1

    Choose a distribution and find interval probabilities

    C1 · Printing Orders

    Count. Orders arrive independently, one at a time, at a constant rate of 2 per hour. The number in one hour is \(\operatorname{Poisson}(2)\), with discrete support \(\{0, 1, 2, \ldots\}\).

    Measurement. Poster completion time \(Y\) has continuous support \([10, 30]\) minutes. Its density rises and falls along straight lines with a peak at 18, so use \(\operatorname{Tri}(10, 30, 18)\).

    The density \(f(y)\) is a height in \(\mathrm{min}^{-1}\). The cumulative probability \(F(y) = P(Y \le y)\) is the area to the left. At the mode, \(F(18) = \frac{1}{2} \times 8 \times \frac{2}{20} = 0.40\), so 40% of poster orders finish within 18 minutes.

    A general shape can fit several models. For example, a steadily decreasing density could be linear or curved.

    C2 · Market-stall Setup

    Interval probability is area under the density. For a uniform model on \([a, b]\), multiply the interval length within the support by the rectangle’s height \(\frac{1}{b-a}\). For a triangular model, set \(\frac{1}{2} \times (b-a) \times H = 1\) to get peak height \(H = \frac{2}{b-a}\).

    Criterion C1

    Characterises discrete and continuous random variables, identifies suitable distributions and supports, and interprets density and cumulative probability in context.

    Criterion C2

    Applies uniform and triangular models and constant or piecewise-linear densities to interval probabilities using geometric areas.

  2. Step 2

    Find probabilities in cycles and waiting times

    C3 · A Repeating Inspection Cycle

    An alarm is equally likely to occur anywhere in a 60-minute cycle. For uniform timing or direction around a circle, divide the selected arc length by the full circumference, or use the equivalent fraction of time or angle.

    C4 · Exponential waits in both directions

    Use an exponential model for the wait to the next event in a constant-rate Poisson process. With time \(t\) in minutes and rate \(\lambda\) per minute, \(P(T > t) = e^{-\lambda t}\).

    Charging-space Availability · Rate to probability. At 12 openings per hour, \(\lambda = \frac{12}{60} = \frac{1}{5}\) per minute. For a wait under 3 minutes or over 8 minutes, add the disjoint tails.

    \[\begin{aligned} &P(T \lt 3 \text{ or } T > 8) \\ &\quad = (1 - e^{-3/5}) + e^{-8/5} \\ &\quad \approx 0.6531 \end{aligned}\]

    Electronic Receipt Rates · Probability to rate. In the morning, \(P(T > 10) = 0.40\). Set \(e^{-10\lambda} = 0.40\) and take natural logs. Then \(\lambda = -\frac{\ln(0.40)}{10}\) receipts per minute, or \(60\lambda \approx \mathbf{5.498}\) receipts per hour.

    Criterion C3

    Uses circular geometry and arc proportions to calculate probabilities for uniform timing and directions around a circle.

    Criterion C4

    Applies exponential models to constant-rate waiting times, infers event rates, and assesses model suitability.

  3. Step 3

    Compare theoretical and sample results

    C5 · Two Labelling Stations

    Both labelling times are on \([3, 15]\) minutes. Station A has a triangular density rising to a mode of 15. Station B has a uniform density.

    C6 · File Transfers

    For \(4 \le X \lt 12\) seconds, add the counts from \([4, 8)\) and \([8, 12)\) to get \(9 + 5 = 14\). The four classes have \(3 + 9 + 5 + 3 = 20\) observations, giving an empirical probability of \(\frac{14}{20} = 0.70\).

    Against the model probability 0.75, the absolute error is \(\lvert 0.70 - 0.75 \rvert = \mathbf{0.05}\). The percentage error is \(\frac{0.05}{0.75} \times 100\% \approx \mathbf{6.67\%}\). Counts can come from raw data, grouped frequencies, or a histogram.

    C7 · Courier Routes

    With \(n = 6\), the midpoint estimate of the sample mean is

    \[\begin{aligned} \bar{x} &= \frac{2 \times 10 + 3 \times 14 + 18}{6} \\ &= \frac{40}{3} \approx \mathbf{13.333}\,\mathrm{min}. \end{aligned}\]

    Weight each squared deviation by its count. The sum is

    \[\begin{aligned} &2(10 - \bar{x})^2 + 3(14 - \bar{x})^2 \\ &\quad + (18 - \bar{x})^2 = \frac{136}{3}\,\mathrm{min}^2. \end{aligned}\]

    Divide by \(n - 1 = 5\) to estimate \(s^2 = \frac{136}{15} \approx \mathbf{9.067}\,\mathrm{min}^2\). Take its square root for \(s \approx \mathbf{3.011}\,\mathrm{min}\).

    Using the unrounded estimates, the mean differs from 14 min by 0.667 min (4.76%) and the variance differs from 12 \(\mathrm{min}^2\) by 2.933 \(\mathrm{min}^2\) (24.44%). In each case, divide the absolute error by the theoretical value and multiply by 100%.

    Criterion C5

    Calculates and interprets the mean, variance, and standard deviation of continuous distributions using standard formulas.

    Criterion C6

    Estimates interval probabilities using sample relative frequencies and compares them with theoretical probabilities.

    Criterion C7

    Calculates sample means, variances, and standard deviations for different sample sizes and data representations and compares them with theory.

  4. Step 4

    Read Student’s \(t\) tables and compare models

    C8 · From cutoffs to areas and back

    For the supplied model \(X = m + aT\), use \(\mathrm{df} = n - 1\). The \(t\) table pairs a positive critical value with its right-tail area. By symmetry, the negative critical value has the same area to its left.

    Direct · Retail Revenue Alerts. For \(n = 7\), \(m = 2\) and \(a = 4\), standardise with \(c = \frac{x - m}{a}\). The cutoffs give \(\frac{-5.772 - 2}{4} = -1.943\) and \(\frac{7.76 - 2}{4} = 1.440\). Row 6 gives tail areas 0.05 and 0.10.

    Inverse · Dispatch Time Alerts. With \(n = 16\), use row 15. The 0.01 lower tail gives \(c_L = -2.602\) by symmetry, and the 0.10 upper tail gives \(c_U = 1.341\). Back-transform with \(x = m + ac\).

    \[\begin{aligned} L &= -2 + 6(-2.602) \\ &\approx -17.61\,\mathrm{min} \\[.4em] U &= -2 + 6(1.341) \\ &\approx 6.05\,\mathrm{min} \end{aligned}\]

    C9 · Sales Revenue Shortfalls

    Compare \(P(X \lt -11.165)\) for \(X_T = -2 + 5T\) and \(X_N = -2 + 5Z\), with \(T\) from the \(t\) model (\(\mathrm{df} = 9\)) and \(Z\) standard normal. Both use the supplied location \(-2\) and the stated approximate standard deviation of 5 as the scale, in thousands of dollars.

    For inverse comparisons, look up each model’s critical values at the same target areas, then back-transform to the original units before calculating differences. For an interval, compare lower endpoints and upper endpoints separately.

    Criterion C8

    Uses Student’s t-distribution tables, degrees of freedom, and one-tailed or two-tailed areas to determine critical values, central probabilities, and tail probabilities or probability ranges.

    Criterion C9

  5. Step 5

    Integrate the methods in a risk investigation

    Ask whether a 60% bond / 40% equity portfolio meets a return target without excessive volatility. Select continuous return models, simulate outcomes, compare sample evidence with theoretical mean and variance, and use justified \(z\)/\(t\) reasoning. Report assumptions, limitations, and a recommendation supported by both probability and portfolio risk.

    Criterion C10

    Integrates continuous probability modelling, simulation, sampling analysis, and portfolio-risk measures to investigate an economic or financial question, justifying the selected methods and communicating results, assumptions, limitations, and evidence-based conclusions.
