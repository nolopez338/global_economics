  1. C1 · Choosing a distribution

    Identify the probability distribution from the context.

    Choosing a probability distribution
    TypeDistributionTypical situation
    DiscreteBernoulliOne trial with two possible outcomes.
    DiscreteBinomialCounting successes in a fixed number of independent trials with the same probability of success.
    DiscreteGeometricCounting the trials needed to get the first success, including the successful trial.
    DiscretePoissonCounting events in a fixed interval of time, space, or exposure when events occur independently at a constant average rate.
    ContinuousUniformA bounded measurement for which equal-length intervals within the range are equally likely.
    ContinuousTriangularA bounded measurement with a practical minimum, maximum, and one most likely value.
    ContinuousLinearA bounded measurement whose density rises or falls at a constant rate across the range.
    ContinuousNormalMeasurements clustered around a central value, with observations becoming less common farther away on either side.

    Criterion C1

    Characterises discrete and continuous random variables, identifies suitable distributions and supports, and interprets density and cumulative probability in context.
