Grade 11 · Term 1 · Interactive outline

From continuous models to measured risk

Build, test, compare, and integrate probability models in one coherent investigation.

  1. Step 1

    Describe continuous probability models

    Distinguish countable discrete outcomes from measurements on an interval. State the support, read density from the PDF, and use the CDF as accumulated area. Normalize uniform, triangular, and piecewise-linear PDFs so total area is 1; then calculate interval probabilities with rectangles, triangles, or trapezoids.

    Criterion C1

    Characterises continuous random variables by distinguishing them from discrete variables and interpreting their probability density function, cumulative distribution function, and support in economic contexts.

    Criterion C2

    Applies uniform and triangular continuous distributions with constant or piecewise-linear probability density functions to calculate interval probabilities using geometric areas.

  2. Step 2

    Extend the family of continuous models

    For an arc-based PDF, normalize and find probability from circular-sector or segment area. For constant-rate waiting time, use exponential density f(t)=λe−λt and survival probability P(T>t)=e−λt. Always test whether a stable rate and memorylessness fit the situation.

    Criterion C3

    Applies circular-arc probability density functions to calculate interval probabilities using the geometry of circles and circular segments.

    Criterion C4

    Applies the exponential distribution to constant-rate waiting-time situations and assesses its suitability.

  3. Step 3

    Summarize distributions and investigate sampling

    Calculate theoretical location and spread with μ=E(X) and Var(X)=E(X²)−μ². Generate samples, estimate an event by its relative frequency, and compare it with theory. Recalculate the sample mean and unbiased variance as n grows; stabilization is expected, not exact agreement.

    Criterion C5

    Calculates and interprets the mean and variance of continuous distributions using standard formulas and functions.

    Criterion C6

    Estimates interval probabilities using relative frequencies from generated samples and compares them with theoretical probabilities.

    Criterion C7

    Calculates sample means and variances for different sample sizes and compares them with the theoretical mean and variance.

  4. Step 4

    Use Student’s t and choose between t and z

    Set df=n−1, identify whether the table area is one-tailed, two-tailed, central, or total tail area, and interpret the critical value. Student’s t has heavier tails than z for finite df and approaches z as df grows. Use z when population σ is known; use t when σ is unknown and estimated by sample s, subject to sampling assumptions.

    Criterion C8

    Uses Student’s t-distribution tables, degrees of freedom, and one-tailed or two-tailed areas to determine critical values, central probabilities, and tail probabilities or probability ranges.

    Criterion C9

    Calculates and compares critical values and probability areas from the standard normal and Student’s t-distributions, selecting and justifying the appropriate distribution according to whether the population standard deviation is known or estimated from the sample.

  5. Step 5

    Integrate the methods in a risk investigation

    Ask whether a 60% bond / 40% equity portfolio meets a return target without excessive volatility. Select continuous return models, simulate outcomes, compare sample evidence with theoretical mean and variance, and use justified z/t reasoning. Report assumptions, limitations, and a recommendation supported by both probability and portfolio risk.

    Criterion C10

    Integrates continuous probability modelling, simulation, sampling analysis, and portfolio-risk measures to investigate an economic or financial question, justifying the selected methods and communicating results, assumptions, limitations, and evidence-based conclusions.
