Grade 11 · Term 1 · C2

Probability is area under the pdf

  1. Idea 1

    Probability is area

    A continuous random variable can take any value across an interval. Its probability density function (pdf) describes the distribution's shape.

    \[P(a\le X\le b)=\text{area under }f_X(x)\text{ from }a\text{ to }b\]
    Total area under every valid pdf = 1.

    Density is a height—not a probability. A height may even be greater than \(1\); the area represents probability.

    For a continuous variable, \(P(X=a)=0\), so \(P(a<X<b)=P(a\le X\le b)\).

  2. Idea 2

    Use one geometric method

    1. Identify the support.
    2. Recognize the shape.
    3. Use total area \(=1\) to find heights or constants.
    4. Mark the requested interval.
    5. Break it into familiar shapes.
    6. Add or subtract their areas.
    7. Interpret the probability in context.
    Rectangle\[A=bh\]
    Triangle\[A=\frac12bh\]
    Trapezoid\[A=\frac12(h_1+h_2)b\]

    The task is mostly: recognize the geometry, then calculate its area.

  3. Shape 1

    Uniform distribution

    On support \([L,U]\), the density has one constant height. The entire pdf is a rectangle.

    \[f_X(x)=\frac1{U-L},\quad L\le x\le U\]

    because \((U-L)h=1\). Therefore:

    \[P(a\le X\le b)=(b-a)h\]

    Uniform → constant height → rectangles.

  4. Shape 2

    Linear distribution

    The pdf is one straight line over all of \([L,U]\). Both endpoint heights may be positive, so the whole area is generally a trapezoid.

    \[f_X(x)=mx+d\qquad 1=\frac12(h_L+h_U)(U-L)\]
    \[P(a\le X\le b)=\frac12\left[f(a)+f(b)\right](b-a)\]
    Linear
    positive ↗ positive
    Triangular
    zero ↗ peak ↘ zero

    Linear → one sloping line → trapezoids.

  5. Shape 3

    Triangular distribution

    On \([L,U]\), the density is zero at both ends, rises to height \(H\) at mode \(M\), then falls in a second straight segment.

    \[1=\frac12(U-L)H\quad\Longrightarrow\quad H=\frac2{U-L}\]

    Find intermediate heights using straight-line proportionality (similar triangles).

    \[P(\text{middle})=1-P(\text{left tail})-P(\text{right tail})\]

    Triangular → zero at both ends, one peak → triangles and trapezoids.

  6. Shape 4

    Piecewise uniform distribution

    The density is constant within each interval but may jump to a different height at a breakpoint.

    \[1=A_1+A_2+A_3+\cdots\]

    Every \(A_i\) is a rectangle. If a requested interval crosses a breakpoint, split it and add the rectangles:

    \[P(a\le X\le b)=A_1+A_2+\cdots\]

    Never use one rectangle across a density change. Split first.

    Piecewise uniform → several constant sections → several rectangles.

  7. Shape 5

    Piecewise-linear distribution

    Several straight segments meet at breakpoints. The pdf may rise, stay flat, then decline.

    \[1=A_1+A_2+A_3+\cdots\]
    • Identify every breakpoint.
    • Find heights at the relevant endpoints.
    • Split any region that crosses a breakpoint.
    • Combine triangles, rectangles, and trapezoids.

    Piecewise linear → multiple straight sections → combine geometric shapes.
