Grade 11 · Term 1 · C3

Uniform probability around a cycle

  1. New idea

    Some variables repeat

    Direction, time of day, and rotation return to their starting value after one complete period.

    \[ 0^\circ\equiv360^\circ,\qquad 0\equiv2\pi, \qquad 0\text{ h}\equiv24\text{ h}. \]
    Circular describes the repeating variable. Uniform means equal-width parts of the cycle have equal probability.

  2. Angle reference

    Degrees and radians name the same positions

    \[ 360^\circ=2\pi,\qquad180^\circ=\pi, \qquad90^\circ=\frac\pi2. \]

    Convert by multiplying by the appropriate form of \(1\):

    \[ \theta_{\rm rad}=\theta_{\rm deg}\frac\pi{180}, \qquad \theta_{\rm deg}=\theta_{\rm rad}\frac{180}{\pi}. \]
    Keep all widths and density units consistent in one calculation.

  3. Cycle cut

    Unwrap the circle into one interval

    A cut lets us display a cycle as \([0,P)\). It creates written endpoints, not a physical gap.

    \[f(x+P)=f(x).\]

    An event close to the cut may appear as two pieces. For example, directions within \(30^\circ\) of the home mark become

    \[ [330^\circ,360^\circ)\cup[0^\circ,30^\circ]. \]
    Split a wrapped event at the cut, then add the two disjoint areas.

  4. Uniform model

    Find the height from total area \(1\)

    On a cycle of length \(P\), a uniform pdf is a rectangle of width \(P\) and constant height \(h\).

    \[ 1=Ph,\qquad h=\frac1P. \]

    Therefore, on one chosen cycle,

    \[ f_X(x)=\frac1P,\quad 0\le x<P, \qquad f_X(x+P)=f_X(x). \]
    For an arc or interval of width \(w\), \(P(\text{event})=w/P\).

  5. Procedure

    Nine steps for a circular-uniform problem

    1Variable, units, period, and cut

    Name what repeats, its units, the cycle length \(P\), and where the displayed cycle begins.

    2Identify the rectangular shape

    Confirm that equal-width arcs or time intervals are equally likely.

    3Use total area \(=1\)

    \[1=Ph\quad\Rightarrow\quad h=\frac1P.\]

    4Write the periodic pdf

    Give the constant rule on one cycle and state \(f(x+P)=f(x)\).

    5Shade the event

    Translate the words into an interval or union of intervals on the displayed cycle.

    6Split at the cut

    If the event wraps, separate its end-of-cycle and start-of-cycle pieces.

    7Find each width

    For every piece, subtract its lower endpoint from its upper endpoint.

    8Add rectangle areas

    \[P(\text{event})=\sum(\text{width})(\text{height}).\]

    9Check and interpret

    Verify \(0\le P(\text{event})\le1\), convert to a percentage if helpful, and answer in context.

  6. Example

    Circular-uniform distribution

    Planetarium spotlight direction

    A Bogotá planetarium programs a spotlight direction \(\Theta\), measured counterclockwise in radians on \([0,2\pi)\). Controlled randomization gives equal chances to equal-width arcs.

    • The reference mark is the cut: \(0\equiv2\pi\).
    • The maintenance sector is \([3\pi/2,2\pi)\).
    • The alignment band lies within \(\pi/4\) of the reference mark and crosses the cut.
    Find: the periodic pdf, the maintenance-sector probability, and the wrapped alignment-band probability.

  7. Solution

    Apply the same geometric routine

    1Variable, period, cut, and shape

    \(\Theta\) is an angle in radians, \(P=2\pi\), the cut is \(0\equiv2\pi\), and equal-arc randomization gives a rectangle.

    2Total area \(=1\)

    \[1=(2\pi)h,\qquad h=\frac1{2\pi}.\]

    3Complete periodic pdf

    \[f_\Theta(\theta)=\frac1{2\pi},\quad0\le\theta<2\pi,\qquad f_\Theta(\theta+2\pi)=f_\Theta(\theta).\]

    4Maintenance rectangle

    \[P\!\left(\frac{3\pi}{2}\le\Theta<2\pi\right)=\left(2\pi-\frac{3\pi}{2}\right)\frac1{2\pi}=\frac14=25\%.\]

    5Split the alignment band

    \[\left[\frac{7\pi}{4},2\pi\right)\cup\left[0,\frac\pi4\right].\]

    6Add and interpret

    \[P(\text{alignment})=\frac\pi4\frac1{2\pi}+\frac\pi4\frac1{2\pi}=\frac14=25\%.\]

    Both results are valid probabilities. One quarter of randomized directions fall in each named region.

  8. Example 2

    Circular-uniform time

    Random daily system-check time

    A Bogotá student team schedules one automated system check per day. Its time \(T\), in hours, is recorded on \([0,24)\), with midnight as the cut. Equal-length time intervals are equally likely.

    • Midnight is represented by the equivalent endpoints \(0\equiv24\).
    • The evening tail runs from 18:00 to midnight.
    • The overnight band runs from 21:00 through 03:00 and crosses the cut.
    Find: the periodic pdf, the evening-tail probability, and the wrapped overnight-band probability.

  9. Solution 2

    Unwrap the 24-hour clock

    1Normalize and state the model

    \[1=24h\Rightarrow h=\frac1{24}.\]
    \[f_T(t)=\frac1{24},\quad0\le t<24,\qquad f_T(t+24)=f_T(t).\]

    2Evening tail

    \[P(18\le T<24)=(24-18)\frac1{24}=\frac14=25\%.\]

    3Split at midnight

    \[[21,24)\cup[0,3].\]

    4Add the overnight areas

    \[P(\text{overnight})=(24-21)\frac1{24}+(3-0)\frac1{24}=\frac14=25\%.\]

    One quarter of randomized checks occur in each named six-hour window.

  10. Example 3

    Circular-uniform direction

    Randomized rotating-device direction

    At a Bogotá university event, a motorized camera randomizes its clockwise starting direction \(D\), measured in degrees on \([0^\circ,360^\circ)\). No direction is preferred.

    • The home mark is the cut: \(0^\circ\equiv360^\circ\).
    • The support-cable sector runs from \(300^\circ\) to the cut.
    • The backdrop-viewing sector runs from \(120^\circ\) through \(210^\circ\).
    Find: the periodic pdf, the support-cable probability, and the backdrop-viewing probability.

  11. Solution 3

    Measure each directional sector

    1Normalize and state the model

    \[1=360h\Rightarrow h=\frac1{360}.\]
    \[f_D(d)=\frac1{360},\quad0\le d<360,\qquad f_D(d+360^\circ)=f_D(d).\]

    2Support-cable tail

    \[P(300^\circ\le D<360^\circ)=(360-300)\frac1{360}=\frac16\approx16.7\%.\]

    3Backdrop sector

    This interval does not cross the cut, so it is one rectangle of width \(210-120=90^\circ\).

    \[P(120^\circ\le D\le210^\circ)=(210-120)\frac1{360}=\frac14=25\%.\]

    4Interpret

    About \(16.7\%\) of starting directions approach the support cable, while \(25\%\) frame the backdrop.

  12. Summary

    Three ideas to carry into the practice activity

    IdeaWhat to doKey relationship
    PeriodState the cycle length and equivalent endpoints.\(f(x+P)=f(x)\)
    Uniform heightNormalize the rectangle over one complete cycle.\(h=1/P\)
    Wrapped eventSplit at the artificial cut and add the areas.\(P(A\cup B)=P(A)+P(B)\) for disjoint pieces
    The circle is continuous across the cut, even when its interval drawing is not.
