Confidence & Interval Estimation

Inferential tools for samples and populations using normal and t-student approaches in Global Economics.

View Outline PDF Outline

C1

Distinguishes population parameters from sample statistics by interpreting and comparing the notation for population and sample means, standard deviations, and proportions in economic and financial contexts.

C2

Compares point and interval estimation and interprets confidence levels through their long-run coverage meaning, distinguishing this from the probability that a particular calculated interval contains the parameter.

C3

Identifies the components of a confidence interval, including point estimate, critical value, standard error, and margin of error, explaining how each component affects precision and reliability.

C4

Constructs confidence intervals for a population mean with known variance using the normal distribution, applying them to contexts such as average income, average investment return, or average price level.

C5

Constructs confidence intervals for a population proportion using sample proportions, applying them to contexts such as poverty rates, defaulting borrowers, or successful investments.

C6

Determines the sample size required to estimate a population's mean or proportion with a specified margin of error, using it to plan economic surveys or financial studies with predetermined precision.

C7

Compares confidence intervals constructed from samples of different sizes and confidence levels, analysing the trade-off between confidence and precision.

C8

Constructs confidence intervals for a population mean or population proportion with unknown variance using the Student’s t-distribution, interpreting the result in finance and economics contexts.

C9

Assesses the validity and limitations of confidence-interval inferences by evaluating the sampling design, potential bias, and independence, including the 10% condition for sampling without replacement and procedure-specific requirements concerning sample size, distributional shape, and outliers.

C10

Integrates research design, data analysis, and confidence-interval inference to investigate an economic or financial question, justifying the selected procedure and communicating estimates, precision, assumptions, limitations, and evidence-based conclusions.
