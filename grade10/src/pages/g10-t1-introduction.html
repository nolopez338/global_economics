Analysis of Decisions

Decision-making with data, payoff tables, and rules such as EMV, Maximin, and Maximax.

Grade 10 · Term 1

From uncertainty to a justified decision

Follow the sequence to organise a decision, apply suitable criteria, test the result, and justify a recommendation.

  1. Step 1

    Characterise the decision

    Identify the decision alternatives, states of nature, consequences, and the economic or financial context.

    Criterion C1

  2. Step 2

    Organise and calculate payoffs

    Place alternatives, states of nature, and payoffs in a payoff table. When needed, calculate profit from revenues and fixed and variable costs.

    Criteria C2–C3

  3. Step 3

    Decide without probabilities

    Use Maximax for the highest possible payoff and Maximin for the best worst-case payoff; interpret the risks and limitations. Then build an opportunity loss table and use Minimax Regret to minimise the maximum loss.

    Criteria C4–C5

  4. Step 4

    Decide with probabilities

    Use stated or estimated probabilities to calculate Expected Monetary Value, then apply the Bayes decision criterion to select the highest expected payoff.

    Criterion C6

  5. Step 5

    Test probability assumptions

    Use subjective probabilities and sensitivity analysis to see how changes in assumptions affect the preferred decision.

    Criterion C7

  6. Step 6

    Compare the decision criteria

    Contrast Maximax, Maximin, Minimax Regret, and EMV/Bayes by their information requirements, risk orientations, recommended alternatives, and limitations.

    Criterion C8

  7. Step 7

    Build and solve a decision tree

    Distinguish decision nodes from chance nodes, assign probabilities and payoffs, and apply backward induction to identify an optimal strategy.

    Criterion C9

  8. Step 8

    Evaluate and justify the decision

    For the economic or financial scenario, select and justify the most appropriate criterion using the available information, risk attitude, and decision context.

    Criterion C10
