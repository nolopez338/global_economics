  1. Introduction · Decision under uncertainty

    Local Café Investment

    A café must choose whether to launch a new line of artisan desserts or keep its current menu before knowing whether a nearby office complex will open on schedule or its opening will be delayed. An on-schedule opening is expected to bring many nearby workers and produce high café traffic; a delayed opening produces low café traffic during the relevant period. The office-complex opening is the external event; high or low café traffic is the resulting state. Each alternative–state pair has an economic consequence: projected profits of 42 or 8 thousand USD for the dessert line, and 28 or 20 thousand USD for the current menu.

    Identify the decision elements

    AlternativesLaunch dessert line; keep current menu

    States of NatureHigh café traffic; low café traffic
    ConsequencesThe profit outcome produced by each alternative–state pair

    The event is the external occurrence; its realized outcome determines the traffic condition represented by the state of nature.

    Event↓State \(S_j\)

  5. Payoff construction · 2

    Two valid payoff-table orientations

    The payoff notation does not depend on where alternatives or states are placed in the table. \(P_{ay}(A_i,S_j)\) means the payoff produced by alternative \(A_i\) together with state \(S_j\), regardless of table orientation.

    Alternatives as rows

    \(S_1\)\(S_2\)
    \(A_1\)\(P_{ay}(A_1,S_1)\)\(P_{ay}(A_1,S_2)\)
    \(A_2\)\(P_{ay}(A_2,S_1)\)\(P_{ay}(A_2,S_2)\)

    Alternatives as columns

    \(A_1\)\(A_2\)
    \(S_1\)\(P_{ay}(A_1,S_1)\)\(P_{ay}(A_2,S_1)\)
    \(S_2\)\(P_{ay}(A_1,S_2)\)\(P_{ay}(A_2,S_2)\)

    Both presentations are valid. To keep our work consistent in class, we will use alternatives as rows and states of nature as columns.

    i identifies the alternative; j identifies the state. The indices identify function inputs, not a row or column by themselves.

  6. Payoff construction · 3 · Narrative

    Build the payoff in ordinary language

    Total Payoff

    The total payoff obtained is the sum of the variable payoff and the fixed payoff.

    Fixed Payoff

    Add the fixed part associated with the chosen alternative and the fixed part associated with the realized state.

    Variable Payoff

    Multiply the quantity produced by the combined choice and situation by the sum of their two per-unit values.

  7. Payoff construction · 4 · Relationships

    Make the operations visible

    Total Payoff

    variable payoff + fixed payoff

    Fixed Payoff

    fixed part from the alternative + fixed part from the state

    Variable Payoff

    quantity from the combination × (per-unit value from the alternative + per-unit value from the state)

  8. Payoff construction · 5 · Inputs

    Identify the two inputs

    Total Payoff

    Payoff for the chosen alternative and realized state = variable payoff + fixed payoff

    Fixed Payoff

    fixed payoff from the chosen alternative + fixed payoff from the realized state

    Variable Payoff

    quantity produced by the chosen alternative–realized state combination × (alternative per-unit value + state per-unit value)

    Assemble the total equation

    Total Payoff

    \(P_{ay}(A_i,S_j)=\)\(q(A_i,S_j)\bigl[v(A_i)+v(S_j)\bigr]\)\(+\)\(f(A_i)+f(S_j)\)

  16. Payoff construction · 13 · Final model

    The complete payoff model

    \[P_{ay}(A_i,S_j)=q(A_i,S_j)\bigl[v(A_i)+v(S_j)\bigr]+f(A_i)+f(S_j)\]

    Total Payoff

    \(P_{ay}(A_i,S_j)\): payoff when alternative \(A_i\) is selected and state \(S_j\) occurs

    Fixed Payoff

    \(f(A_i)+f(S_j)\): fixed payoffs associated with the alternative and state

    Variable Payoff

    \(q(A_i,S_j)\bigl[v(A_i)+v(S_j)\bigr]\): combination quantity × alternative and state per-unit payoffs

    \(P_{ay}\)
    total payoff
    \(q\)
    quantity
    \(v\)
    per-unit payoff
    \(f\)
    fixed payoff
    \(A_i\)
    chosen alternative
    \(S_j\)
    realized state

  27. Synthesis

    The model we constructed

    One selected alternative \(A_i\) and one realized state \(S_j\) determine one total payoff.

    \[P_{ay}(A_i,S_j)=q(A_i,S_j)\bigl[v(A_i)+v(S_j)\bigr]+f(A_i)+f(S_j)\]

    Total Payoff

    \(P_{ay}(A_i,S_j)=\)Variable Payoff\(+\)Fixed Payoff

    Variable Payoff

    \(q(A_i,S_j)\bigl[v(A_i)+v(S_j)\bigr]\)

    Combination quantity × combined per-unit payoff

    Fixed Payoff

    \(f(A_i)+f(S_j)\)

    Combined fixed payoff from the alternative and state

    \(q(A_i,S_j)\)
    quantity produced by the alternative–state combination
    \(v(A_i)+v(S_j)\)
    combined per-unit payoff
    \(f(A_i)+f(S_j)\)
    combined fixed payoff

    Takeaway: Use only the components that apply to the problem.

  28. Reusable method · Conclusion

    From a story to a payoff table

    Final workflow

    1. Identify the alternatives \(A_i\).
    2. Identify the uncertain event and the resulting states \(S_j\).
    3. Describe the consequence for every \((A_i,S_j)\) pair.
    4. Identify the applicable variable and fixed payoff components.
    5. Write and evaluate \(P_{ay}(A_i,S_j)\) for every pair.
    6. Place alternatives in rows and states in columns.
    7. Check units and confirm that every table cell contains exactly one payoff.

    Final check

    • Did I distinguish the event from the states?
    • Did I use only the applicable payoff components?
    • Does every alternative–state pair have exactly one payoff?
    Story→Decision elements→Payoff model→Calculate each pair→Payoff table

  29. Final summary

    General Decision Model

    Decision Elements

    Elements of a general decision under uncertainty
    ElementGeneral form
    Alternatives\(A_1,A_2,\ldots,A_m\)
    Uncertain eventThe external occurrence whose outcome is unknown when the decision is made
    States of nature\(S_1,S_2,\ldots,S_n\)
    ConsequencesThe outcomes produced by every alternative–state pair \((A_i,S_j)\)

    General Payoff Table

    General \(m\times n\) payoff table
    Alternative\(S_1\)\(S_2\)\(\cdots\)\(S_n\)
    \(A_1\)\(P_{ay}(A_1,S_1)\)\(P_{ay}(A_1,S_2)\)\(\cdots\)\(P_{ay}(A_1,S_n)\)
    \(A_2\)\(P_{ay}(A_2,S_1)\)\(P_{ay}(A_2,S_2)\)\(\cdots\)\(P_{ay}(A_2,S_n)\)
    \(\vdots\)\(\vdots\)\(\vdots\)\(\ddots\)\(\vdots\)
    \(A_m\)\(P_{ay}(A_m,S_1)\)\(P_{ay}(A_m,S_2)\)\(\cdots\)\(P_{ay}(A_m,S_n)\)

    General Payoff Equation

    \[P_{ay}(A_i,S_j)=q(A_i,S_j)\bigl[v(A_i)+v(S_j)\bigr]+f(A_i)+f(S_j)\]
    • \(q(A_i,S_j)\): quantity determined by the alternative–state combination
    • \(v(A_i)\) and \(v(S_j)\): per-unit payoff components
    • \(f(A_i)\) and \(f(S_j)\): fixed payoff components
    • \(i=1,\ldots,m\) and \(j=1,\ldots,n\)
