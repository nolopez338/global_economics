Maximax and Maximin Criteria

Grade 10 Term 1: make decisions under uncertainty when probabilities are not available.

Grade 10 · Term 1 · C3

From a coffee shop decision to general decision criteria

  1. 1 · Context

    A coffee shop decision

    A small coffee shop is considering whether to introduce a new dessert line or keep its current menu.

    Customer traffic during the promotional weekend is uncertain, and the manager has no reliable information about the probability of high or low traffic.

    \(A_1\)

    Launch dessert line

    \(A_2\)

    Keep current menu

  2. 2 · The question

    What should the manager choose?

    The manager wants to make a perfectly logical decision, but probabilities are not available.

    Consider two possible attitudes:

    Optimistic?

    Conservative?

  3. 3 · The payoff table

    The possible payoffs

    Alternative High traffic \(S_1\) Low traffic \(S_2\)
    \(A_1\): Launch dessert line \(42\) \(8\)
    \(A_2\): Keep current menu \(28\) \(20\)
  4. 4 · Optimistic decision

    What would an optimistic decision-maker do?

    Look at the best possible outcome

    For each alternative, find the highest payoff that could occur.

    Launch dessert line Best payoff: \(42\)
    vs.
    Keep current menu Best payoff: \(28\)
  5. 5 · Maximax

    Calculate the best payoff for each alternative

    \[ M_1 = \max\{42,8\} = 42 \]

    Launch dessert line

    \[ M_2 = \max\{28,20\} = 28 \]

    Keep current menu

  6. 6 · Maximax decision

    Choose the highest of the best payoffs

    1. Start with the general rule

    \[ A^*_{\mathrm{Maximax}} = \arg\max_i M_i \]

    2. List the best payoff for each alternative

    \[ A^*_{\mathrm{Maximax}} = \arg\max\{M_1,M_2\} \]

    3. Substitute the values from the example

    \[ A^*_{\mathrm{Maximax}} = \arg\max\{42,28\} \]

    4. Choose the alternative with the highest value

    \[ \max\{42,28\}=42 \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad A^*_{\mathrm{Maximax}}=A_1 \]

    The Maximax criterion recommends launching the dessert line because \(42\) is the highest possible payoff among all alternatives.

  7. 7 · Conservative decision

    What would a conservative decision-maker do?

    Look at the worst possible outcome

    For each alternative, find the lowest payoff that could occur.

    Launch dessert line Worst payoff: \(8\)
    vs.
    Keep current menu Worst payoff: \(20\)
  8. 8 · Maximin

    Calculate the worst payoff for each alternative

    \[ m_1 = \min\{42,8\} = 8 \]

    Launch dessert line

    \[ m_2 = \min\{28,20\} = 20 \]

    Keep current menu

  9. 9 · Maximin decision

    Choose the highest of the worst payoffs

    1. Start with the general rule

    \[ A^*_{\mathrm{Maximin}} = \arg\max_i m_i \]

    2. List the worst payoff for each alternative

    \[ A^*_{\mathrm{Maximin}} = \arg\max\{m_1,m_2\} \]

    3. Substitute the values from the example

    \[ A^*_{\mathrm{Maximin}} = \arg\max\{8,20\} \]

    4. Choose the alternative with the highest value

    \[ \max\{8,20\}=20 \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad A^*_{\mathrm{Maximin}}=A_2 \]

    The Maximin criterion recommends keeping the current menu because \(20\) is the highest worst-case payoff.

  10. 10 · Complete analysis

    Add the Max and Min values to the payoff table

    Alternative \(S_1\) \(S_2\) Max Min
    \(A_1\): Launch dessert line \(42\) \(8\) \[=42\] \[=8\]
    \(A_2\): Keep current menu \(28\) \(20\) \[=28\] \[=20\]
  11. 11 · Compare

    One table, two possible decisions

    Maximax

    \[42 > 28\]

    Choose \(A_1\): Launch dessert line

    Maximin

    \[20 > 8\]

    Choose \(A_2\): Keep current menu

    The criteria differ because they focus on different aspects of each alternative.

  12. 12 · General notation

    Representing a general payoff

    \[P_{ay}(A_i,S_j)\]

    \(P_{ay}(A_i,S_j)\) represents the payoff obtained when alternative \(A_i\) is selected and state of nature \(S_j\) occurs.
