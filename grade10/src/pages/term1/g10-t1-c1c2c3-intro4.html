  1. Introduction · Decision under uncertainty

    Local Café Investment

    A café must choose whether to launch a new line of artisan desserts or keep its current menu before knowing whether a nearby office complex will open on schedule or its opening will be delayed. An on-schedule opening is expected to bring many nearby workers and produce high café traffic; a delayed opening produces low café traffic during the relevant period. The office-complex opening is the external event; high or low café traffic is the resulting state. Each alternative–state pair has an economic consequence: projected profits of 42 or 8 thousand USD for the dessert line, and 28 or 20 thousand USD for the current menu.

  2. Introduction · Decision elements

    Identify the four elements

    Café decision elements
    AlternativesLaunch dessert line; keep current menu
    EventWhether the nearby office complex opens on schedule or is delayed
    States of NatureHigh café traffic; low café traffic
    ConsequencesThe profit outcome produced by each alternative–state pair

    The event is the external occurrence; its realized outcome determines the traffic condition represented by the state of nature.

  3. Introduction · Payoff table

    Organize every alternative–state pair

    Alternatives form the rows, and the resulting traffic states form the columns. The columns are not rewritten descriptions of the office-complex event. After the event determines the traffic state, the selected alternative and realized state identify the applicable consequence and payoff.

  4. Payoff construction · 1

    From consequence to payoff

    Alternative \(A_i\)+
    Event↓State \(S_j\)
    →Consequence→Payoff \(\operatorname{Pay}(A_i,S_j)\)
    • The decision-maker selects \(A_i\).
    • The uncertain event determines which \(S_j\) occurs.
    • The pair \((A_i,S_j)\) produces a consequence.
    • \(\operatorname{Pay}(A_i,S_j)\) is the numerical economic value assigned to that consequence.
    Consequence = a description of what happens.
    Payoff = the numerical value assigned to what happens.
    Alternative\(S_1\)\(S_2\)
    \(A_1\)\(\operatorname{Pay}(A_1,S_1)\)\(\operatorname{Pay}(A_1,S_2)\)
    \(A_2\)\(\operatorname{Pay}(A_2,S_1)\)\(\operatorname{Pay}(A_2,S_2)\)

  5. Payoff construction · 2

    Keep the payoff table organized

    We use alternatives as rows and states of nature as columns. The entry at their intersection is the payoff produced by that alternative–state pair.

    Our consistent payoff-table orientation
    AlternativeState \(S_1\)State \(S_2\)
    \(A_1\)\(\operatorname{Pay}(A_1,S_1)\)\(\operatorname{Pay}(A_1,S_2)\)
    \(A_2\)\(\operatorname{Pay}(A_2,S_1)\)\(\operatorname{Pay}(A_2,S_2)\)

    \(i\) identifies the selected alternative; \(j\) identifies the realized state.

  6. Payoff construction · 3 · Economic meaning

    Begin with revenue and costs

    First think in ordinary economic language: money coming in is revenue, while money given up is cost. The payoff is what remains after all costs are subtracted.

    Total Payoff

    Payoff = revenue − costs

    Revenue

    Money coming in.

    Costs

    Money subtracted from revenue.

  7. Payoff construction · 4 · Components

    Separate variable and fixed parts

    Revenue and costs can each change with quantity or remain fixed. This gives the three-level structure we will build.

    Total Payoff

    Revenue − Costs

    Revenue

    Variable Revenue + Fixed Revenue

    Costs

    Variable Costs + Fixed Costs

    Variable Revenue

    Revenue that depends on quantity.

    Fixed Revenue

    Revenue that does not depend on quantity.

    Variable Costs

    Costs that depend on quantity.

    Fixed Costs

    Costs that do not depend on quantity.

  8. Payoff construction · 5 · Inputs

    Identify what determines each payoff

    The selected alternative and the realized state jointly determine the revenue, costs, and payoff. Let \(A_i\) name the selected alternative and \(S_j\) name the realized state.

    Total Payoff

    Payoff for \((A_i,S_j)\) = revenue for \((A_i,S_j)\) − costs for \((A_i,S_j)\)

    Revenue

    Revenue associated with the alternative–state pair.

    Costs

    Costs associated with the alternative–state pair.

  9. Payoff construction · 6 · Function notation

    Name payoff, revenue, and cost

    Use \(\operatorname{Pay}\) for payoff, \(r\) for revenue, and \(c\) for cost. Each function uses the same alternative and state inputs.

    Total Payoff

    \[\operatorname{Pay}(A_i,S_j)=r(A_i,S_j)-c(A_i,S_j)\]

    Revenue

    \(r(A_i,S_j)\)

    Costs

    \(c(A_i,S_j)\)

  10. Payoff construction · 7 · Ordinary language

    Describe all four components

    Variable Revenue

    Quantity multiplied by the sum of the per-unit revenue associated with the selected alternative and the per-unit revenue associated with the realized state.

    Fixed Revenue

    The fixed revenue associated with the selected alternative plus the fixed revenue associated with the realized state.

    Variable Costs

    Quantity multiplied by the sum of the per-unit cost associated with the selected alternative and the per-unit cost associated with the realized state.

    Fixed Costs

    The fixed cost associated with the selected alternative plus the fixed cost associated with the realized state.

  11. Payoff construction · 8 · Component notation

    Name the per-unit and fixed components

    The letter \(v\) means “per unit” and \(f\) means “fixed.” The second letter tells us whether the amount is revenue or cost.

    Variable Revenue

    \(vr(A_i)\): alternative per-unit revenue
    \(vr(S_j)\): state per-unit revenue

    Fixed Revenue

    \(fr(A_i)+fr(S_j)\)

    Variable Costs

    \(vc(A_i)\): alternative per-unit cost
    \(vc(S_j)\): state per-unit cost

    Fixed Costs

    \(fc(A_i)+fc(S_j)\)

  12. Payoff construction · 9 · Quantity notation

    Name the quantity from the combination

    \(q(A_i,S_j)\) is the quantity resulting from the selected alternative \(A_i\) and realized state \(S_j\). Quantity multiplies each sum of per-unit amounts, but it does not multiply fixed amounts.

    Variable Revenue

    \(q(A_i,S_j)[vr(A_i)+vr(S_j)]\)

    Fixed Revenue

    \(fr(A_i)+fr(S_j)\)

    Variable Costs

    \(q(A_i,S_j)[vc(A_i)+vc(S_j)]\)

    Fixed Costs

    \(fc(A_i)+fc(S_j)\)

  13. Payoff construction · 10 · Revenue function

    Build total revenue

    Add variable revenue and fixed revenue. Both the alternative and the state can contribute to each part.

    Revenue

    \[r(A_i,S_j)=q(A_i,S_j)[vr(A_i)+vr(S_j)]+fr(A_i)+fr(S_j)\]

    Variable Revenue

    \(q(A_i,S_j)[vr(A_i)+vr(S_j)]\)

    Fixed Revenue

    \(fr(A_i)+fr(S_j)\)

  14. Payoff construction · 11 · Cost function

    Build total costs

    Add variable costs and fixed costs. Both the alternative and the state can contribute to each part.

    Costs

    \[c(A_i,S_j)=q(A_i,S_j)[vc(A_i)+vc(S_j)]+fc(A_i)+fc(S_j)\]

    Variable Costs

    \(q(A_i,S_j)[vc(A_i)+vc(S_j)]\)

    Fixed Costs

    \(fc(A_i)+fc(S_j)\)

  15. Payoff construction · 12 · Assembly

    Substitute revenue and costs

    Start with payoff = revenue − costs. When substituting, put the entire cost expression in parentheses, so every cost is subtracted.

    Total Payoff

    \(\operatorname{Pay}(A_i,S_j)=r(A_i,S_j)-c(A_i,S_j)\)

    Revenue

    \(q(A_i,S_j)[vr(A_i)+vr(S_j)]+fr(A_i)+fr(S_j)\)

    Costs

    \(\big(q(A_i,S_j)[vc(A_i)+vc(S_j)]+fc(A_i)+fc(S_j)\big)\)

  16. Payoff construction · 13 · Final model

    The complete payoff model

    \[\operatorname{Pay}(A_i,S_j)=q(A_i,S_j)[vr(A_i)+vr(S_j)]+fr(A_i)+fr(S_j)-\big(q(A_i,S_j)[vc(A_i)+vc(S_j)]+fc(A_i)+fc(S_j)\big).\]

    The first group is all revenue. The parenthesized group is all costs, so the subtraction applies to variable costs and fixed costs.

  17. Synthesis

    See the complete three-level structure

    Total Payoff

    Revenue − Costs

    Revenue

    Variable Revenue + Fixed Revenue

    Costs

    Variable Costs + Fixed Costs

    Alternative and state

    Each variable or fixed component distinguishes the part associated with \(A_i\) from the part associated with \(S_j\).

    Decision story→Revenue and costs→Variable and fixed parts→Payoff

    Remember: quantity multiplies per-unit revenue and per-unit costs only. Fixed revenue and fixed costs are added to their own totals independently of quantity, and the whole cost total is then subtracted.

  18. Reusable method

    How to solve payoff-construction problems

    1. Identify alternatives \(A_i\).
    2. Identify the uncertain event and states \(S_j\).
    3. Identify each \((A_i,S_j)\) consequence.
    4. Decide whether \(\operatorname{Pay}(A_i,S_j)\) is given directly or must be calculated.
    5. For a calculated payoff, identify revenue \(r(A_i,S_j)\) and costs \(c(A_i,S_j)\).
    6. Separate revenue into variable revenue and fixed revenue.
    7. Separate costs into variable costs and fixed costs.
    8. For every component, distinguish the alternative part from the state part.
    9. Identify \(q(A_i,S_j)\) and multiply it only by the per-unit components.
    10. Add variable and fixed revenue to find total revenue.
    11. Add variable and fixed costs to find total costs.
    12. Calculate revenue minus the entire parenthesized cost expression.
    13. Put alternatives in rows and states in columns.
    14. Check units, parentheses, and that every pair has exactly one payoff.
    Story→Revenue − Costs→Components→Calculate \(\operatorname{Pay}(A_i,S_j)\)→Payoff table
