  1. Introduction · Decision under uncertainty

    Local Café Investment

    A café must choose whether to launch a new line of artisan desserts or keep its current menu before knowing whether a nearby office complex will open on schedule or its opening will be delayed. An on-schedule opening is expected to bring many nearby workers and produce high café traffic; a delayed opening produces low café traffic during the relevant period. The office-complex opening is the external event; high or low café traffic is the resulting state. Each alternative–state pair has an economic consequence: projected profits of 42 or 8 thousand USD for the dessert line, and 28 or 20 thousand USD for the current menu.

  2. Introduction · Decision elements

    Identify the four elements

    Café decision elements
    AlternativesLaunch dessert line; keep current menu
    EventWhether the nearby office complex opens on schedule or is delayed
    States of NatureHigh café traffic; low café traffic
    ConsequencesThe profit outcome produced by each alternative–state pair

    The event is the external occurrence; its realized outcome determines the traffic condition represented by the state of nature.

  3. Introduction · Payoff table

    Organize every alternative–state pair

    Alternatives form the rows, and the resulting traffic states form the columns. The columns are not rewritten descriptions of the office-complex event. After the event determines the traffic state, the selected alternative and realized state identify the applicable consequence and payoff.

  4. Payoff construction · 1

    From consequence to payoff

    Alternative \(A_i\)+
    Event↓State \(S_j\)
    →Consequence→Payoff \(P_{ay}(A_i,S_j)\)
    • The decision-maker selects \(A_i\).
    • The uncertain event determines which \(S_j\) occurs.
    • The pair \((A_i,S_j)\) produces a consequence.
    • \(P_{ay}(A_i,S_j)\) is the numerical economic value assigned to that consequence.
    Consequence = a description of what happens.
    Payoff = the numerical value assigned to what happens.
    Alternative\(S_1\)\(S_2\)
    \(A_1\)\(P_{ay}(A_1,S_1)\)\(P_{ay}(A_1,S_2)\)
    \(A_2\)\(P_{ay}(A_2,S_1)\)\(P_{ay}(A_2,S_2)\)

  5. Payoff construction · 2

    Two valid payoff-table orientations

    The payoff notation does not depend on where alternatives or states are placed in the table. \(P_{ay}(A_i,S_j)\) means the payoff produced by alternative \(A_i\) together with state \(S_j\), regardless of table orientation.

    Alternatives as rows

    \(S_1\)\(S_2\)
    \(A_1\)\(P_{ay}(A_1,S_1)\)\(P_{ay}(A_1,S_2)\)
    \(A_2\)\(P_{ay}(A_2,S_1)\)\(P_{ay}(A_2,S_2)\)

    Alternatives as columns

    \(A_1\)\(A_2\)
    \(S_1\)\(P_{ay}(A_1,S_1)\)\(P_{ay}(A_2,S_1)\)
    \(S_2\)\(P_{ay}(A_1,S_2)\)\(P_{ay}(A_2,S_2)\)

    Both presentations are valid. To keep our work consistent in class, we will use alternatives as rows and states of nature as columns.

    i identifies the alternative; j identifies the state. The indices identify function inputs, not a row or column by themselves.

  6. Payoff construction · 3 · Narrative

    Build the payoff in words

    This first step names every part in ordinary language before introducing equations or notation.

    Whole payoff

    Payoff is the sum of fixed payoff and variable payoff.

    Fixed payoff

    Fixed payoff combines the fixed payoff derived from the chosen alternative and the fixed payoff derived from the state of the world that occurs.

    Variable payoff

    Variable payoff combines the unit value determined by the alternative–state combination multiplied by the quantity derived from the alternative, and the same unit value multiplied by the quantity derived from the state that occurs.

  7. Payoff construction · 4 · Verbal equations

    Turn each sentence into an equation

    The words now show how the parts are combined, while every quantity still keeps its full descriptive name.

    \[\text{Payoff}=\text{Fixed Payoff}+\text{Variable Payoff}\]
    \[\text{Fixed Payoff}=\text{Fixed Payoff from the alternative}+\text{Fixed Payoff from the state}\]
    \[\begin{aligned}\text{Variable Payoff}={}&\text{Unit Value from the alternative–state combination}\times\text{Quantity from the alternative}\\&+\text{Unit Value from the alternative–state combination}\times\text{Quantity from the state}\end{aligned}\]

  8. Payoff construction · 5 · Abbreviated payoff

    Abbreviate the payoff names

    We shorten the three payoff labels, but retain descriptive arguments so their meanings remain visible.

    \[\mathrm{Pay}=\mathrm{Pay}^{F}+\mathrm{Pay}^{V}\]
    \[\mathrm{Pay}^{F}=\mathrm{Pay}^{F}(\text{Alternative})+\mathrm{Pay}^{F}(\text{State})\]
    \[\begin{aligned}\mathrm{Pay}^{V}={}&\mathrm{UnitValue}(\text{Alternative},\text{State})\,\mathrm{Quantity}(\text{Alternative})\\&+\mathrm{UnitValue}(\text{Alternative},\text{State})\,\mathrm{Quantity}(\text{State})\end{aligned}\]

  9. Payoff construction · 6 · Inputs

    Name the chosen alternative and realized state

    Let \(A_i\) identify the chosen alternative and \(S_j\) identify the state that occurs; the payoff relationships themselves do not change.

    \[\mathrm{Pay}^{F}=\mathrm{Pay}^{F}(A_i)+\mathrm{Pay}^{F}(S_j)\]
    \[\mathrm{Pay}^{V}=v(A_i,S_j)q(A_i)+v(A_i,S_j)q(S_j)\]

  10. Payoff construction · 7 · Factorization

    Factor the shared unit value

    Both variable-payoff terms contain the same unit value, so we factor it out without removing either quantity.

    \[\mathrm{Pay}^{V}=v(A_i,S_j)\left[q(A_i)+q(S_j)\right]\]

  11. Payoff construction · 8 · Combined quantity

    Define the combined quantity

    We give the sum of the alternative-derived quantity and state-derived quantity one two-input name.

    \[q(A_i,S_j)=q(A_i)+q(S_j)\]

  12. Payoff construction · 9 · Substitution

    Use the combined quantity

    Substituting the quantity just defined makes the variable-payoff expression shorter while preserving both quantity sources.

    \[\mathrm{Pay}^{V}=q(A_i,S_j)v(A_i,S_j)\]

  13. Payoff construction · 10 · Modeling assumption

    Let the realized state determine unit value

    In this model, the unit value is determined by the realized state, so its alternative input can be removed explicitly.

    \[v(A_i,S_j)=v(S_j)\]

    Therefore:

    \[\mathrm{Pay}^{V}=q(A_i,S_j)v(S_j)\]

  14. Payoff construction · 11 · Complete components

    Rename and combine the fixed payoffs

    We rename the fixed payoff from the alternative as \(f(A_i)\) and the fixed payoff from the state as \(f(S_j)\), then add them to the variable payoff.

    \[\mathrm{Pay}=q(A_i,S_j)v(S_j)+f(A_i)+f(S_j)\]

  15. Payoff construction · 12 · Payoff symbol

    Introduce the payoff symbol

    The symbol \(P_{ay}\) now replaces the abbreviated payoff label; we do not attach function arguments yet.

    \[P_{ay}=q(A_i,S_j)v(S_j)+f(A_i)+f(S_j)\]

  16. Payoff construction · 13 · Function notation

    Write payoff as a function

    Finally, the arguments show explicitly that the complete payoff is evaluated for the chosen alternative and the realized state.

    \[P_{ay}(A_i,S_j)=q(A_i,S_j)v(S_j)+f(A_i)+f(S_j)\]

  17. Synthesis

    From one component to four

    One component

    \(q(A_i,S_j)v^A(A_i)\)  or  \(q(A_i,S_j)v^S(S_j)\)  or  \(f^A(A_i)\)  or  \(f^S(S_j)\)

    Two components

    Examples: \(q(A_i,S_j)\left(v^A(A_i)+v^S(S_j)\right)\) or \(f^A(A_i)+f^S(S_j)\). Other two-component subsets are valid.

    Three components

    Water Treatment: \(q(A_i,S_j)\left(v^A(A_i)+v^S(S_j)\right)+f^A(A_i)\). Other three-component subsets are valid.

    Four components

    \(q(A_i,S_j)\left(v^A(A_i)+v^S(S_j)\right)\)+\(f^A(A_i)+f^S(S_j)\)

    same structure+applicable component→greater detail

    Component count is not table size: four components does not mean four alternatives, four states, or a 4 × 4 table. Complexity increases by adding only the applicable payoff components.

  18. Reusable method

    How to solve payoff-construction problems

    1. Identify alternatives \(A_i\).
    2. Identify the uncertain event.
    3. Identify states \(S_j\).
    4. Identify each \((A_i,S_j)\) consequence.
    5. Decide whether \(P_{ay}(A_i,S_j)\) is direct or calculated.
    6. Separate calculated information into Variable and Fixed payoff.
    7. For every component, decide whether it is a function of the Alternative \(A_i\) or the State \(S_j\).
    8. Identify \(q(A_i,S_j)\) whenever a variable component is present.
    9. Write only the components that apply.
    10. Substitute values for each \((A_i,S_j)\) pair.
    11. Calculate every \(P_{ay}(A_i,S_j)\).
    12. Put alternatives in rows and states in columns.
    13. Check units and ensure every pair has exactly one payoff.
    Story→Identify components→Formula→Calculate \(P_{ay}(A_i,S_j)\)→Payoff table
