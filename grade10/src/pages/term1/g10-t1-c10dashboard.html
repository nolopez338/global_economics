1. Payoff Table

Enter a payoff in every cell (including any zeros). Losses and decimals are welcome. Probabilities must each be between 0 and 1, and their total must be within 0.000000001 (10⁻⁹) of 1. Probabilities are never normalized.

2. Summary

All values use . Results display two decimals; calculations keep full precision. Values within an absolute tolerance of 10⁻⁹ are treated as tied, using the best value or cutoff as the comparison anchor.

How are these statistics calculated?

EMV: multiply each payoff by its state's probability, then add. Max Payoff: the largest payoff for that alternative. Min Payoff: its smallest payoff.

Regret: the best payoff among all original alternatives in a state minus your alternative's payoff. Max Regret: the largest of its state regrets. These benchmarks stay fixed during staged filtering.

3. Criterion Decisions

Each decision compares the complete original set. Different criteria can choose different alternatives; the staged sequence below answers a separate question.

4. Staged Filtering

Apply rules in order, using only the preceding survivors. Eliminated alternatives never reenter. Statistics are computed once from the complete table. Ties at the cutoff all survive, in their original input order.
