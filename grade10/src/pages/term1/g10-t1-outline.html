From uncertainty to a justified decision

  1. Step 1

    Extract and organise the information

    Key Alternatives (Food truck, Shop) States (high or low demand) Uncertain event Consequence Truck payoffs Shop payoffs

    Extract the decision alternatives, states of nature, consequences, and relevant economic or financial information from the scenario. When payoffs are not given directly, calculate them using revenues and fixed and variable costs, and then organise the alternatives, states, and payoffs in a payoff table for comparison.

    Criteria for this step

    Characterises decision-making problems under uncertainty by identifying decision alternatives, states of nature, consequences, and the economic or financial context of the decision.

    Criteria for this step

    Organises decision problems using payoff tables, placing alternatives, states of nature, and payoffs in a clear structure for comparison.

    Criteria for this step

    Calculates profits by combining revenues with fixed and variable costs across different states of nature, determining the corresponding payoffs for each alternative, and organising the results in payoff tables for comparison.

  2. Step 2

    Decide without probabilities

    Use Maximax for the highest possible payoff and Maximin for the best worst-case payoff; interpret the risks and limitations. Then build an opportunity loss table and use Minimax Regret to minimise the maximum loss.

    Criteria for this step

    Applies the Maximin and Maximax criteria to make optimistic or conservative decisions without probabilities, selecting the alternative with the highest possible payoff or the best worst-case, interpreting risks or limitations.

    Criteria for this step

    Constructs opportunity loss tables and applies the Minimax regret criterion to select the alternative that minimises the maximum opportunity loss.

  3. Step 3

    Decide with probabilities and test assumptions

    Use stated or estimated probabilities to calculate Expected Monetary Value and apply the Bayes decision criterion to select the highest expected payoff. Then use subjective probabilities and sensitivity analysis to determine how changes in probability assumptions affect the preferred decision.

    Criteria for this step

    Calculates Expected Monetary Value using stated or estimated probabilities and applies the Bayes decision criterion to select the alternative with the highest expected payoff.

    Criteria for this step

    Uses subjective probabilities and sensitivity analysis to examine how changes in probability assumptions affect the preferred decision.

  4. Step 4

    Compare criteria and build filtering trees

    Key Recommended alternatives Statistic each rule reads Tree decisions Keep · Drop · Select

    Explain why the four rules can recommend different alternatives. Then draw a filtering tree, applying each rule in order using the given statistics for the alternatives still in contention.

    Criteria for this step

    Compares the Maximax, Maximin, Minimax Regret, and EMV/Bayes decision criteria by contrasting their information requirements, risk orientations, recommended alternatives, and limitations.

    Criteria for this step

    Constructs and interprets decision trees for staged filtering rules, applying decision criteria in the specified order to identify the final alternative.

  5. Step 5

    Evaluate and justify the decision

    For the economic or financial scenario, select and justify the most appropriate criterion using the available information, risk attitude, and decision context.

    Criteria for this step

    Evaluates economic and financial decision-making scenarios by selecting and justifying the most appropriate decision criterion according to the information available, risk attitude, and decision context.
