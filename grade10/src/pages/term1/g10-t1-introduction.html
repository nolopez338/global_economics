Five worked decision problems

  1. Problem 1 · Narrative

    Local Café Investment

    A small café must decide whether to launch a new line of artisan desserts or keep its current menu before it knows the actual foot traffic. The dessert-line projections come from expected daily sales: high traffic could generate 42 thousand USD in profit, but low traffic could leave only 8 thousand USD after the new offering is introduced. Historical results for the current menu project 28 thousand USD with high traffic and 20 thousand USD with low traffic. The outcomes differ because the launch benefits more from busy days but is more exposed when few customers arrive, while the familiar menu produces steadier results.

  4. Problem 2 · Narrative

    Green Energy Production Mix

    A renewable-energy company must select an electricity-generation mix before the weather is known. Its modeled annual profits come from electricity sales, where earnings depend on the megawatt-hours generated and sold. If the company builds wind turbines, projected profit is 90 thousand USD in windy weather, 45 thousand USD in sunny weather, and 30 thousand USD in cloudy weather. If it builds solar farms, projected profit is 35 thousand USD in windy weather, 95 thousand USD in sunny weather, and 25 thousand USD in cloudy weather. If it builds a balanced hybrid system, projected profit is 70 thousand USD in windy weather, 65 thousand USD in sunny weather, and 60 thousand USD in cloudy weather. The balanced system gives up the highest specialist payoff but remains comparatively productive across all three weather conditions.

  7. Problem 3 · Narrative

    Global Shipping Network Design

    A multinational logistics firm must choose a shipping-network design for long-term freight contracts before future trade conditions are known. Management's modeled profits come from global freight services and contracts delivered on schedule. With a centralized mega-hub network, projected profit is 220 thousand USD during a trade boom, 140 thousand USD under stable trade, 40 thousand USD with moderate disruptions, and −30 thousand USD with severe disruptions. With a regional multi-hub network, projected profit is 180 thousand USD during a trade boom, 160 thousand USD under stable trade, 110 thousand USD with moderate disruptions, and 60 thousand USD with severe disruptions. The centralized design has the larger boom-time payoff, while the regional design remains more resilient as disruptions become more severe.

  10. Problem 4 · Narrative

    Introductory Direct-Payoff Decision

    A decision-maker must choose between two unnamed alternatives before an uncertain event reveals one of two possible states. Analysts have already converted each possible consequence into a direct profit payoff in thousand USD. The first alternative has projected profits of 10 or 4 depending on the state, while the second has projected profits of 7 or 8. These supplied values differ because each choice responds differently to the eventual conditions; no further payoff calculation is needed.

  13. General case · Narrative

    How a decision under uncertainty works

    A decision-maker chooses among possible alternatives before an uncertain event determines which state of nature occurs. The selected alternative and the state that actually occurs together produce a consequence for the decision-maker.

    ConsequencesThe resulting payment or outcome for each alternative under each state
