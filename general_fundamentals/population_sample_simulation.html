Organize the quantities

Build the interval for the mean

Displayed results are rounded; calculations retain full precision. Known model σ is used, with no finite-population correction. The target is the model mean μ, not the empirical mean of this displayed collection.

What changes? What stays fixed?

  • At fixed confidence, increasing n reduces SE and margin of error.
  • For the same sample, increasing confidence widens the interval.
  • Drawing another sample changes x̄ and the interval. Each calculated interval either covers the fixed μ or it does not.
  • A 95% confidence procedure covers the fixed population mean in about 95% of repeated independent studies under this model.

This interval estimates a mean. It is not a range containing 95% of students, and the fixed μ does not have a 95% probability of being inside an already-calculated interval.

Why 95%? Try independent studies

Each study generates n fresh independent heights from the same normal model. These studies leave your displayed students and selected sample unchanged. The experiment uses your current n and confidence.

Solid intervals labeled “covers” include μ; dashed intervals labeled “misses” do not. Observed coverage varies and need not approach nominal confidence monotonically. Changing n or confidence resets this experiment.
