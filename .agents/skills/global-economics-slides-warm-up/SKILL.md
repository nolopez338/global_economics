# Global Economics Slides Warm-up

Create a separate warm-up file with one familiar situation and an intuitive question that gives an existing lesson's central mathematical idea a meaningful purpose. Include only the warm-up slides, not the full lesson. Leave the source lesson and any practice unchanged; never author a missing lesson through this skill.

## Resolve sources and editing mode

- Accept a slides path, a practice path, or both, and read applicable repository instructions. An existing deck is always required. If only a practice is supplied, locate the corresponding deck using actual neighboring filenames, source comments, and metadata: book year, grade, term, lesson, criteria, and meaningful variant suffixes. Typical sibling folders are `3-slides` and `1-practice_activities`; verify correspondence rather than mechanically substituting suffixes. Do not silently choose an unrelated variant or create a missing deck. Ask only when essential deck identity or an instructional boundary remains unresolved; never require a practice to proceed.
- Treat the deck as the primary instructional source. Read its actual explanations, representations, examples, and conclusions, including relevant definitions or inputs. Identify the central mathematical idea and what it helps students explain, compare, predict, or decide. Distinguish the upcoming learning from reasoning already accessible to students; topic titles and criterion wording alone are insufficient. A corresponding practice, when available, may supply supporting context but must not determine the warm-up in place of the deck.
- Write the output beside the source deck, appending `-warm-up` to its filename stem before `.tex`: `G10_T2_L1_C1_slides.tex` produces `G10_T2_L1_C1_slides-warm-up.tex` in the same directory. Leave the original deck unchanged. Reuse an existing warm-up output for revisions; if the supplied path already ends in `-warm-up.tex`, update that file without appending the suffix again. Record the original deck path in source comments to retain its instructional provenance.
- Use [global-economics-slides](../global-economics-slides/SKILL.md) for established Beamer, highlight, reveal, numbering, and portability conventions. Reuse the source deck's relevant preamble and definitions to make the warm-up independently compilable; do not copy its lesson frames, import additional classroom examples, or reapply its full authoring sequence. This skill defines the separate output and its content allowance.
- Route ordinary changes through [update-latex-complete](../update-latex-complete/SKILL.md) and its linked verification workflow. For an explicit fast/source-only request, use [update-latex-fast](../update-latex-fast/SKILL.md) instead: its no-verification boundary overrides the checks and PDF delivery below. Make pedagogical choices during source editing, then stop without post-edit checking.

## Shape a two-minute contextual question

Transform the deck's central idea into a short, familiar, plausible situation. Briefly establish the context and ask **one** interesting question inviting a prediction, comparison, estimate, judgment, or explanation. The lesson's mathematics should later make the response more precise, justify it, quantify it, or refine it. Give the mathematical idea a meaningful role in the situation rather than decorating a routine calculation with context.

Students must be able to offer a reasoned initial response using everyday reasoning, accessible observations, or prior knowledge. Supply enough information for a defensible response; avoid arbitrary guessing and answers dependent on unstated assumptions. Do not require new terminology, formulas, procedures, or formal calculations introduced later, even if they appear in a practice introduction or opening legend.

Use one focused question with at most one closely related request to explain the reasoning. Favor concise wording, accessible quantities, and familiar representations. Avoid lengthy contexts, derivations, substantial graph construction, research, or tools that undermine an intuitive opening. Create a fresh situation within the deck's verified scope; do not reproduce or reveal upcoming example or exercise answers.

Budget **at most 120 seconds total**, including reading, thinking, and the intuitive check/connection. Allocate each part explicitly in source comments; for example, 15 s reading + 65 s work + 40 s check/connection = 120 s. Adapt these allocations to the actual task and learners; a nominal time label does not make a long task feasible. Show a simple student work-time cue on the prompt, in the deck's language, matching the allotted work time.

## Build the standalone warm-up narrowly

- In an existing warm-up output, find the prompt and check by their content, headings, comments, and overlays, including language variants, and update them in place instead of adding duplicates. If the source lesson already contains a warm-up, use it as the revision starting point in the separate output while leaving the source unchanged. The output contains only the warm-up prompt and check/bridge; do not copy the opening legend or other lesson frames. Its intended classroom use is after the source lesson's complete opening highlight legend and before its first instructional example.
- Default to two logical frames: contextual prompt, then a concise intuitive check and a short bridge explaining how the lesson will investigate the question more precisely. Keep the check accessible before instruction; do not give a formal solution or prematurely teach the lesson. When multiple responses are reasonable, acknowledge relevant assumptions or uncertainty. A single frame with a stable answer overlay is also acceptable. Keep the check/answer hidden until the intended reveal, including in titles, diagrams, captions, and hints.
- Match the deck's language, visual style, mathematical notation, semantic highlights, footer, and logical frame numbering. Reuse existing macros and toggle settings; keep standalone definitions inline and avoid introducing repository inputs or absolute resource paths. Reserve overlay geometry with the deck's stable reveal mechanism. Allow totals to update normally; do not manipulate counters or add automatic section-divider frames.
- Preserve all source lesson content, its preamble, and its complete opening legend in the original file. Do not change shared mappings or modify the practice. Consult the slide skill's linked highlight companions only if a local compatibility issue requires them, without expanding scope.
- Add concise LaTeX comments beside the warm-up recording the repository-relative deck path and relevant explanation/example/conclusion anchors, its central idea, how the context gives that idea a purpose, students' accessible initial reasoning, and the later mathematical refinement. Record any supporting practice path/anchors only if used, plus realistic reading/work/check-connection allocations and total. Keep implementation notes out of student-facing content.

## Verify and deliver

In complete mode, follow the linked update workflow for scoped source review, mathematics, isolated compilation, rendered inspection, relevant variants, and repair. In particular, verify:

- The question follows from the deck's actual central idea, gives it a meaningful contextual role, and supports a reasoned response before instruction without new terminology, formulas, procedures, or formal calculations. It does not expose upcoming answers or depend on practice availability.
- The context supplies enough information for defensible initial reasoning; quantities, units, assumptions, and uncertainty are handled appropriately. The intuitive check remains accessible, and the bridge identifies how the later mathematics will refine the response without prematurely teaching it. Source comments document this connection.
- One focused question, with at most one related reasoning request, and the entire reading/thinking/check-connection sequence realistically fit within 120 seconds; source allocations and the student work-time cue agree.
- The original deck and any practice remain unchanged. The sibling `-warm-up.tex` output contains only one warm-up prompt and check/bridge, with no copied lesson or opening-legend frames; the answer is visibly hidden until its reveal. Revisions reuse the same output without doubling its filename suffix.
- Readable layout, stable first/final and any intermediate overlays, footer/section labels, logical frame numbers and settled totals, and standalone portability are preserved.

Deliver the separate `-warm-up.tex` and a matching `-warm-up.pdf` when compilation and inspection are available and appropriate. Identify the original lesson as the unchanged instructional source. Report checks that could not run; never present a stale PDF as revised. Briefly explain how the contextual question draws on this deck's central idea, invites accessible initial reasoning, and leads to later mathematical refinement. In fast mode, deliver only the warm-up source and change explanation, with the fast workflow's required unverified-status statement.
