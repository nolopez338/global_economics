# Global Economics Classroom Activities

## Scope

Create and maintain the printable student activity only. Its required worked example includes a complete solution within the activity. A separate appended solutions section is outside this skill's responsibility and belongs to a different solution-authoring skill. Do not add or author that section. When updating a source with existing appended solutions, preserve their content and appearance, including their own formatting; scope activity styles to the printable portion and verify preservation after compilation.

## Fixed activity requirements

Every classroom activity contains exactly **one worked example, one guided example, and five independent practice exercises** in total, regardless of the number of source problem families. The worked example has a complete solution with clear, step-by-step reasoning. The guided example has structured prompts and sufficient workspace for students to complete the solution. All five independent exercises remain unsolved in the student handout: exercises 1–3 consolidate the lesson's core skills; exercises 4–5 are slightly more challenging while staying within the same topic and assessment criterion.

All five required section headings must have compact light gray (`black!10`) backgrounds with visibly rounded corners of a consistent 2 mm radius as mandatory structural styling for every activity created or updated with this skill, and complete criterion statements have white rounded boxes with thin gray borders. These mandatory structural styles are distinct from prohibited semantic instructional highlights: do not color or shade concepts, notation, values, or mathematics to encode instructional meaning, use semantic highlight commands, or include highlight legends in the printable portion. Remove inherited semantic highlighting while preserving substantive content and ordinary formatting. Do not invoke highlight-authoring skills or copy highlight definitions, imports, commands, or legends into the activity. Preserve existing appended solutions without applying this prohibition to them.

## Concise tasks and independent practice

Every task and reusable procedure has at most three numbered steps, including task-specific instructions. Consolidate related actions without losing mathematical coverage. Remove requests to justify answers or perform checks from student task prompts throughout the activity. Keep intermediate reasoning, contextual interpretations, and appropriate checks within the fully worked solution; keep useful prompts, partially completed tables, blank workspace, and graphing space within the guided example.

Each independent description must supply all givens needed to apply General tasks: context, observation period where relevant, definitions, values, units, constraints, and necessary assumptions. Incorporate any essential problem-specific mathematical output directly into the description. Omit repeated task prompts, procedural hints, starter calculations, checklists, response lines, blank answer tables, graph templates, and reserved workspace. Do not mention separate paper or where to solve the exercises. Derive mathematical outputs from the actual source topic; this rule does not prescribe a particular lesson's models or representations.

## Build the five sections

Retain the source criterion, notation, and reasoning demands. Vary contexts and values while preserving the mathematical structure, task sequence, and solution procedure within each family. Supply the givens, constraints, and units students need. Use these exact headings once each, in order.

1. **Equations and basic procedure** — Retain substantive plain formulas, notation, conditions, and a reusable procedure of at most three numbered steps.
2. **General tasks** — List only required mathematical outputs in at most three concise numbered steps per task, without explanations, teaching commentary, requests for justification, or verification/check instructions. Retain required contextual interpretations as mathematical outputs.
3. **Worked example** — Include exactly one fully solved example for the entire activity. Explain each step, show intermediate results, and include checks and a final interpretation.
4. **Guided Example** — Include exactly one fresh example for the entire activity. Provide structured prompts, a starter calculation, or partially filled tables where helpful, with sufficient workspace at each step. Leave the key reasoning and final answer for students to complete.
5. **Independent Practice** — Include exactly five fresh, unsolved exercises containing only numbered titles and concise, self-contained problem descriptions; a compact given-data table is permitted. The first three consolidate the lesson's core skills. The last two are slightly more challenging while remaining aligned with the same topic and assessment criterion.

Number problems continuously: worked example 1, guided example 2, and independent exercises 3–7. Independent exercises 1–3 (problem numbers 3–5) consolidate; independent exercises 4–5 (problem numbers 6–7) add modest challenge. Keep family coverage in source order where compatible with this progression. Use consistent family and task labels where the same structure recurs. Do not add extra examples or exercises to cover more families. Keep the printable sections free of semantic instructional highlights and highlight legends; apply the required structural heading and criterion styles.

For Grade 10 Term 2 Lesson 1 C1, Type A starts with supplied outcomes and probabilities. Type B calculates ordered outcome probabilities from independent events. Both then define the variable, map outcomes to values, list the support, build the PMF, and find and interpret an event probability. Use one complete worked example that models the common procedure and one guided example that scaffolds its use with the other starting structure. Distribute both families across exactly five independent exercises, with the first three consolidating and the last two adding modest difficulty within C1. This still yields exactly one worked example, one guided example, and five independent exercises, all without semantic instructional highlights. Derive the family structure for other topics from their own sources.

Inspect packages and helpers before adding definitions; reuse loaded xcolor/tcolorbox and keep definitions local. Do not redefine a helper used by appended solutions; use an activity-only helper or a local group ending after the printable portion is flushed.

Use exactly two table-style families, detailed with reusable helpers and a before/after comparison in the layout guide:

Where content permits, use two top-aligned `.49\linewidth` minipages separated by `\hfill`, each starting with `\vspace{0pt}`, and size each `tabularx` to its local `\linewidth`. Scope `\small`, `\tabcolsep=3pt`, `\extrarowheight=0pt`, `\parskip=0pt`, and `\arraystretch=1` locally. Start with ragged-right wrapping widths `.21\linewidth`, `.34\linewidth`, and `X`, with `@{}` outer padding. Remove unnecessary row gaps; use legible inline mathematics. Preserve needed definitions and formulas; adjust widths or use full width when needed. This is a notation reference, never a highlight legend.
2. **General instructional and problem tables:** generalize the ruled three-column family demonstrated by `\ExampleInformationTable` and `\GuidedEstimationTable` to information, calculations, worked and guided examples, and problem tables. Adapt the number of columns to the task. Scope `\small`, `\tabcolsep=4pt`, and `\arraystretch=1.16` locally; use vertical column rules and `\hline` between rows. Start three-column information tables with `.14\linewidth`, `.32\linewidth`, and `X`; compact calculation tables with `.10\linewidth`, `.20\linewidth`, and `X`. The reference's remaining booktabs problem tables and older four-column macros are inconsistencies, not additional default styles.

Compact primarily through semantic consolidation, width allocation, and side-by-side placement. Replace repeated classification columns with bold full-width `\multicolumn` group headings and horizontal rules, preserving classifications and logical order. Split related groups into two top-aligned `.49\linewidth` blocks when useful. Merge Calculation, Result, and Meaning into a broad column where appropriate, retaining formula, numerical substitution, result with units, and contextual meaning as readable paragraphs or aligned mathematics. Consolidate related rows without deleting reasoning (for example, retain both endpoint calculations and the final interval in one interval row). Show shared information once; separate differing cases, teams, or confidence levels into clearly identified blocks with spanning case headings and repeated column headings. Move explanatory calculations immediately beside or below tables when useful, retaining interpretation.

Keep guided tables partially blank with adequate writing space; explicit row gaps are adjustable starting points. Let independent descriptions follow one another compactly, without reserved workspace or solutions. Keep task instructions consistent with the actual columns, group headings, and response spaces students see. Do not compact by indiscriminate font reduction, scaled tables, fixed page counts, or copied negative vertical spacing. Use full-width tables or natural page breaks when needed.
