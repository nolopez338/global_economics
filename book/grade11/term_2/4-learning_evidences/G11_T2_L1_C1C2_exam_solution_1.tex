\section*{Learning objective}
Distinguish fixed population parameters from variable sample statistics in economic and financial contexts; calculate known-population-standard-deviation confidence intervals and interpret their long-run coverage.

A 1-point penalty per criterion applies if the student's name is missing or the assessment is submitted late.

\end{description}

\section*{Task reference material}
\textbf{C1 pairings.} Population $\mathcal P$, size $N$; sample $X$, size $n$; population/sample mean $(\mu,\bar x)$; standard deviation $(\sigma,s)$; proportion $(p,\hat p)$. A condition count is $k$, so $\hat p=k/n$. A sample-proportion change is $100(\hat p_B-\hat p_A)$ percentage points.

\textbf{C2 information table.} Use contiguous groups in this order: Population parameters ($\mu,\mu_{\mathrm{known}},\sigma$); Sample statistics ($\bar x,n,\mathrm{SE}$); Procedure settings ($C=1-\alpha,\alpha,\alpha/2,z_{1-\alpha/2}$). Use the Symbol and Value and Meaning in context headings shown in the shared tasks. As in the classroom reference, the derived SE is placed in the sample-statistics group; here it uses known $\sigma$ and fixed $n$, so it is not an estimate using $s$. The later-known notation refers to the same fixed mean, not a second parameter.

\textbf{Two-sided standard normal reference.} Use the following numerical critical values for all calculations. They are supplied rounded values; retain all digits generated by them when calculating endpoints and widths.

\[
\begin{aligned}
\alpha&=1-C,& \text{each tail}&=\alpha/2,&\mathrm{SE}&=\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt n},\\
\mathrm{MoE}&=z_{1-\alpha/2}\mathrm{SE},&L&=\bar x-\mathrm{MoE},&U&=\bar x+\mathrm{MoE},\\
\mathrm{CI}&=[L,U],&W&=U-L=2\mathrm{MoE}.&&
\end{aligned}
\]
For each confidence level separately, a subsequent interval covers when
$L_i\leq\mu_{\mathrm{known}}\leq U_i$. Equality at an endpoint counts as coverage.
\[
\mathrm{Cov}(C)=\frac{N_{\mathrm{cover}}(C)}{N(C)}\,100\%,\qquad
E_{\mathrm{abs}}(C)=|\text{observed percentage}-100C|\text{ percentage points}.
\]
The interval table uses the shared task headings, with separate endpoint rows; worked tables below separate concepts, calculations and interpretations for clarity. Proportions and confidence settings are unitless; a mean, standard deviation, standard error, margin, endpoint and width use the measurement's units.

\textbf{Common procedure assumptions.} The scenarios specify independent random studies, negligible within-study sampling fractions and sufficiently regular numerical populations for a normal approximation to the sampling distribution of the mean. The finite-population correction is negligible and omitted. Thus nominal normal coverage is an approximation, additionally subject to supplied critical-value rounding. Sample standard deviations describe samples; they do not replace known population standard deviations in this procedure.

\sectionScored{Retail electricity costs}{C1-\sml{4}\quad C2-\sml{4}}{sec:p1}

\textbf{Original shared description}\score{8 overall}

An energy-cost review concerns all 280,000 registered retail businesses operating in Medellin throughout September 2026. Each business contributes its total September electricity bill, measured in thousand COP. A separate yes-or-no condition records whether the business used electronic invoicing in September.

The initial independent random sample contains 196 businesses. Its mean bill is 310 thousand COP, its sample standard deviation is 92 thousand COP, and 49 businesses used electronic invoicing. A second independent random sample from exactly the same business population and September period also contains 196 businesses. Its mean bill is 326 thousand COP, its sample standard deviation is 103 thousand COP, and 98 businesses used electronic invoicing.

A certified spread-only population report makes the population standard deviation of September bills known as 98 thousand COP; it does not disclose the population mean. The two sample standard deviations remain separate descriptive quantities. The initial study estimates the initially unknown mean at 80\% and 98\% confidence.

Assume the bill distribution is sufficiently regular, without dominant outliers, for a normal approximation to sample means of this size. Each study draws a simple random sample without replacement from the full fixed register. Samples are selected independently across studies; overlap between different samples is permitted. Each study selects only 0.07\% of the population, so dependence within a draw and the finite-population correction are negligible.

Ten subsequent independent studies each use 196 businesses, the same known population standard deviation and both the 80\% and 98\% normal confidence procedures. These are additional studies, separate from both samples above. Each study builds both intervals around its own sample mean. The 98\% interval is wider and contains that study's 80\% interval. A later comprehensive investigation of the entire September population establishes its mean bill as 315 thousand COP. Repeated samples do not establish this value. The table records mutually exclusive retrospective coverage categories for these same ten studies.
