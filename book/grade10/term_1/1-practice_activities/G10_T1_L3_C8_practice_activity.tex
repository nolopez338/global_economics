\section{Introduction}\label{sec:c8-introduction}
\phantomsection\label{tone:legend}
\PracticeHighlightLegend{G10-T1-C8}

All problems use \textbf{\GTenToneHL{payoff}{profit} \GTenToneHL{payoff}{payoffs}}: larger values are better, and negative values represent losses. Alternatives $\GTenToneHL{alternative}{A_i}$ occupy rows; mutually exclusive \GTenToneHL{state}{states} \GTenToneHL{state}{of} \GTenToneHL{state}{nature} $\GTenToneHL{state}{S_j}$ occupy columns. The \GTenToneHL{payoff}{payoff} $\GTenToneHL{payoff}{P_{ay}}(\GTenToneHL{alternative}{A_i},\GTenToneHL{state}{S_j})$ is the \GTenToneHL{payoff}{profit} if $\GTenToneHL{alternative}{A_i}$ is chosen and $\GTenToneHL{state}{S_j}$ occurs. A decision is made before the state is known.

\subsection{Four ways to compare the same alternatives}
\textbf{\GTenToneHL{best}{Maximax}} selects the \GTenToneHL{alternative}{alternative} with the highest \GTenToneHL{best}{row} \GTenToneHL{best}{maximum}. It asks, ``Which \GTenToneHL{alternative}{alternative} offers the greatest possible \GTenToneHL{payoff}{profit}?'' \textbf{\GTenToneHL{worst}{Maximin}} selects the \GTenToneHL{alternative}{alternative} with the highest \GTenToneHL{worst}{row} \GTenToneHL{worst}{minimum}. It asks, ``Which \GTenToneHL{alternative}{alternative} has the best worst-case \GTenToneHL{payoff}{profit}?'' Neither needs \GTenToneHL{probability}{probabilities}, even when \GTenToneHL{probability}{probabilities} are available.

\textbf{\GTenToneHL{maxregret}{Minimax} \GTenToneHL{maxregret}{Regret}} first compares \GTenToneHL{alternative}{alternatives} within each state:
\[
\GTenToneHL{benchmark}{b}(\GTenToneHL{state}{S_j})=\max_i \GTenToneHL{payoff}{P_{ay}}(\GTenToneHL{alternative}{A_i},\GTenToneHL{state}{S_j}),\qquad
\GTenToneHL{regret}{r}(\GTenToneHL{alternative}{A_i},\GTenToneHL{state}{S_j})=\GTenToneHL{benchmark}{b}(\GTenToneHL{state}{S_j})-\GTenToneHL{payoff}{P_{ay}}(\GTenToneHL{alternative}{A_i},\GTenToneHL{state}{S_j}).
\]
Regret is the \GTenToneHL{payoff}{profit} forgone compared with the best \GTenToneHL{alternative}{alternative} for the state that actually occurs; it is not necessarily a cash loss. Select the \GTenToneHL{alternative}{alternative} with the \emph{smallest \GTenToneHL{maxregret}{maximum} \GTenToneHL{maxregret}{regret}}. This criterion needs the full \GTenToneHL{payoff}{payoff} table, but no \GTenToneHL{probability}{probabilities}.

\textbf{\GTenToneHL{expected}{EMV}/Bayes} selects the highest \GTenToneHL{expected}{Expected} \GTenToneHL{expected}{Monetary} \GTenToneHL{expected}{Value}:
\[
\GTenToneHL{expected}{EMV}(\GTenToneHL{alternative}{A_i})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\GTenToneHL{probability}{P}(\GTenToneHL{state}{S_j})\GTenToneHL{payoff}{P_{ay}}(\GTenToneHL{alternative}{A_i},\GTenToneHL{state}{S_j}),\qquad
\GTenToneHL{probability}{P}(\GTenToneHL{state}{S_j})\geq0,\quad\sum_{j=1}^{n}\GTenToneHL{probability}{P}(\GTenToneHL{state}{S_j})=1.
\]
It requires a \GTenToneHL{probability}{probability} for every state as well as the \GTenToneHL{payoff}{payoffs}. In these problems the state \GTenToneHL{probability}{probabilities} are the same for every \GTenToneHL{alternative}{alternative}. Maximizing expected money expresses a risk-neutral orientation: it does not directly penalize uncertainty or protect against a large loss. An \GTenToneHL{expected}{EMV} is a weighted average, not a guaranteed \GTenToneHL{payoff}{profit} or necessarily an attainable single outcome.

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.15\textwidth}*{3}{>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}X}@{}}
\toprule
Criterion & Information and selection & Risk orientation & Main limitation\\
\midrule
\GTenToneHL{best}{Maximax} & Payoffs; highest \GTenToneHL{best}{row} \GTenToneHL{best}{maximum} & Optimistic; pursues upside & Ignores other outcomes and their likelihoods.\\
\GTenToneHL{worst}{Maximin} & Payoffs; highest \GTenToneHL{worst}{row} \GTenToneHL{worst}{minimum} & Conservative; protects the worst \GTenToneHL{payoff}{payoff} & Ignores upside and how unlikely the worst state may be.\\
\GTenToneHL{maxregret}{Minimax} \GTenToneHL{maxregret}{Regret} & Full \GTenToneHL{payoff}{payoff} table; smallest \GTenToneHL{best}{row} \GTenToneHL{best}{maximum} \GTenToneHL{regret}{regret} & Cautious about missed opportunities & Ignores likelihoods; depends on the \GTenToneHL{alternative}{alternatives} included.\\
\GTenToneHL{expected}{EMV}/Bayes & Payoffs and valid \GTenToneHL{probability}{probabilities}; highest \GTenToneHL{expected}{EMV} & Risk-neutral about money & Sensitive to \GTenToneHL{probability}{probability} estimates; may recommend an unaffordable downside.\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}
Criteria can disagree because they compare different summaries of the \emph{same} information. Agreement does not mean that their reasoning is identical. Report all tied \GTenToneHL{alternative}{alternatives}; do not invent a tie-breaking rule. Without \GTenToneHL{probability}{probability} information, \GTenToneHL{expected}{EMV} cannot be calculated unless an additional \GTenToneHL{probability}{probability} assumption is explicitly justified. Equal \GTenToneHL{probability}{probabilities} are not implied by uncertainty.

\textbf{How to complete the numerical problems.} The summary tables are already completed; do not recalculate row maxima, row minima, regrets, or EMVs. Identify the recommended \GTenToneHL{alternative}{alternative}(s) by comparing the appropriate summary column. In the answer table, record the winning summary value and name the \GTenToneHL{quantity}{quantity} it represents (for example, ``Row minimum: 35''). Then justify and compare the recommendations using the supplied summaries and the original \GTenToneHL{payoff}{payoffs}.

\subsection{Choosing a Criterion}\label{sec:c8-match}
Which criterion fits each \emph{stated priority}? Give its required information and one limitation, and briefly contrast another feasible criterion.
\begin{enumerate}
\item A start-up knows its \GTenToneHL{payoff}{payoffs} but lacks reliable \GTenToneHL{probability}{probabilities}. Which criterion suits a founder seeking maximum upside and accepting the downside?
\item A family business has reliable \GTenToneHL{probability}{probabilities}. Which criterion suits its priority of the highest worst-case \GTenToneHL{payoff}{profit}?
\item A delivery manager lacks reliable \GTenToneHL{probability}{probabilities}. Which criterion minimizes the greatest \GTenToneHL{payoff}{profit} forgone relative to the best plan in each state?
\item A retailer trusts its \GTenToneHL{probability}{probabilities} and makes repeated seasonal decisions. Which criterion suits its priority of average monetary \GTenToneHL{payoff}{profit}?
\item A museum knows its \GTenToneHL{payoff}{payoffs} but lacks attendance \GTenToneHL{probability}{probabilities}. What information is missing for an \GTenToneHL{expected}{EMV} choice, and why would equal \GTenToneHL{probability}{probabilities} be an assumption?
\end{enumerate}
\subsubsection*{Solution}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \GTenToneHL{best}{Maximax} fits the optimistic priority and needs only \GTenToneHL{payoff}{payoffs}, but ignores downside outcomes. \GTenToneHL{worst}{Maximin} is also feasible and instead protects the worst \GTenToneHL{payoff}{payoff}.
\item \GTenToneHL{worst}{Maximin} fits the conservative priority and needs only \GTenToneHL{payoff}{payoffs}, but ignores upside and likelihoods. \GTenToneHL{expected}{EMV}/Bayes is feasible but prioritizes expected \GTenToneHL{payoff}{profit}.
\item \GTenToneHL{maxregret}{Minimax} \GTenToneHL{maxregret}{Regret} needs the full \GTenToneHL{payoff}{payoff} table, without \GTenToneHL{probability}{probabilities}, but may sacrifice worst-case protection. \GTenToneHL{worst}{Maximin} is feasible and instead protects the minimum \GTenToneHL{payoff}{profit}.
\item \GTenToneHL{expected}{EMV}/Bayes fits the risk-neutral priority and needs \GTenToneHL{payoff}{payoffs} and reliable \GTenToneHL{probability}{probabilities}, but cannot guarantee a season's \GTenToneHL{payoff}{profit}. \GTenToneHL{worst}{Maximin} is feasible and instead protects the worst outcome.
\item A nonnegative \GTenToneHL{probability}{probability} for each attendance state, summing to one, is missing. Equal \GTenToneHL{probability}{probabilities} would assume equally likely states without evidence. The three \GTenToneHL{payoff}{payoff} criteria remain feasible but answer different priorities.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Evaluating Decision Claims}\label{sec:c8-critique}
Is each claim valid? Correct it if needed, using a numerical example where possible.
\begin{enumerate}
\item ``\GTenToneHL{worst}{Maximin} and \GTenToneHL{maxregret}{Minimax} \GTenToneHL{maxregret}{Regret} always agree because both are cautious.''
\item ``An \GTenToneHL{expected}{EMV} of 55 guarantees a seasonal \GTenToneHL{payoff}{profit} of 55.''
\item ``Regret of 25 means an actual monetary loss of 25.''
\item ``Agreement with \GTenToneHL{best}{Maximax} makes Bayes optimistic.''
\item ``Valid \GTenToneHL{probability}{probabilities} make \GTenToneHL{expected}{EMV} best for every decision-maker.''
\end{enumerate}
\subsubsection*{Solution}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Invalid. In delivery expansion, \GTenToneHL{worst}{Maximin} selects $\GTenToneHL{alternative}{A_3}$ (minimum 35); \GTenToneHL{maxregret}{Minimax} \GTenToneHL{maxregret}{Regret} selects $\GTenToneHL{alternative}{A_2}$ (\GTenToneHL{maxregret}{maximum} \GTenToneHL{maxregret}{regret} 25, minimum 10). Worst-case protection and limited missed opportunities are different priorities.
\item Invalid. Delivery plan $\GTenToneHL{alternative}{A_4}$ has \GTenToneHL{expected}{EMV} 55 but can earn only 60, 65, or 20. Expected \GTenToneHL{payoff}{profit} is not guaranteed \GTenToneHL{payoff}{profit}.
\item Invalid. With low festival attendance, $\GTenToneHL{alternative}{A_1}$ earns 10 but has \GTenToneHL{regret}{regret} $35-10=25$. Regret measures forgone \GTenToneHL{payoff}{profit}, not an actual loss.
\item Invalid. Both choose investment $\GTenToneHL{alternative}{A_1}$, but \GTenToneHL{best}{Maximax} uses its maximum of 90 and Bayes its \GTenToneHL{expected}{EMV} of 55.75. Bayes remains risk-neutral.
\item Invalid. The farm's \GTenToneHL{expected}{EMV} choice $\GTenToneHL{alternative}{A_2}$ has minimum 20 and fails the required 30. Only $\GTenToneHL{alternative}{A_3}$, with minimum 35 and \GTenToneHL{expected}{EMV} 40.5, meets the obligation despite valid \GTenToneHL{probability}{probabilities}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Decision Committee Report}\label{sec:c8-report}
Return to the delivery expansion problem in Section~\ref{sec:c8-worked}. Four committee members prioritize, respectively, the largest possible \GTenToneHL{payoff}{profit}, the best worst-case \GTenToneHL{payoff}{profit}, the smallest maximum missed opportunity, and the highest expected \GTenToneHL{payoff}{profit}.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Which criterion and plan fit each member's priority? Give the deciding value and information used or ignored.
\item Which plan would you recommend? Compare two plans numerically, state your priority and one limitation, and explain a reasonable disagreement.
\item If the forecast is withdrawn, which summaries remain usable and which recommendation loses support? Does one remaining criterion become universally best?
\end{enumerate}
\subsubsection*{Solution}
\begin{enumerate}
\item In the stated order: \GTenToneHL{best}{Maximax} chooses $\GTenToneHL{alternative}{A_1}$ (\GTenToneHL{best}{row} \GTenToneHL{best}{maximum} 100), ignoring downside and \GTenToneHL{probability}{probabilities}; \GTenToneHL{worst}{Maximin} chooses $\GTenToneHL{alternative}{A_3}$ (\GTenToneHL{worst}{row} \GTenToneHL{worst}{minimum} 35), ignoring upside and \GTenToneHL{probability}{probabilities}. \GTenToneHL{maxregret}{Minimax} \GTenToneHL{maxregret}{Regret} chooses $\GTenToneHL{alternative}{A_2}$ (\GTenToneHL{maxregret}{maximum} \GTenToneHL{maxregret}{regret} 25), comparing all \GTenToneHL{payoff}{payoffs} but ignoring likelihoods; \GTenToneHL{expected}{EMV}/Bayes chooses $\GTenToneHL{alternative}{A_4}$ (\GTenToneHL{expected}{EMV} 55), using \GTenToneHL{payoff}{payoffs} and \GTenToneHL{probability}{probabilities} without protecting the worst outcome.
\item One valid report is: ``We prioritize expected \GTenToneHL{payoff}{profit} and recommend $\GTenToneHL{alternative}{A_4}$: its \GTenToneHL{expected}{EMV} is 55 versus 47 for $\GTenToneHL{alternative}{A_2}$. This depends on reliable \GTenToneHL{probability}{probabilities} and does not guarantee 55. A member prioritizing missed opportunities may prefer $\GTenToneHL{alternative}{A_2}$, with \GTenToneHL{maxregret}{maximum} \GTenToneHL{maxregret}{regret} 25 versus 40 for $\GTenToneHL{alternative}{A_4}$.'' Other choices are valid with a stated priority, numerical support, and a limitation.
\item Row maxima, row minima, and maximum regrets remain usable, retaining choices $\GTenToneHL{alternative}{A_1}$, $\GTenToneHL{alternative}{A_3}$, and $\GTenToneHL{alternative}{A_2}$, respectively. The EMVs and Bayes choice $\GTenToneHL{alternative}{A_4}$ lose their forecast basis. No remaining criterion becomes universally best: each serves a different priority.
\end{enumerate}
