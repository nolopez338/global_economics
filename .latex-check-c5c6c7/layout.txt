Learning objective
Use continuous probability models and sample data to describe typical values, variability, and interval probabilities,
and interpret differences between sample estimates and theoretical values.



Criteria assessment
This exam assesses C5, C6 and C7 separately, with 24 points per criterion (72 points in total). Each task shows its
point allocation. Partial credit may be awarded when the correct procedure is clearly shown but arithmetic errors are
made. Answers without the required procedure will not receive credit. Failure to write your name on the task sheet or
submitting it after the deadline will result in a one-point deduction from each criterion. For this exam, a minimum of
15 out of 24 points is required to pass each criterion. Use unrounded intermediate values; round final statistics to three
decimal places and percentage errors to two decimal places where necessary.



Evaluated criteria
 C5 Calculates and interprets the theoretical mean, variance, and standard deviation of uniform, triangular, linear,
    exponential, and normal distributions.
 C6 Estimates interval probabilities using relative frequencies from samples and compares them with theoretical
    probabilities.
 C7 Calculates sample means, variances, and standard deviations for different sample sizes and data representations
    and compares them with theoretical values.

C5.2(b) – Reasoning                                                                                                    [2]

2.3   Problem C6.3: Download times, grouped data                                                                    [6]

Two independent simulated samples from the same model give these frequencies. All times are in seconds; classes are
left-inclusive and right-exclusive.

                                        Class       [0, 5) [5, 10) [10, 15) [15, 20)
                                      Sample A         4         8        6        2
                                      Sample B        18         78      76        28

The model gives P (5 ≤ X < 15) = 0.80.

Questions/Tasks

 C6 Find each sample size, empirical interval probability, and absolute error.                                      [4]
 C6 Which estimate is closer? Does increasing sample size guarantee smaller error in every sample?                  [2]


C6.3(a) – Comparing samples                                                                                          [4]

The event contains the two middle classes, giving

           nA = 4 + 8 + 6 + 2 = 20,    kA = 8 + 6 = 14,      PbA = 14/20 = 0.70,    EA = |0.70 − 0.80| = 0.10.

                                nB = 18 + 78 + 76 + 28 = 200,         kB = 78 + 76 = 154,

                                PbB = 154/200 = 0.77,         EB = |0.77 − 0.80| = 0.03.

These relative frequencies are exact for the recorded samples because the event is a union of complete classes, although
they estimate a model probability.

C6.3(b) – Sample size                                                                                              [2]

B is closer because 0.03 < 0.10. Larger random samples generally produce more stable estimates, but random variation
means that a larger sample need not have smaller error in every instance.
