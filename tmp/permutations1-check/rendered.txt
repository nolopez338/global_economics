                                          Permutations and Combinations I
                                                      1. Progressive Problems
1. Six books have different titles. In how many ways can all six be placed in a single row on a shelf? Each book is used once, and two
   rows are different if their left-to-right orders differ.
2. A club has nine distinct members. In how many ways can it choose a committee of four different members? There are no assigned
   roles, and the order in which members are chosen does not matter.
3. Eight distinct students enter a contest with three awards: first, second, and third place. In how many ways can the awards be
   assigned if each award goes to one student and no student receives more than one award?
4. In how many different left-to-right strings can all the letters of BALLOON be arranged? Use each letter exactly as often as it appears
   in the word. Copies of the same letter are indistinguishable, and the strings need not be English words.
5. Six distinct books, labelled A, B, C, D, E, F , are placed in a row, each exactly once. How many left-to-right arrangements have A
   and B next to each other, but C and D not next to each other? Either order within an adjacent pair is allowed.
6. Four girls, Ada, Bella, Cleo, and Dina, and three boys, Eli, Finn, and Gus, are available for a photograph. Choose five different
   students and arrange them in a row. The row must include Ada, at least two girls, and at least two boys, and no two boys may
   stand next to each other. How many different left-to-right rows are possible? All seven students are distinguishable, and each
   selected student appears exactly once.

                                                      2. Olympiad Challenges
7. Three different numbers are chosen from {1, 2, . . . , 10}. No two chosen numbers may differ by 1. How many unordered sets can be
   chosen? Each number can be used at most once.
8. Arrange the numbers 1, 2, . . . , 7 in a row, each exactly once. Call an index i ∈ {1, 2, . . . , 6} a success if i is immediately followed by
   i + 1, in that order. How many permutations have exactly two successes? Successes may overlap: for instance, the consecutive
   segment 3, 4, 5 contributes successes at both i = 3 and i = 4. Two permutations are different if their left-to-right orders differ.
