We estimate a population mean from a simple random sample of independent observations (or a negligible fraction of a much larger population). The population standard deviation σ 1 is supplied and known, not an estimated sample SD. The population is normal or the sample mean is well approximated by a normal distribution. These assumptions support the two-sided normal intervals below. Controlled comparisons. Hold x̄ fixed to isolate changes in width. Actual sample means can change between samples; a new sample need not have the same centre. 2 1.2 Example 1: sample size and transport spending For weekly household transport spending, suppose the known population SD is 20 thousand COP. Identify the population mean, units and model assumptions. Record what varies and what stays fixed. Distinguish individual variability σ from uncertainty in the mean SE . √ 2. For varying n use SE = σ / n and MoE = z ∗ SE . For varying C , keep SE fixed and choose z ∗ from the table. 3. Calculate without premature rounding. Organize a supporting table of n, SE and MoE, or C, z ∗ and MoE. A row supplies coordinates for all three relevant graphs. 4. Axis convention: A vs B always means A vertically and B horizontally. Label both axes, with units or % where appropriate. Select numerical domains and scales that show every point; the confidence range 80–100% keeps 99% and 99.9% distinguishable. Use real spacing, not category positions. 5. Plot accurate coordinates. In these solutions, filled circles are values from the tables; smooth solid curves use the stated model. Sample size is integer: the continuous n curve is only a visual guide between feasible integer values. 6. Check shape and context. At fixed confidence, MoE vs SE is a line through the origin of slope z ∗ . At fixed SE, z ∗ vs MoE is a line through the origin of slope 1/SE. Confidence vs either is nonlinear: C = 2Φ( z ∗ ) − 1, C = 2Φ( MoE / SE ) − 1. These smooth confidence curves use the normal-distribution relationship, not straight joins of rounded table entries. Points can lie very slightly off the curve because the supplied z ∗ values are rounded. On a percentage vertical axis the plotted value is 100C. Reading coverage. A 95% procedure captures the fixed population mean in about 95% of repeated samples under its assumptions. This is nominal long-run coverage, not a probability assigned to the fixed mean after an interval has been observed. Smaller width means greater precision; coverage and precision are different properties. 5 3 General Worked Problems Problems: 3.1. Household grocery spending (C3) 3.2.
