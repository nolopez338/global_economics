\subsection{Key terms and highlight guide}\label{subsec:c3-key-terms}

Greens connect sample quantities; reds connect population quantities. Two blues distinguish \CtwoConfidence{confidence level} from \CtwoCritical{critical value}. Warm shades separate \CtwoSE{standard error}, \CtwoMoE{margin}, \CtwoEndpoint{endpoints} and \CtwoWidth{width}. Symbols and labels preserve these distinctions without color.

\textbf{Opening navigation:} \hyperref[subsec:c3-transport-example]{Example 1: vary sample size};
\hyperref[subsec:c3-quick-check]{Example 2: vary confidence};
\hyperref[tab:notation-reference]{detailed notation reference}.

\textbf{Model used throughout.} We estimate a population mean from a simple random sample of independent observations (or a negligible fraction of a much larger population). The population standard deviation $\CtwoSD{\sigma}$ is supplied and known, not an estimated sample SD. The population is normal or the sample mean is well approximated by a normal distribution. These assumptions support the two-sided normal intervals below.

\textbf{Controlled comparisons.} Hold $\CtwoPoint{\bar{x}}$ fixed to isolate changes in width. Actual sample means can change between samples; a new sample need not have the same centre.

\subsection{Example 1: sample size and transport spending}\label{subsec:c3-transport-example}
For weekly household transport spending, suppose the known population SD is \CtwoSD{20} thousand COP. Keep the estimate at \CtwoPoint{60} thousand COP and confidence at \CtwoConfidence{95\%}, so $\CtwoCritical{z^*}=\CtwoCritical{1.960}$.
With $\CtwoSize{n}=\CtwoSize{100}$, $\CtwoSE{\mathrm{SE}}=\CtwoSD{20}/\sqrt{\CtwoSize{100}}=\CtwoSE{2}$ and $\CtwoMoE{\mathrm{MoE}}=\CtwoCritical{1.960}(\CtwoSE{2})=\CtwoMoE{3.920}$ thousand COP.
With $\CtwoSize{n}=\CtwoSize{400}$, these become \CtwoSE{1} and \CtwoMoE{1.960} thousand COP. Four times as many households halves both measures of uncertainty, with unchanged confidence.

\subsection{Example 2: confidence and transport spending}\label{subsec:c3-quick-check}
Use the same known $\CtwoSD{\sigma}=\CtwoSD{20}$ and fixed $\CtwoSize{n}=\CtwoSize{100}$, so $\CtwoSE{\mathrm{SE}}=\CtwoSE{2}$ thousand COP. Raising confidence from \CtwoConfidence{90\%} to \CtwoConfidence{99\%} raises $\CtwoCritical{z^*}$ from \CtwoCritical{1.645} to \CtwoCritical{2.576} and the margin from \CtwoMoE{3.290} to \CtwoMoE{5.152} thousand COP. Keeping the centre at \CtwoPoint{60} gives endpoints \CtwoEndpoint{56.710, 63.290} and \CtwoEndpoint{54.848, 65.152}; full widths are \CtwoWidth{6.580} and \CtwoWidth{10.304} thousand COP.

\subsection*{Relationships and critical values}
\begin{gather*}
\CtwoSE{\mathrm{SE}}(\CtwoSize{n})=\frac{\CtwoSD{\sigma}}{\sqrt{\CtwoSize{n}}},\qquad
\CtwoMoE{\mathrm{MoE}}(\CtwoSize{n},\CtwoConfidence{C})=
\CtwoCritical{z^*}(\CtwoConfidence{C})\frac{\CtwoSD{\sigma}}{\sqrt{\CtwoSize{n}}},\\[3pt]
\CtwoCritical{z^*}(\CtwoConfidence{C})=\Phi^{-1}\!\left(\frac{1+\CtwoConfidence{C}}{2}\right),\qquad
\text{interval}=\CtwoPoint{\bar{x}}\pm\CtwoMoE{\mathrm{MoE}},\qquad
\CtwoWidth{W}=2\CtwoMoE{\mathrm{MoE}}.
\end{gather*}
$\Phi(z)$ is the fraction of the standard normal distribution to the left of $z$. Its inverse $\Phi^{-1}(p)$ asks which $z$ has fraction $p$ to its left. Thus the two tails outside $\pm z^*$ each have area $(1-C)/2$. You may use this table without evaluating an inverse function.

\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrrrr@{}}\toprule
\CtwoConfidence{Confidence (\%)}&\CtwoConfidence{80}&\CtwoConfidence{85}&\CtwoConfidence{90}&\CtwoConfidence{92}&\CtwoConfidence{95}\\
\CtwoCritical{Approx. $z^*$}&\CtwoCritical{1.282}&\CtwoCritical{1.440}&\CtwoCritical{1.645}&\CtwoCritical{1.751}&\CtwoCritical{1.960}\\\midrule
\CtwoConfidence{Confidence (\%)}&\CtwoConfidence{96}&\CtwoConfidence{97}&\CtwoConfidence{98}&\CtwoConfidence{99}&\CtwoConfidence{99.9}\\
\CtwoCritical{Approx. $z^*$}&\CtwoCritical{2.054}&\CtwoCritical{2.170}&\CtwoCritical{2.326}&\CtwoCritical{2.576}&\CtwoCritical{3.291}\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}\end{center}
\textbf{Rounding policy.} Use the supplied three-decimal critical values consistently for numerical decisions, retain unrounded square roots and intermediate results, and report calculated SE, margins, endpoints and widths to three decimals in the stated monetary unit (exact values may omit trailing zeros). Sample sizes are integers. Use decimal $C$ in formulas (95\% means 0.95), but percentage labels on confidence axes.

\subsection{Reusable graphing procedure}
These steps guide every investigation; they are not extra tasks in a problem.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=7pt]
\item Identify the population mean, units and model assumptions. Record what varies and what stays fixed. Distinguish individual variability $\CtwoSD{\sigma}$ from uncertainty in the mean $\CtwoSE{\mathrm{SE}}$.
\item For varying $\CtwoSize{n}$ use $\CtwoSE{\mathrm{SE}}=\CtwoSD{\sigma}/\sqrt{\CtwoSize{n}}$ and $\CtwoMoE{\mathrm{MoE}}=\CtwoCritical{z^*}\CtwoSE{\mathrm{SE}}$. For varying $\CtwoConfidence{C}$, keep SE fixed and choose $\CtwoCritical{z^*}$ from the table.
\item Calculate without premature rounding. Organize a supporting table of $n$, SE and MoE, or $C$, $z^*$ and MoE. A row supplies coordinates for all three relevant graphs.
\item \textbf{Axis convention: A vs B always means A vertically and B horizontally.} Label both axes, with units or \% where appropriate. Select numerical domains and scales that show every point; the confidence range 80--100\% keeps 99\% and 99.9\% distinguishable. Use real spacing, not category positions.
\item Plot accurate coordinates. In these solutions, filled circles are values from the tables; smooth solid curves use the stated model. Sample size is integer: the continuous $n$ curve is only a visual guide between feasible integer values.
\item Check shape and context. At fixed confidence, MoE vs SE is a line through the origin of slope $z^*$. At fixed SE, $z^*$ vs MoE is a line through the origin of slope $1/\mathrm{SE}$. Confidence vs either is nonlinear:
\[
\CtwoConfidence{C}=2\Phi(\CtwoCritical{z^*})-1,
\qquad \CtwoConfidence{C}=2\Phi(\CtwoMoE{\mathrm{MoE}}/\CtwoSE{\mathrm{SE}})-1.
\]
These smooth confidence curves use the normal-distribution relationship, not straight joins of rounded table entries. Points can lie very slightly off the curve because the supplied $z^*$ values are rounded. On a percentage vertical axis the plotted value is $100C$.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Reading coverage.} A 95\% procedure captures the fixed population mean in about 95\% of repeated samples under its assumptions. This is nominal long-run coverage, not a probability assigned to the fixed mean after an interval has been observed. Smaller width means greater precision; coverage and precision are different properties.

\[

\subsection{A. Monthly electricity costs for small shops (C3)}\label{prob:c3-3}
A business association estimates the town's mean monthly electricity cost per small shop. Known $\sigma=100$ thousand COP describes individual shops. Design A samples 100 shops at 95\% confidence; design B samples 400 shops at 99\% confidence. Samples are random and independent from a much larger population, and the sample mean is approximately normal. Hold both comparison means at 120 thousand COP.
\QuestionsTasks[sec:advanced]{
{3}/{sol:c3-3}/{Investigate which design produces the narrower interval when both sample size and confidence change. Calculate SE, MoE, endpoints and full width for each design. \subsubsection*{Worked solution: competing design effects}\label{sol:c3-3}
The model uses $\CtwoSE{\mathrm{SE}}=\CtwoSD{100}/\sqrt{\CtwoSize{n}}$ thousand COP and $\CtwoMoE{\mathrm{MoE}}=\CtwoCritical{z^*}\CtwoSE{\mathrm{SE}}$. Hence

\text{B: }\CtwoSE{\mathrm{SE}_B}&=\CtwoSD{100}/\sqrt{\CtwoSize{400}}=\CtwoSE{5},&
\CtwoMoE{\mathrm{MoE}_B}&=\CtwoCritical{2.576}(\CtwoSE{5})=\CtwoMoE{12.880}.
\end{align*}
