                           Confidence intervals and economic report audits
                                      Global Economics | Grade 11 | Term 2 | Lesson 1
  C4 Constructs confidence intervals for a population mean with known variance using the normal distribution, applying
  them to contexts such as average income, average investment return, or average price level.

                                           Equations and basic procedure
Use independent random observations from a normal population, or a sufficiently large sample for the normal approximation,
with known population variance. All data here are hypothetical. Each inverse report is independent of every other report
and of the forward comparison. Its endpoints are exact relative to its stated construction cutoff.
Symbol                      Concept                                       Formula
µ, x̄                       Population and sample means                   µ is fixed but unknown; x̄ is the observed interval center.
σ, σ 2 , s                  Population SD, variance; sample SD            Known σ > 0 is required; σ 2 has squared response units.
                                                                          A sample SD s is not a substitute here.
                                                                                                            √
n, SE                       Sample size; standard error                   n is a positive integer; SE = σ/ n is the sampling SD of
                                                                          the mean.
C, α                        Confidence and significance                   α = 1 − C; each tail has area α/2.
Φ, z ∗                      Standard normal CDF; positive cutoff          Φ(z) = P (Z ≤ z), Z ∼ N (0, 1); z ∗ = Φ−1 ((1 + C)/2).
                                                                                     √
E, [L, U ], W               Margin, endpoints and full width              E = z ∗ σ/ n; [L, U ] = [x̄ − E, x̄ + E]; W = 2E.
x̄, E                       Recovery from endpoints                       x̄ = (L + U )/2; E = (U − L)/2.
                                                                                                 √
n, σ                        Inverse rules                                 n = (z ∗ σ/E)2 ; σ = E n/z ∗ .
Use z ∗ = 1.645 for 90%, 1.96 for 95%, and 2.576 for 99%. Use the stated cutoff in inverse calculations. Means, SD, SE,
margins, bounds and widths share response units; n, C, α, z ∗ are unitless.

    1. Define the population mean and units; organize each report’s knowns and unknowns and convert confidence information
       into C, α, α/2, z ∗ .
    2. For forward comparisons, calculate the full two-size, two-confidence grid. For inverse reports, find the midpoint and
                                   √
       half-width, then rearrange E n = z ∗ σ for the missing quantity.
    3. Interpret the interval and the economic decision. At fixed other inputs, width increases with C and σ, and decreases
            √
       with n.

Confidence describes long-run coverage of the fixed population mean, not the fraction of individual values inside an interval
or a probability assigned to the fixed mean after sampling. Rounded endpoints can make inverse recovery nonunique;
rounding n upward does not recover a historical count.

 Task 1 — Construct intervals and select a research budget.


    1. Population mean and units; known quantities; C, α, α/2, z ∗ at both levels.
    2. A ruled comparison table for all four (n, C) combinations, with SE, E, [L, U ] and W .
    3. The smallest offered sample meeting the width limit at both levels; interval interpretations and effects of confidence
       and sample size on width.

 Task 2 — Recover sample means and sample sizes.


    1. Each report’s population mean, units, confidence settings, midpoint and half-width.
    2. The recovered x̄ and n for each report.
    3. Report eligibility under the stated count and observed-mean thresholds; interval interpretations.

                                                    Worked example

1. Courier reference-week earnings                                                                              Complete
A cooperative studies mean reference-week earnings in thousand COP per courier. Independent random samples are large
enough for the normal approximation. For its research-budget comparison, keep x̄ = 450 and known σ = 60 at both offered
sizes n = 36, 144. Use C = 95% and α = 0.01, with cutoffs 1.96 and 2.576. The full-width limit is 30 thousand COP at
both levels.

Task 1 — Construct intervals and select a research budget.

1. Model and settings.
µ is the population mean reference-week earnings. Here x̄ = 450, σ = 60 and σ 2 = 3600 in squared thousand COP. A
courier is one sampled unit.
Symbol             Concept                                   In context...
C                  95% confidence                            C = 0.95, α = 0.05, α/2 = 0.025, z ∗ = 1.96.
α                  1% significance                           C = 1 − 0.01 = 0.99, α/2 = 0.005, z ∗ = 2.576.

2. Four constructions.
                            √                  √
The standard errors are 60/ 36 = 10 and 60/ 144 = 5 thousand COP. Multiply each by the relevant cutoff and
subtract/add the resulting margin from/to 450.
n and C            SE and margin E                  Endpoint calculations and full width
36, 95%            SE = 10; E = 1.96(10) = 19.6     [450 − 19.6, 450 + 19.6] = [430.4, 469.6]; W = 39.2.
36, 99%            SE = 10; E = 2.576(10) = 25.76   [450 − 25.76, 450 + 25.76] = [424.24, 475.76]; W = 51.52.
144, 95%           SE = 5; E = 1.96(5) = 9.8        [450 − 9.8, 450 + 9.8] = [440.2, 459.8]; W = 19.6.
144, 99%           SE = 5; E = 2.576(5) = 12.88     [450 − 12.88, 450 + 12.88] = [437.12, 462.88]; W = 25.76.

3. Decision and interpretation.
Choose 144 couriers: 19.6 ≤ 30 and 25.76 ≤ 30, whereas both widths for 36 couriers exceed 30. Quadrupling n halves SE
and width. Raising confidence from 95% to 99% increases width by 2.576/1.96 ≈ 1.3143 at either size. At 95% confidence,
the respective intervals estimate the population mean as 430.4–469.6 and 440.2–459.8 thousand COP; at 99%, they estimate
it as 424.24–475.76 and 437.12–462.88 thousand COP. In repeated sampling each procedure covers µ at its stated rate.
These are not ranges for individual earnings. Checks. Every midpoint is 450; for example, 459.8 − 440.2 = 19.6 = 2(9.8).
All margins are positive and the four intervals are symmetric.
