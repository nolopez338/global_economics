Learning objective

Distinguish fixed population parameters from variable sample statistics in economic and financial contexts; calculate
known-population-standard-deviation confidence intervals and interpret their long-run coverage.


Criteria assessment

Answer exactly three problems. Problems 1 and 2 each award 3 points to C1 and 3 to C2, for 6 points each. Problem 3 is
worth 5 points overall. Its equal allocation of 2.5 to C1 and 2.5 to C2 is proposed: the governing brief specifies only its
overall value.
With that proposed split, C1 has 8.5 available points, C2 has 8.5 and the exam has 17. Each criterion requires at
least 4 points to pass independently. Points cannot transfer between criteria. An overall total alone does not
establish a pass in both criteria. These are the exam’s thresholds; no cumulative assessment policy is supplied.


Evaluated criteria

C1 Distinguishes population parameters from sample statistics by interpreting and comparing population and sample
    means, standard deviations and proportions in economic and financial contexts.

C2 Compares point and interval estimation and interprets confidence through long-run coverage, distinguishing this from
    probability assigned to a particular calculated interval containing the parameter.

Original shared description                                                                                   [6 overall]
An energy-cost review concerns all 200,000 registered retail businesses operating in Medellin throughout October 2026.
Each business contributes its total October electricity bill, measured in thousand COP. A separate yes-or-no condition
records whether the business used electronic invoicing in October.
The initial independent random sample contains 100 businesses. Its mean bill is 240 thousand COP, its sample standard
deviation is 58 thousand COP, and 40 businesses used electronic invoicing. A second independent random sample from
exactly the same business population and October period also contains 100 businesses. Its mean bill is 252 thousand COP,
its sample standard deviation is 66 thousand COP, and 55 businesses used electronic invoicing.
A certified spread-only population report makes the population standard deviation of October bills known as 60 thousand
COP; it does not disclose the population mean. The two sample standard deviations remain separate descriptive quantities.
The initial study estimates the initially unknown mean at 95% confidence.
Assume the bill distribution is sufficiently regular, without dominant outliers, for a normal approximation to sample
means of this size. Each study draws a simple random sample without replacement from the full fixed register. Samples
are selected independently across studies; overlap between different samples is permitted. Each study selects only 0.05%
of the population, so dependence within a draw and the finite-population correction are negligible.
Ten subsequent independent studies each use 100 businesses, the same known population standard deviation and the same
95% normal confidence procedure. These are additional studies, separate from both samples above. A later comprehensive
investigation of the entire October population establishes its mean bill as 245 thousand COP. Repeated samples do not

Interpretation. Under the stated model, repeated independent use of each confidence procedure covers the fixed
population mean in approximately its nominal percentage of intervals over the long run. A finite collection of intervals
need not have exactly nominal coverage, and successive observed proportions need not approach it monotonically. The
discrepancies here can arise from finite-study variation; they do not by themselves invalidate the procedure or prove that
nominal coverage is wrong.
The population mean is fixed. Once an interval is calculated, it either contains that mean or does not; no nominal
probability is assigned to this already determined event. Coverage counts compare each subsequent study’s own interval
with the later-established population mean. They do not count sample means or individual observations inside the initial
interval. The initial and comparison samples are excluded from the subsequent-study denominator.

One valid example of a student-created scenario (hypothetical). A platform investigates all 100,000 completed
deliveries by its registered couriers in Bogota on November 2, 2026. The measurement is the fee for each delivery in
thousand COP. The separate yes-or-no condition is whether the delivery included optional parcel insurance.
The initial independent random sample of 64 deliveries has mean fee 32 thousand COP, sample standard deviation 7
thousand COP and 24 insured deliveries. A second independent random sample of 64 deliveries from that identical
population and date has mean fee 34 thousand COP, sample standard deviation 9 thousand COP and 32 insured deliveries.
A certified spread-only report gives the known population standard deviation of delivery fees as 8 thousand COP, distinct
from either sample standard deviation; the population mean is initially withheld. The initial study uses 95% confidence.
Assume the fee distribution is sufficiently regular with no dominant outliers, so the normal approximation to sample
means is suitable. Each sample is selected without replacement from the full register. Study selections are independent
and may overlap. Each study samples 0.064% of the population, so the finite-population correction is negligible.
Five subsequent independent studies, separate from the two original samples, use 64 deliveries each, the same known
population standard deviation and the same 95% normal procedure. A hypothetical later comprehensive investigation of
the entire same-day delivery population establishes the population mean as 33 thousand COP; this is not established by
the repeated samples. The subsequent interval endpoints below are in thousand COP.

The observed coverage of the later-established mean delivery fee is 15 percentage points below nominal 95%. Each listed
interval has full width 2(1.96) = 3.92 thousand COP, consistent with the stated n, known σ and critical value. Its center
is its own subsequent sample mean, found as (Li + Ui )/2, not the initial mean.
Interpretation. Under the stated model, repeated independent use of each confidence procedure covers the fixed
population mean in approximately its nominal percentage of intervals over the long run. A finite collection of intervals
need not have exactly nominal coverage, and successive observed proportions need not approach it monotonically. The
discrepancies here can arise from finite-study variation; they do not by themselves invalidate the procedure or prove that
nominal coverage is wrong.
The population mean is fixed. Once an interval is calculated, it either contains that mean or does not; no nominal
probability is assigned to this already determined event. Coverage counts compare each subsequent study’s own interval
with the later-established population mean. They do not count sample means or individual observations inside the initial
interval. The initial and comparison samples are excluded from the subsequent-study denominator.
