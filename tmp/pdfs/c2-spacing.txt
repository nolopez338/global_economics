                                      C2 – Point and interval estimation
                                       Global Economics | Grade 11 | Term 2 | Lesson 1
C2: Compare point and interval estimation and interpret confidence through long-run coverage, distinguishing this from
the probability that a particular calculated interval contains the parameter.

                                              Equations and basic procedure
Assume independent random observations, known σ, and normally distributed x̄ (exact for normal populations). Estimate
fixed, unknown µ with x̄. Normal simulations use stated fixed means and the original σ and n. A simulated interval covers
the fixed simulation mean when L ≤ µsim ≤ U . No sample SD s is provided or needed.

Task 2 – Calculate MoE and state the interval. In independent practice and the worked example, use four columns:
Symbol, Concept, Calculation, and Result and meaning in context. In the guided example, combine the last two

brief context. Use rows for the point estimate, margin of error, confidence interval, and full width. In independent practice,

confidence level; give the shared point estimate once. When rounding, distinguish unrounded and reported intervals and

Task 3 – Calculate observed coverage and its absolute error relative to the confidence level. Interpret the

coverage, and absolute error separately, and give a separate interpretation. Report error in percentage points.

Check the centre: (36.08 + 43.92)/2 = 40 minutes. The simulation intervals have the same 7.84-minute width, but their
centres follow their fresh sample means. With the same sample and SE, higher confidence increases z ∗ , MoE, and width
while preserving the centre, so the wider interval contains the narrower one. Increasing n with other settings fixed reduces
SE and width.

The fixed simulation mean is 41 minutes. Samples 1, 2, 4, and 5 cover it because their endpoints satisfy L ≤ 41 ≤ U .
Sample 3 misses: its lower endpoint, 41.08, exceeds 41.

Four of the five simulated cycling-trip intervals contain the fixed simulation mean of 41 minutes, so observed coverage is
80%, which is 15 percentage points below the 95% confidence level. The covering count varies randomly across finite runs;
with only five intervals, observed coverage changes in steps of 20 percentage points and need not equal 95%. Over many
fresh samples under the model, about 95% of intervals made by this procedure cover the fixed population mean. Each
calculated interval either covers or misses; confidence does not describe the proportion of individual trip durations in the
interval.
