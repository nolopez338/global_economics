Learning objective
Model discrete monetary outcomes in economic and financial contexts; derive and verify probability mass functions (PMFs), and
construct and interpret cumulative distribution functions (CDFs) with tables and graphs.

Criteria assessment
This standalone key authors the original questions required by the governing assessment brief and reproduces each task before its
worked solution. Questions 1–2 assess C1; Questions 3–6 assess C2. Show reasoning and exact probabilities. All contextual rules are
stated in ordinary language.
Independent passing thresholds: C1 requires at least 4 out of 6 points; C2 requires at least 4 out of 14 points.
Points cannot transfer between criteria. The overall available total is 20 points. An overall total alone does not establish a
pass in both criteria. These are the passing thresholds for this assessment; no cumulative policy is specified.

Evaluated criteria
C1 Characterises discrete random variables in economic and financial contexts by identifying their support, probability mass function
and possible outcomes.
C2 Interprets PMFs and CDFs by verifying their defining properties and using them to calculate point, interval, cumulative, and
complementary probabilities in financial contexts.

Online order contribution

[C1-3]

A small seller handles three orders, in first, second and third order. Each order independently has exactly two mutually exclusive
possible outcomes: the buyer keeps it with probability 0.6 , or cancels it with probability 0.4 . These probabilities apply to every
order. Each kept order contributes 5 USD and each cancellation contributes no money. A fixed handling cost of 4 USD is then
deducted once from the combined contribution. There are no other receipts or costs. Record the net contribution for the three
orders.

[1]

Calculate and interpret the probability that the net contribution is exactly 6 USD .
From the grouped PMF , P ( X = 6 ) = pX (6) = 54/125 = 0.432 = 43.2% . The seller earns a net contribution of exactly 6 USD
with probability 43.2% ; this happens exactly when two orders are kept and one is cancelled.
