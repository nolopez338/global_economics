Model used throughout.We estimate a population mean from a simple random sample of independent
observations (or a negligible fraction of a much larger population). The population standard deviationσ
is supplied and known, not an estimated sample SD. The population is normal or the sample mean is well
approximated by a normal distribution. These assumptions support the two-sided normal intervals below.
Controlled comparisons.Hold ¯xfixed to isolate changes in width. Actual sample means can change
between samples; a new sample need not have the same centre.

2.2 Reusable graphing procedure
These steps guide every investigation; they are not extra tasks in a problem.
1. Identify the population mean, units and model assumptions. Record what varies and what stays fixed.
Distinguish individual variability σ from uncertainty in the meanSE .
2. For varying n use SE = σ /√ n and MoE = z∗ SE . For varying C , keep SE fixed and choose
z∗ from the table.
3. Calculate without premature rounding. Organize a supporting table ofn, SE and MoE, orC, z∗ and MoE.
A row supplies coordinates for all three relevant graphs.
4. Axis convention: A vs B always means A vertically and B horizontally.Label both axes, with
units or % where appropriate. Select numerical domains and scales that show every point; the confidence
range 80–100% keeps 99% and 99.9% distinguishable. Use real spacing, not category positions.
5. Plot accurate coordinates. In these solutions, filled circles are values from the tables; smooth solid curves
use the stated model. Sample size is integer: the continuousn curve is only a visual guide between feasible
integer values.
6. Check shape and context. At fixed confidence, MoE vs SE is a line through the origin of slopez∗. At fixed
SE,z ∗ vs MoE is a line through the origin of slope1/SE. Confidence vs either is nonlinear:
C = 2Φ( z∗ )−1, C = 2Φ( MoE / SE )−1.
These smooth confidence curves use the normal-distribution relationship, not straight joins of rounded table
entries. Points can lie very slightly off the curve because the suppliedz∗ values are rounded. On a percentage
vertical axis the plotted value is100C.
Reading coverage.A 95% procedure captures the fixed population mean in about 95% of repeated samples
under its assumptions. This is nominal long-run coverage, not a probability assigned to the fixed mean after
an interval has been observed. Smaller width means greater precision; coverage and precision are different
properties.
