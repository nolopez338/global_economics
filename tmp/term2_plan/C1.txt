A utility studies the amount charged per bill. Itstarget populationisall May electricity bills

Write P for the population and X for the sample . Here n is 40 bills; N is the number of bills in
the full list, which is not given. The selected bills come from that population; a sample and its size are different
objects.
Thepopulation parameter µ is unknown. It represents themean charge across all May electricity bills
for small shops in the district. Thesample statistic ¯x = 210 thousand COP is the mean charge across
the 40 selected billsand estimates µ .
One observation yj is the charge on thejth selected bill, in thousand COP; it is not a sample. The sample
mean summarizes the observations. The sample standard deviations describes their spread and estimates
σ , the population standard deviation, in the same units. For the condition “paid by the due date,”ˆpis the
sample fraction and estimates the population fractionp ; both are unitless.

Context subscripts identify the population or sample being summarized; a measure subscript identifies the
variable or condition. For example,X2D comes from P2D , and its mean is ¯x2D . Each problem defines its
own scope. Parameters stay fixed for that scope and measure; statistics may vary across draws. Changing the
month, district or variable changes what is being summarized.
3
2.2 Practice procedure
1. Name the unit being studied and define thetarget population , including any time, place or eligibility
limits. Then identify the selected units as X (or Xi for draw i), distinguish yj from the sample, and
record n and N when known.
2. State the variable and its units for a mean or standard deviation. For a proportion, state the condition
and identify the denominator as the size of the group being described. To findˆp, divide the number of
selected units meeting the condition by the sample size.
3. Match the quantity to its symbol. Ask whether it summarizes the whole target population or the selected
units. Write the contextual meaning even when the numerical value is unknown.
4. Before subtracting, check that the variable or condition and the units match. Name the two groups and
say whether the result compares two sample statistics, a sample statistic with a population parameter, or
two known population parameters. Express differences between percentages in percentage points.
5. For a redraw from the same population with the same measures, the parameters stay fixed and the
statistics can vary. If the target population changes, redefine the parameters for the new group. A
difference between sample statistics alone cannot establish the size or direction of a difference between
population parameters.

speed and net credits to describe how much cash merchants receive per payment. It flags a settlement as late if
it takes more than two business days, and separately flags any transaction that required manual verification
before release to track staff workload.
From all July card transactions for its merchants in that city, the processor randomly selects 50. The mean
settlement time is 1.6 business days, and the mean net credit is 145 thousand COP. Five settlements are late
