                        C2 – Constructing PMF and CDF Tables and Graphs
                                           Global Economics | Grade 10 | Term 2 | Lesson 1
C2: Interprets PMFs and CDFs by verifying their defining properties and using them to calculate point, interval, cumulative,
and complementary probabilities in financial contexts.
                                                  Equations and basic procedure

Symbol or notation       Concept         Brief meaning

X | x | SX               Quantity        Variable | Value | Support of the random variable X
ω                        Outcome         One complete result.
pX (x) = P (X = x)       PMF             The Probability Mass Function (PMF) describes the probability that a discrete
                                         random variable X takes a specific value x.
FX (x) = P (X ≤ x)       CDF             The Cumulative Distribution Function (CDF) describes the probability that a
                                         random variable X takes a value less than or equal to a specified value x.
Task 1 — PMF table and graph. Define the random variable clearly stating what it represents and its possible values
or Support. Construct a PMF table showing the probability of each possible value, and draw a labeled graph representing
the probability distribution. Verify the PMF adds up to 1.
Task 2 — CDF table and graph. Construct a CDF table showing the cumulative probability for each interval of values,
including the intervals below the minimum and at or above the maximum possible values. Draw a labeled step graph
representing the cumulative distribution, using filled and open points to indicate included and excluded endpoints.

1. Museum kiosk payments
Payment fees. Payment fees. A customer makes a payment at a museum kiosk using one of four methods: cash, debit card, credit
card or mobile wallet, with probabilities 0.15, 0.25, 0.35 and 0.25, respectively. The museum pays no processing fee for cash payments,
a fee of 2 USD for either type of card, and a fee of 4 USD for mobile wallet payments. No other processing charges apply. Complete
Tasks 1 and 2 to describe the processing fee using PMF and CDF tables and graphs. Then use the CDF to find the probability that
the fee is at most 3 USD and the probability that it is at most 5 USD.

Fee at most 3 USD (FX (3)) Since 2 ≤ 3 < 4, P (X ≤ 3) = FX (3) = P (X = 0) + P (X = 2) = 0.15 + 0.60 = 0.75. Thus, there is a
75% probability that the museum pays at most 3 USD in processing fees. Although P (X = 3) = 0, the CDF includes all fees below 3
USD as well.
Fee at most 5 USD (FX (5)) Since 5 ≥ 4, use the final row: P (X ≤ 5) = FX (5) = 0.15 + 0.60 + 0.25 = 1. Thus, there is a 100%
probability that the processing fee is at most 5 USD: as the maximum possible fee is 4 USD.

3. Workshop scrap buyback
Uneven buyback payments. A workshop scrap centre buys one standard-sized bag containing only one material type.
The exhaustive types are rejected material, paper, cardboard, plastic, steel and aluminium, with probabilities .20, .12, .18,
.15, .10, .25, respectively. Payments are 0 USD for rejected material, 4 USD for either paper or cardboard, 9 USD for either
plastic or steel, and 15 USD for aluminium. There are no other payments. Record the buyback payment.
Task 1 — PMF table and graph. Define X, its units and support. Identify the possible complete outcomes, their
probabilities and their corresponding values of X. Construct a PMF table and a labeled PMF graph. Verify the defining
properties of the PMF.
Task 2 — CDF table and graph. Construct a CDF table covering all real cutoffs and a labeled CDF graph. Verify the
defining properties of the CDF and that its jumps reproduce the PMF. Determine and interpret FX (9) in this context.

4. Delivery loss claims
Missing probability and capped payout. A delivery insurer can receive only 0, 1, 2, 3 or 4 claims in one day. Their
probabilities, in that order, are .15, .20, a missing probability, .20, .10. It pays 5 USD per claim, capped at 15 USD in total
for the day; it pays the smaller of 5 times the count and 15 USD. There are no other payments. Record the daily payout.
Task 1 — PMF table and graph. Determine the missing claim-count probability. Define X, its units and support.
Identify the possible complete outcomes, their probabilities and their corresponding values of X. Construct a PMF table
and a labeled PMF graph. Verify the defining properties of the PMF.
Task 2 — CDF table and graph. Construct a CDF table covering all real cutoffs and a labeled CDF graph. Verify the
defining properties of the CDF and that its jumps reproduce the PMF. Determine and interpret FX (10) in this context.

5. Tote bag sales
Independent purchases and discount. A school enterprise offers one tote bag to each of three visitors. Independently,
each buys one with probability 0.3 or passes with probability 0.7, regardless of the discount. Each item costs 7 USD. Only
if all three buy, subtract a 5 USD discount from the combined bill. Otherwise there is no discount. There are no other sales
or charges. Record total revenue.
Task 1 — PMF table and graph. Define X, its units and support. Identify the possible complete outcomes, their
probabilities and their corresponding values of X. Construct a PMF table and a labeled PMF graph. Verify the defining
properties of the PMF.
Task 2 — CDF table and graph. Construct a CDF table covering all real cutoffs and a labeled CDF graph. Verify the
defining properties of the CDF and that its jumps reproduce the PMF. Determine and interpret FX (14) in this context.

6. Two-stage media fund
Signed two-stage net results. A hypothetical classroom fund receives two cash returns. The first is 17 USD, 0 USD or 7
USD with probabilities .20, .30, .50, respectively. Independently, the second is 0 USD with probability 0.4 or 7 USD with
probability 0.6. These are all possible returns. A fixed participation cost of 7 USD is charged once. There are no other
receipts or charges. Record the sum of both returns minus the cost; a negative net result means a loss.
Task 1 — PMF table and graph. Define X, its units and support. Identify the possible complete outcomes, their
probabilities and their corresponding values of X. Construct a PMF table and a labeled PMF graph. Verify the defining
properties of the PMF.
Task 2 — CDF table and graph. Construct a CDF table covering all real cutoffs and a labeled CDF graph. Verify the
defining properties of the CDF and that its jumps reproduce the PMF. Determine and interpret FX (0) in this context.
