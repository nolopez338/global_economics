                      Bernoulli Models and Individual Break-Even Analysis
                                     Global Economics | Grade 10 | Term 2 | Lesson 1
 C3: Models binary economic outcomes with Bernoulli random variables, interpreting success and failure in contexts such
 as default, gain, loss, or claim occurrence.

                                           Equations and basic procedure
One observation in its stated period has two mutually exclusive, exhaustive states. The event label “success” means that E
occurs; it need not mean a monetary gain. All data are hypothetical. All monetary payoffs are net gains or losses after the
stated costs. Each Yi has one strictly positive and one strictly negative payoff.

1. Campus concert food stand or indoor games                                                                             Complete
A student club compares two hypothetical fundraising choices for one weekend: a food stand at a campus outdoor concert
or an indoor games afternoon. The concert either goes ahead or is cancelled; initially, it goes ahead on three out of every
eight weekends. After food, room, equipment, and publicity costs as applicable, the food stand has a net gain of USD 105 if
the concert goes ahead and a net loss of USD 45 if it is cancelled. The games afternoon has a net loss of USD 30 if the
concert goes ahead, because it draws potential players away, and a net gain of USD 90 if it is cancelled, because students
seek an indoor alternative. Only one option would be chosen; both use the same concert outcome.

p = P (E), initially p = 3/8, and both activities use this same X; no independence is assumed. For 0 < p < 1,

                                                         Independent Practice

3. Campus portraits or an online CV workshop                                                                               Core: service
A recent graduate compares two hypothetical freelance activities for one day: campus portrait sessions or an online CV
workshop. The university’s orientation day either goes ahead or is cancelled; initially, it goes ahead on three out of every
five days considered. After equipment, software, and advertising costs as applicable, portraits have a net gain of USD 84 if
orientation goes ahead and a net loss of USD 36 if it is cancelled. The CV workshop has a net loss of USD 48 if orientation
goes ahead, since potential participants are busy on campus, and a net gain of USD 72 if it is cancelled, since they are free
to attend online. Only one activity would be chosen; both use the same orientation outcome.

4. Meal preparation in a shared kitchen                                                                                      Core: sales
Housemates compare two hypothetical plans for a small meal-preparation side business over one month: disposable food
boxes or reusable containers. Monthly subscriptions either meet their advertised target or fall short; initially, the target
is met in two out of every five months. After ingredients, packaging, kitchen use, and cleaning costs as applicable, the
disposable-box plan has a net gain of USD 98 if the target is met and a net loss of USD 42 otherwise. The reusable-container
plan has a net gain of USD 70 if the target is met and a net loss of USD 70 otherwise, reflecting its container and cleaning
costs. Only one plan would be chosen; both use the same subscription-target outcome.

5. Freelance video editing equipment                                                                                      Core: capacity
A freelance video editor compares two hypothetical equipment choices over one quarter: a personal laptop or a workstation
rented only when a large project is confirmed. A large project either arrives or does not arrive; initially, one arrives in four
out of every nine quarters. After software, promotion, and any equipment rental costs, the laptop option has a net gain of
USD 150 with the project and a net loss of USD 75 without it. The workstation option has a net gain of USD 375 with the
project, because faster editing allows more paid deliverables, and a net loss of USD 75 without it, when no workstation is
rented. Only one option would be chosen; both use the same project outcome.

6. First-job event shifts                                                                             Extension: precision
A young adult compares two hypothetical job offers for one paid shift during a week: catering work or ticket-desk work
at the same event. The event either is cancelled or goes ahead; initially, cancellation occurs in five out of every twelve
weeks. After transport bookings and required preparation costs, the catering offer has a net loss of USD 27.50 if the event
is cancelled and a net gain of USD 82.50 if it goes ahead. The ticket-desk offer has a net loss of USD 45 if it is cancelled
and a net gain of USD 67.50 if it goes ahead. Cancellation means no shift pay, but preparation and booked transport still
cost money. Only one offer would be accepted; both use the same weekly event outcome. The decimal amounts are exact.

7. Online tutoring or an offline study workshop                                                     Extension: relabelling
A student tutor compares two hypothetical choices for a scheduled revision weekend within one month: online tutoring
from a shared apartment or an offline study workshop at a nearby library. The apartment building’s internet either is
interrupted during that weekend or remains uninterrupted; initially, an interruption occurs in seven out of every twenty
months. After preparation, advertising, internet, and room-booking costs as applicable, online tutoring has a net loss of
USD 64 with interruption and a net gain of USD 96 with uninterrupted service. The offline workshop has a net gain of USD
112 with interruption, as affected students seek help at the library, and a net loss of USD 28 with uninterrupted service,
when too few attend. Only one option would be chosen; both use the same monthly internet outcome. For each option, also
express net profit as a constant plus a coefficient times an indicator that equals one for uninterrupted service and zero for
interruption. Express its expected net profit in terms of the probability of uninterrupted service, which is one minus the
interruption probability; find the exact break-even probability of uninterrupted service and calculate its initial expected net
profit under this relabelling. Use the interruption probability on the horizontal axis of both expected-value graphs.
