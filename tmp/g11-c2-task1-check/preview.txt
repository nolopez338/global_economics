Evaluated criteria

C2 Compares point and interval estimation and interprets confidence levels through their long-run coverage
    meaning, distinguishing this from the probability that a particular calculated interval contains the
    parameter.

How to practise. Try each task before opening its blue solution link. Use the arrows to return to the contents,
problem list, or problem. Use the same three tasks throughout: (1) organize the information in a table and
calculate SE ; (2) calculate MoE , state and interpret each interval; (3) calculate observed coverage from
the evidence and answer one multiple-choice question.

For 95% confidence, C = 0.95 , α = 1 − 0.95 = 0.05 and each tail has area α /2 = 0.025 . The
 critical value z ∗ = 1.960 gives margin of error MoE = 1.960 × 2 = 3.92 minutes . Thus 40 ±

1.3     Example 2: repeating the cycling procedure
A separate, hypothetical normal population has known fixed mean µ = 41 minutes and the same σ =
 14 minutes . Simulate 50 fresh independent samples of n = 49 trips and apply Example 1’s 95% procedure
to each. Each new x̄ gives new endpoints; the population mean stays fixed. If 3 intervals miss 41, then 47
cover, so the observed coverage is (47/50) × 100% = 94% .
This finite-run rate does not change C = 0.95 or α = 0.05 . Under the model, about 95% of intervals
cover over many repetitions. Each realized interval either contains the mean or does not; confidence does not
give the percentage of individual trip times inside it.
Quick check. Which can change in a new run: the sample means, the observed coverage, or the chosen
confidence level? The first two can change; the level stays fixed unless the procedure is changed.

2.2   Observed coverage and the fixed mean
                                  number of intervals containing the fixed population mean
          Observed coverage =                                                              × 100%.
                                             total number of simulated intervals
In the original estimation problem, µ is fixed but unknown. In a simulation, the model supplies a known fixed
 µ so that coverage can be checked: an interval [L, U ] covers exactly when L ≤ µ ≤ U , including equality at
either endpoint.
For narrative evidence, use the covering count, or subtract misses from the total. For a diagram, count the
horizontal intervals meeting or crossing the fixed-mean reference line, then count all intervals. For a table,
sum the relevant covering cells and use the total number of intervals for that procedure. Paired procedures
applied to 100 samples each have denominator 100, even though there are 200 intervals altogether.
The nominal confidence level C is chosen before sampling and sets α = 1 − C . Never calculate α from
 observed coverage . Under the model, α is the long-run fraction of intervals that miss the fixed mean, not
a probability that a particular realized interval misses it. Observed coverage is calculated after a finite run
and can differ from that level. Under the stated assumptions, confidence describes the procedure’s long-run
coverage of the fixed parameter. A realized interval either contains the parameter or does not; 95% confidence
does not assign a 95% probability to that fixed interval containing it, and does not describe the percentage of
individual observations inside it.
