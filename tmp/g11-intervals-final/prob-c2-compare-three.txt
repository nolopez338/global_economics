3.4 Three confidence levels for repair times
A repair cooperative takes one independent random sample of n = 64 completed appliance repairs from a
specified month. The sample mean repair time is x¯ = 50 minutes. Assume normally distributed repair times
with known population standard deviation σ = 16 minutes. Use this same sample to calculate exactly three
two-sided confidence intervals: 90%, 95%, and 99%, with z∗ = 1.645, 1.960, and 2.576, respectively. Keep
the sample mean, sample size, population standard deviation, and standard error unchanged across the three
procedures. Do not round intermediate calculations or endpoints.
In a separate hypothetical simulation, a normal population has known fixed mean µ = 51 minutes and the same
σ = 16 minutes. From each of 200 fresh samples of size n = 64, all three unrounded intervals are constructed
around that sample’s mean. The mutually exclusive rows below count the outcomes for these triples. The
simulated samples are separate from the cooperative’s original sample.
Outcome for the intervals from one simulated sample Number of samples
All three cover the simulated mean 178
Only the 95% and 99% intervals cover 12
Only the 99% interval covers 7
None of the three covers 3

C2 Task 3 – Calculate observed coverage and answer the multiple-choice question. Show covering
counts, denominators, and rates for each level. Which option gives the rates in the order 90%, 95%, 99%

A. 90%, 95%, 99%. Every finite simulation must match its nominal levels exactly.
B. 89%, 95%, 98.5%. Higher confidence gives wider intervals with the same sample mean as centre;
confidence describes long-run coverage of the fixed population mean.
C. 89%, 95%, 98.5%. Higher confidence moves the interval centre closer to the unknown population
mean.
D. 89%, 95%, 98.5%. The cooperative’s calculated 99% interval has a 99% probability of containing its
fixed population mean.
