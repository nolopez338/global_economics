                                    C2 – Point and interval estimation
                                     Global Economics | Grade 11 | Term 2 | Lesson 1
C2: Compare point and interval estimation and interpret confidence through long-run coverage, distinguishing this from
the probability that a particular calculated interval contains the parameter.

                                           Equations and basic procedure
Assume independent random observations, known σ, and normally distributed x̄ (exact for normal populations). Each
problem’s studies sample the same fixed population for the same target period, using the same n, known σ, and confidence-
interval procedure at each stated level. Initially estimate unknown µ with x̄. A later comprehensive population investigation
establishes µknown , the value of that same parameter, treated as known for this activity; repeated sample estimates alone do
not establish it. No sample SD s is needed.
Each subsequent study constructs its own interval [Li , Ui ] around its own sample mean x̄i . It covers the population mean
when Li ≤ µknown ≤ Ui . The nominal confidence level describes the procedure’s long-run interval coverage, including the
procedure used for the initial interval. It is neither the percentage of subsequent sample means nor individual observations
inside the initial interval, nor a probability assigned to the fixed mean after calculation.

                                              General assessment tasks
Task 1 – Identify and organize the information; calculate SE. In independent practice, use four columns: Symbol,
Concept, Type, and Meaning in context. The examples use two side-by-side three-column tables, with the categories
as section titles instead of a Type column. Arrange three contiguous groups in this order: Population parameters (µ,
µknown , σ); Sample statistics (x̄, n, SE); Procedure Settings (C, α, α/2, z ∗ ).
Task 2 – Calculate MoE and state the interval. In independent practice, use four columns: Symbol, Concept,
Calculation, and Result and meaning in context. In both examples, combine the last two columns as Calculation,
Result and meaning in context. Show formulas and substitutions, then results with units and brief context. Use rows
for the point estimate, margin of error, confidence interval, and full width. In independent practice, also use separate
endpoint rows. In the examples, include endpoint calculations in the confidence-interval row. Group by confidence level;
give the shared point estimate once. Keep all calculated endpoint digits. Calculate the full width as W = U − L = 2MoE.
Task 3 – Compare observed interval coverage with nominal confidence. For each level separately, count subsequent
intervals satisfying Li ≤ µknown ≤ Ui ; divide by the total number of intervals and multiply by 100%. Compare with the
corresponding nominal confidence percentage used in the initial study and calculate the absolute difference in percentage
points. Interpret discrepancies as finite-sample variation under the stated model: coverage need not equal nominal confidence,
especially with few studies, and need not approach it monotonically as studies are added.
Legacy labels in the preserved solutions refer to µknown , the later established mean of the same population.
