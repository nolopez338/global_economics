
# High School Algebra — Learning Progression

## Overview

**Goal:** Develop a complete understanding of high school algebra, progressing from arithmetic foundations to advanced algebraic functions and mathematical modeling.

**Level:** Beginner to advanced high school (Algebra I and Algebra II).

**Estimated duration:** 16–20 weeks.

**Recommended workload:** 5 hours per week.

**Prerequisites:** Basic arithmetic.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this progression, the learner should be able to:

1. Manipulate algebraic expressions accurately.
2. Solve equations and inequalities.
3. Understand and represent mathematical functions.
4. Analyze linear, quadratic, polynomial, rational, exponential, and logarithmic functions.
5. Interpret algebraic relationships graphically, numerically, and symbolically.
6. Construct and solve mathematical models of real-world situations.
7. Explain mathematical procedures and justify solutions.

---

## Stage 1: Arithmetic Foundations

**Duration:** 1–2 weeks

### Topics
- Integers and signed numbers
- Fractions and decimals
- Ratios, proportions, and percentages
- Order of operations
- Powers and roots
- Properties of real numbers

### Learning Outcomes
- Perform arithmetic operations accurately.
- Simplify numerical expressions.
- Apply exponent rules.
- Work confidently with fractions and negative numbers.

### Example
Evaluate:

(3/4 + 2/3) × 12

### Mastery Criteria
Solve multistep arithmetic problems independently and explain the operations used.

---

## Stage 2: Algebraic Expressions

**Duration:** 2 weeks

### Topics
- Variables and constants
- Algebraic notation
- Terms, coefficients, and expressions
- Evaluating expressions
- Combining like terms
- Distributive property
- Expanding expressions
- Translating verbal statements into algebra

### Learning Outcomes
- Interpret variables as quantities.
- Construct algebraic expressions from descriptions.
- Simplify and expand expressions.
- Evaluate expressions for given variable values.

### Example

### Mastery Criteria
Translate verbal relationships into expressions and simplify them correctly.

## Stage 3: Equations and Inequalities

**Duration:** 2 weeks

### Topics
- Equality and equivalent transformations
- One-step and multistep linear equations
- Equations with variables on both sides
- Equations involving fractions
- Literal equations and rearranging formulas
- Linear inequalities
- Compound inequalities
- Absolute value equations and inequalities
- Word problems

### Learning Outcomes
- Solve linear equations systematically.
- Rearrange formulas to isolate variables.
- Solve inequalities and represent solution sets.
- Interpret solutions in context.
- Identify equations with no solution or infinitely many solutions.

### Example

Solve equations and inequalities, verify results, and explain each transformation.

- Relations and functions

- Domain and range
- Independent and dependent variables
- Slope and rate of change

- Slope-intercept and point-slope forms

### Learning Outcomes

### Mastery Criteria

## Recommended Learning Method

For each stage, follow this sequence:

1. **Conceptual Understanding**
   - Define the mathematical concepts.
   - Explain their meaning.
   - Explore simple examples and counterexamples.

2. **Procedural Development**
   - Study worked examples.
   - Practice basic procedures.
   - Progress toward multistep problems.

3. **Multiple Representations**
   - Use symbolic expressions.
   - Construct tables.
   - Analyze graphs.
   - Interpret verbal descriptions.

4. **Applications**
   - Solve contextual problems.
   - Construct mathematical models.
   - Interpret solutions and limitations.

5. **Assessment**
   - Solve unfamiliar problems independently.
   - Explain the reasoning.
   - Identify and correct errors.
   - Review prerequisite concepts when necessary.

## Progression Rules for an LLM Tutor

When using this roadmap as instructional context, the LLM should:

1. Follow the stages in the specified order unless the learner demonstrates prerequisite mastery.
2. Diagnose prerequisite knowledge before introducing advanced topics.
3. Prioritize conceptual understanding over memorization.
4. Introduce formal mathematical notation alongside clear explanations.
5. Use progressively challenging examples and exercises.
6. Connect symbolic, graphical, numerical, and contextual representations.
7. Ask learners to justify mathematical transformations.
8. Distinguish conceptual errors from arithmetic mistakes.
9. Provide hints before complete solutions when practicing.
10. Include both routine exercises and unfamiliar transfer problems.
11. Check domain restrictions and verify solutions.
12. Revisit prerequisite concepts when persistent difficulties appear.
13. Evaluate mastery through independent problem-solving, not merely recognition of worked examples.
14. Use an approximate 80–90% independent success target on representative problems as an instructional heuristic, adjusting based on task difficulty and evidence of understanding.
15. Advance when the learner demonstrates sufficient conceptual and procedural mastery.

## Final Competencies

After completing all nine stages, the learner should be prepared to:

- Study precalculus.
- Understand more advanced mathematical functions.
- Solve complex algebraic problems.
- Develop mathematical models.
- Interpret algebraic solutions in real-world contexts.
- Begin studying trigonometry and introductory calculus.
