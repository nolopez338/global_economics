Grade 10 · Grade 11 · Curriculum Outline

Six-step term topic outline

Open each grade-and-term link to review the corresponding term page and its complete learning evidence.

  1. Step 1

    Grade 10 — Term 1

    Decision making under uncertainty: structuring alternatives, states of nature, consequences, costs, revenues, and payoffs; applying Maximax, Maximin, Minimax Regret, Expected Monetary Value/Bayes, subjective probabilities, sensitivity analysis, and decision trees to justify economic or financial choices.

  2. Step 2

    Grade 10 — Term 2

    Discrete probability modelling in economic and financial contexts: random variables, PMFs and CDFs, Bernoulli, binomial, geometric, negative binomial, and Poisson distributions, plus expected value, variance, and distribution selection for interpreting return, risk, claims, defaults, arrivals, and other discrete outcomes.

  3. Step 3

    Grade 10 — Term 3

    Continuous probability and empirical data analysis: density functions, uniform models, probability rules, frequency distributions, quartiles, percentiles, graphical comparisons, standard normal probabilities, standardisation, inverse normal reasoning, portfolio risk, covariance, correlation, diversification, and evidence-based financial risk analysis.

  4. Step 4

    Grade 11 — Term 1

    Advanced continuous probability and risk modelling: PDFs, CDFs, support, uniform, triangular, circular-arc, and exponential distributions; mean and variance for continuous models; simulation and sampling comparisons; Student’s t tools; normal-versus-t choices; and integrated portfolio-risk investigations.

  5. Step 5

    Grade 11 — Term 2

    Confidence and interval estimation: population parameters versus sample statistics, point and interval estimates, confidence levels, critical values, standard errors, margins of error, confidence intervals for means and proportions, sample-size decisions, t-intervals, finite population correction, interpretation, precision, and limitations.

  6. Step 6

    Grade 11 — Term 3

    Hypothesis testing for economics and finance: test elements, null and alternative hypotheses, tail direction, Type I and Type II errors, significance and power, Z-tests and t-tests for means and proportions, paired and independent samples, automated calculations, visualisation, and evidence-based research communication.
