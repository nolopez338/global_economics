  1. Step 1

    Grade 10 — Term 1

    Assessment criteria extracted from the current Grade 10 — Term 1 page.

    Criteria for this step

    • C1 Characterises decision-making problems under uncertainty by identifying decision alternatives, states of nature, consequences, and the economic or financial context of the decision.

    Criteria for this step

    • C2 Organises decision problems using payoff tables, placing alternatives, states of nature, and payoffs in a clear structure for comparison.

    Criteria for this step

    • C3 Calculates profits by combining revenues with fixed and variable costs across different states of nature, determining the corresponding payoffs for each alternative, and organising the results in payoff tables for comparison.

    Criteria for this step

    • C4 Applies the Maximin and Maximax criteria to make optimistic or conservative decisions without probabilities, selecting the alternative with the highest possible payoff or the best worst-case, interpreting risks or limitations.

    Criteria for this step

    • C5 Constructs opportunity loss tables and applies the Minimax regret criterion to select the alternative that minimises the maximum opportunity loss.

    Criteria for this step

    • C6 Calculates Expected Monetary Value using stated or estimated probabilities and applies the Bayes decision criterion to select the alternative with the highest expected payoff.

    Criteria for this step

    • C7 Uses subjective probabilities and sensitivity analysis to examine how changes in probability assumptions affect the preferred decision.

    Criteria for this step

    • C8 Compares the Maximax, Maximin, Minimax Regret, and EMV/Bayes decision criteria by contrasting their information requirements, risk orientations, recommended alternatives, and limitations.

    Criteria for this step

    • C9 Builds and solves decision trees by distinguishing decision nodes and chance nodes, assigning probabilities and payoffs, and applying backward induction to identify an optimal strategy.

    Criteria for this step

    • C10 Evaluates economic and financial decision-making scenarios by selecting and justifying the most appropriate decision criterion according to the information available, risk attitude, and decision context.

  2. Step 2

    Grade 10 — Term 2

    Assessment criteria extracted from the current Grade 10 — Term 2 page.

    Criteria for this step

    • C1 Characterises discrete random variables in economic and financial contexts by identifying their support, probability mass function and possible outcomes.

    Criteria for this step

    • C2 Interprets PMFs and CDFs by verifying their defining properties and using them to calculate point, interval, cumulative, and complementary probabilities in financial contexts.

    Criteria for this step

    • C3 Models binary economic outcomes with Bernoulli random variables, interpreting success and failure in contexts such as default, gain, loss, or claim occurrence.

    Criteria for this step

    • C4 Applies the binomial distribution to fixed-trial financial situations by verifying its conditions and calculating probabilities using the binomial probability formula \(P(X=k)=\binom{n}{k}p^k(1-p)^{n-k}\).

    Criteria for this step

    • C5 Models waiting-time situations with geometric distributions, calculating the probability of the first success occurring on a given trial in financial or business contexts.

    Criteria for this step

    • C6 Applies the negative binomial distribution to situations that count the number of trials needed to obtain a fixed number of successes.

    Criteria for this step

    • C7 Evaluates rare-event situations with the Poisson distribution, interpreting the parameter \(\lambda\) in contexts such as claims, arrivals, defects, or defaults within a fixed interval.

    Criteria for this step

    • C8 Calculates and interprets the expected value and variance of discrete probability distributions as measures of return and risk in economic and financial contexts.

    Criteria for this step

    • C9 Compares Bernoulli, binomial, negative binomial, geometric, and Poisson distributions by selecting the most appropriate model for a given financial-risk situation.

    Criteria for this step

    • C10 Assesses investment alternatives for inclusion in a portfolio by comparing their discrete return distributions, expected returns, variances, and risk–return trade-offs, using the results to support investment portfolio decision-making.

  3. Step 3

    Grade 10 — Term 3

    Assessment criteria extracted from the current Grade 10 — Term 3 page.

    Criteria for this step

    • C1 Characterises continuous random variables by interpreting probability density functions, recognising that probabilities correspond to areas under curves and that \(P(X=x)=0\).

    Criteria for this step

    • C2 Applies the uniform continuous distribution on an interval \([a,b]\) to calculate probabilities as areas of rectangles in economic and financial contexts.

    Criteria for this step

    • C3 Applies probability rules for simple, combined, and complementary events across discrete and continuous probability models to calculate and interpret likelihoods in economic and financial contexts.

    Criteria for this step

    • C4 Uses frequency distributions, quartiles, percentiles, and graphical representations to describe empirical economic data and compare its shape, centre, and spread with a continuous probability model.

    Criteria for this step

    • C5 Applies the standard normal distribution \(Z \sim N(0,1)\) and standard normal tables to calculate probabilities, critical values, and probability areas in finance and economics contexts.

    Criteria for this step

    • C6 Uses standardisation \(Z=(X-\mu)/\sigma\) to transform normal random variables and solve probability problems involving real-life economic and financial data.

    Criteria for this step

    • C7 Applies inverse normal reasoning to find values from given probabilities, interpreting percentiles, confidence-related values, and value at risk in financial contexts.

    Criteria for this step

    • C8 Interprets the mean and variance of continuous probability distributions through graphical and contextual representations.

    Criteria for this step

    • C9 Analyses investment portfolios by interpreting assets, weights, expected returns, variance, covariance, and correlation as elements that determine portfolio risk.

    Criteria for this step

    • C10 Evaluates financial-risk situations using continuous distributions, including normal, to interpret uncertainty, portfolio behaviour and preparation for statistical estimation.

  4. Step 4

    Grade 11 — Term 1

    Assessment criteria extracted from the current Grade 11 — Term 1 page.

    Criteria for this step

    • C1 Characterises continuous random variables by distinguishing them from discrete variables and interpreting their probability density function, cumulative distribution function, and support in economic contexts.

    Criteria for this step

    • C2 Applies uniform and triangular continuous distributions with constant or piecewise-linear probability density functions to calculate interval probabilities using geometric areas.

    Criteria for this step

    • C3 Applies circular-arc probability density functions to calculate interval probabilities using the geometry of circles and circular segments.

    Criteria for this step

    • C4 Applies the exponential distribution to constant-rate waiting-time situations and assesses its suitability.

    Criteria for this step

    • C5 Calculates and interprets the mean and variance of continuous distributions using standard formulas and functions.

    Criteria for this step

    • C6 Estimates interval probabilities using relative frequencies from generated samples and compares them with theoretical probabilities.

    Criteria for this step

    • C7 Calculates sample means and variances for different sample sizes and compares them with the theoretical mean and variance.

    Criteria for this step

    • C8 Uses Student’s t-distribution tables, degrees of freedom, and one-tailed or two-tailed areas to determine critical values, central probabilities, and tail probabilities or probability ranges.

    Criteria for this step

    • C9 Calculates and compares critical values and probability areas from the standard normal and Student’s t-distributions, selecting and justifying the appropriate distribution according to whether the population standard deviation is known or estimated from the sample.

    Criteria for this step

    • C10 Integrates continuous probability modelling, simulation, sampling analysis, and portfolio-risk measures to investigate an economic or financial question, justifying the selected methods and communicating results, assumptions, limitations, and evidence-based conclusions.

  5. Step 5

    Grade 11 — Term 2

    Assessment criteria extracted from the current Grade 11 — Term 2 page.

    Criteria for this step

    • C1 Distinguishes population parameters from sample statistics by interpreting and comparing the notation for population and sample means, standard deviations, and proportions in economic and financial contexts.

    Criteria for this step

    • C2 Compares point and interval estimation and interprets confidence levels through their long-run coverage meaning, distinguishing this from the probability that a particular calculated interval contains the parameter.

    Criteria for this step

    • C3 Identifies the components of a confidence interval, including point estimate, critical value, standard error, and margin of error, explaining how each component affects precision and reliability.

    Criteria for this step

    • C4 Constructs confidence intervals for a population mean with known variance using the normal distribution, applying them to contexts such as average income, average investment return, or average price level.

    Criteria for this step

    • C5 Constructs confidence intervals for a population proportion using sample proportions, applying them to contexts such as poverty rates, defaulting borrowers, or successful investments.

    Criteria for this step

    • C6 Determines the sample size required to estimate a population's mean or proportion with a specified margin of error, using it to plan economic surveys or financial studies with predetermined precision.

    Criteria for this step

    • C7 Compares confidence intervals constructed from samples of different sizes and confidence levels, analysing the trade-off between confidence and precision.

    Criteria for this step

    • C8 Constructs confidence intervals for a population mean or population proportion with unknown variance using the Student’s t-distribution, interpreting the result in finance and economics contexts.

    Criteria for this step

    • C9 Assesses the validity and limitations of confidence-interval inferences by evaluating the sampling design, potential bias, and independence, including the 10% condition for sampling without replacement and procedure-specific requirements concerning sample size, distributional shape, and outliers.

    Criteria for this step

    • C10 Integrates research design, data analysis, and confidence-interval inference to investigate an economic or financial question, justifying the selected procedure and communicating estimates, precision, assumptions, limitations, and evidence-based conclusions.

  6. Step 6

    Grade 11 — Term 3

    Assessment criteria extracted from the current Grade 11 — Term 3 page.

    Criteria for this step

    • C1 Identifies the essential elements required to conduct a hypothesis test in finance and economics, including the population parameter of interest, hypothesised value, sample statistic, sample size, variability, significance level, and relevant sampling conditions.

    Criteria for this step

    • C2 Formulates null and alternative hypotheses for a population parameter and determines whether the corresponding test is left-tailed, right-tailed, or two-tailed from the direction of the research claim.

    Criteria for this step

    • C3 Distinguishes Type I and Type II errors by identifying the incorrect decision associated with each and explaining their consequences in economic and financial contexts.

    Criteria for this step

    • C4 Interprets the significance level and power of a hypothesis test, explaining how sample size, effect size, variability, and the selected significance level influence the test’s ability to detect a false null hypothesis.

    Criteria for this step

    • C5 Conducts hypothesis tests for a population mean using the Z-test when the variance is known and the t-test when the variance is unknown, selecting the appropriate statistic and critical value.

    Criteria for this step

    • C6 Conducts hypothesis tests for a population proportion using the Z-test, interpreting the test statistic in contexts such as default rates, investment success, or market participation.

    Criteria for this step

    • C7 Conducts paired-sample t-tests to evaluate mean differences between matched or repeated observations, verifying the required conditions and interpreting the results in economic and financial contexts.

    Criteria for this step

    • C8 Conducts independent two-sample t-tests to compare the means of two populations, verifying the required conditions and interpreting the results in economic and financial contexts.

    Criteria for this step

    • C9 Applies computational thinking and development skills to automate hypothesis testing procedures, validate statistical calculations, represent results visually, and generate clear contextual interpretations.

    Criteria for this step

    • C10 Integrates research design, data management, statistical inference, critical evaluation, and scientific communication to investigate economic or financial questions and support evidence-based conclusions.
